candidate `n_neighbors` values, then full Isomap fits at only the 3-4 surviving values, then the
three-metric adjacent-k stability table that the plateau criterion will read.

Purpose: These are the two diagnostics that stand between the milestone and Isomap's best-documented
failure modes. sklearn does not raise on a disconnected k-NN graph — it warns and silently bridges
components with fabricated long edges, and every downstream number is then built on geodesic
distances that correspond to no real path along the data. Symmetrically, a single short-circuit edge
at too-large k reroutes many shortest paths and produces a globally distorted embedding that looks
fine. The scan catches the first; the stability comparison catches the second. Both must be visible
before any fit is trusted.

fixed in a cell that executes before the first fit, and a cell-index assertion enforces it. The
milestone's garden-of-forking-paths concern is explicit, and this is the phase where it bites — the
thresholds must not be tunable to whatever the sweep happens to produce.

### Open item 1 — the three numeric thresholds for the D-10 plateau criterion

**All three are fixed here, in `§4.0`, which executes before the first stage-2 fit. The ordering is
asserted mechanically by a cell-index check, not left to reading discipline.**

**Metric 1 — Procrustes disparity, threshold `<= 0.10`.**
`scipy.spatial.procrustes(A, B)` normalizes both configurations to unit Frobenius norm, applies the
optimal rotation and scaling, and returns a `disparity` in `[0, 1]` equal to the sum of squared
differences after that alignment. Choosing Procrustes rather than a raw coordinate difference is the
whole point of D-10's first metric: it absorbs the axis sign and rotation ambiguity that
ARCHITECTURE.md's Determinism section notes is inherent to any eigendecomposition-based embedding
and is not a bug to fix. A disparity of 0.10 means at least 90% of the normalized configuration
variance is shared after optimal alignment — loose enough to tolerate the genuine smooth deformation
expected as k grows and the neighbourhood widens, tight enough that a short-circuit edge switching
on fails it decisively, because one shortcut reroutes many shortest paths and moves a large fraction
of points (PITFALLS Pitfall 2).

**Metric 2 — relative leading-eigenvalue change, threshold `<= 0.15`, computed scale-free.**
For adjacent `k1 < k2`, the statistic is
`max over i in 1..N_COMPONENTS of | lam_i(k2)/lam_1(k2) - lam_i(k1)/lam_1(k1) |`.
Each spectrum is divided by its own leading eigenvalue before comparison. That normalization is
load-bearing: geodesic distances shrink smoothly as k grows, so every eigenvalue drifts downward
together, and an un-normalized comparison would trip on uniform global rescaling rather than on
shape change. Isomap's global scale is arbitrary anyway (FEATURES.md section 4). What ISO-02 actually
asks is whether the *shape* of the leading spectrum is stable, which is exactly what the
self-normalized profile measures. The statistic's maximum possible value is 1.0, so 0.15 is a
meaningful fraction of the available range rather than an arbitrarily tiny number.

**Metric 3 — Spearman correlation of geodesic distances, threshold `>= 0.95`.**
Computed on a fixed sample of `GEO_PAIR_COUNT = 100_000` index pairs drawn once with
`np.random.default_rng(GEO_PAIR_SEED)` and reused unchanged for every adjacent-k comparison, so the
comparison is apples-to-apples across k. PITFALLS Pitfall 2 states this check almost verbatim: "the
rank ordering of pairwise geodesic distances for a fixed random subset of point pairs should change
smoothly as `n_neighbors` is nudged," and a sharp discontinuity is the signature of a short-circuit
edge switching on or off. Spearman is precisely that rank-order statistic. Sampling rather than using
all ~50 million pairs is what keeps each slim npz inside D-13's ~1 MB budget; 100,000 pairs is far
more than enough to distinguish 0.95 from 0.99.

**Load-bearing design consequence.** D-13 deliberately does not persist `dist_matrix_` for sweep
fits — that is what keeps peak cache at ~1 GB instead of ~5 GB. So the sampled pair distances must be
extracted **inside the fit loop, while `dist_matrix_` is still in memory**, and only the 100,000-value
vector is stored. Without that, metric 3 is either uncomputable from cache or costs four copies of a
763 MiB matrix.

**Plateau selection rule (also pre-registered here; applied by plan 04).**
`k*` is the centre of the widest maximal run of consecutive *swept* k values in which all three
adjacent-pair metrics pass. A run spanning `m` swept values contains `m-1` adjacent pairs.
`MIN_PLATEAU_RUN = 3`, so at least two consecutive passing adjacent pairs are required. The centre of
an even-length run is the **lower-index** (smaller k) member — the tie-break is stated here so it is
deterministic rather than incidental. If no run of length `>= 3` exists there is no defensible
plateau, and the notebook halts with remediation options enumerated rather than silently substituting
a different criterion such as smallest-connected-k.
