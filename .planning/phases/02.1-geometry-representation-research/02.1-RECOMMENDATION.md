## GEOM-03 — What 41% Negative Mass Means

Classical MDS reconstructs coordinates from a distance matrix by double-centring its squared
entries into a Gram matrix `B = -1/2 J D^2 J` and eigendecomposing it. When `D` is genuinely a
Euclidean distance matrix, `B` is positive-semidefinite by construction and every eigenvalue is
the squared extent of the embedding along one principal direction — a non-negative quantity,
because a squared Euclidean length cannot be negative. A negative eigenvalue of `B` is therefore
not a small numerical residue of that picture; it is a direction along which the input metric
behaves as though a squared distance had been *subtracted* rather than added — a direction no
real Euclidean coordinate can represent at all, because no real vector has a negative squared
norm. Isomap inherits this exactly: `D` here is the graph geodesic distance matrix, so a negative
eigenvalue of its double-centred `B` says the geodesic metric itself, not merely the embedding
sklearn produced from it, is not Euclidean-embeddable.

This spectrum's specific shape is what makes the finding a finding rather than a curiosity. Of the
full 10,000-eigenvalue spectrum, **5,029 are strictly negative**, and together they carry
`m = 0.412071` — 41% — of total absolute eigenvalue mass. `r = 0.052419` passes its own 0.10 bound
at every `k` tested (`02-FINDINGS.md` §4.4), which is the second half of the shape: this is not
one short-circuit edge inflating a single eigenvalue into dominance. It is 5,029 directions, none
individually dominant, collectively carrying mass comparable to the positive part. A spectrum with
one large negative eigenvalue and an otherwise clean tail would be a data-quality problem — a bad
edge, a broken distance. A spectrum with a long, diffuse negative tail carrying 41% of the mass is
a description of the metric itself: roughly half of this manifold's directions of variation are of
a kind that no flat target space, at any dimension, can hold.

`02-FINDINGS.md` §8 states the reading that survives any disagreement about where this project's
own pass/marginal/fail bounds should sit: **at 41% of absolute mass, the negative part is not a
correction to a Euclidean picture — it is comparable in magnitude to the positive part.** That
claim does not depend on the 0.05/0.15 thresholds chosen for this project's gate; it would be true
under any reasonable threshold, because 41% is not a number in the neighbourhood of a rounding
error under any convention.

**Corroborating routes, cited rather than re-argued.** `02-FINDINGS.md` §5 reports
`CURVE_DIVERGENCE_MAX = 0.697664` at `d = 5` between the Tenenbaum residual curve (`1 - R^2`
between geodesic and embedded pairwise distances) and an eigenvalue-based residual curve
(normalized by positive mass only) — a 70-percentage-point disagreement between two curves both
bounded in `[0, 1]`, because the eigenvalue-based curve, blind to the negative 41%, reports far
more variance captured than the geodesic-vs-embedded `R^2` actually finds. The negative mass thus
surfaces through a second, independent route, not only the direct eigenvalue count. Every negative
eigenvalue's magnitude sits 10–11 orders of magnitude above the float64 noise floor
(`7.174e-09` at `k=15`) — real structure, not rounding. And four separate experiments closed the
alternative explanations available at the time: numerical error and implementation bug (the
audited spectrum reproduces to `rtol=1e-8` against sklearn's own truncated cross-check), kNN hop
inflation (`m(k)` flat-to-slightly-increasing across `k in {5,10,15,30}` while `GEO_AMBIENT_RATIO`
fell 2.83→1.86 and `LONG_EDGE_FRACTION` rose 0.0065→0.0139, so densification demonstrably worked
and bought nothing), L2 normalization (`m` moves 0.28% when normalization is exactly inverted),
and both the survey column (paired HSC gives `m = 0.4226`) and the particular 10,000 objects (a
~90% disjoint resample gives `m = 0.411948` with identical positive/negative counts).

**The one limit carried forward honestly, not dropped.** `02-FINDINGS.md` §6.2 closes the claim
"normalization caused it" but explicitly does not close the broader claim "the data occupies a
shell and shell-like geometry contributes to the negativity." The raw embedding norms were already
tightly concentrated at 16.029 ± 0.504 (cv = 3.1%) *before* L2 normalization was applied — the
embeddings were already near-constant-norm, which is exactly why inverting normalization barely
moved the geometry. That null result is honest about what it licenses and what it does not: it
rules out normalization as the mechanism, and it says nothing about whether shell geometry itself
is part of what the manifold's non-Euclidean structure reflects. This document inherits that limit
unresolved; nothing in plan 02.1-03's evidence bears on it either.

## GEOM-03 — Correction or Retention

**The mechanism.** Cailliez's constant shift, Lingoes' additive constant, and eigenvalue clipping
all force the double-centred Gram matrix to be positive-semidefinite — and therefore force `m` to
zero and the gate to a pass — regardless of what the input distance matrix actually is. Clipping
sets every negative eigenvalue to zero; the Lingoes-type shift adds a constant to every eigenvalue
sufficient to lift the most negative one to zero. Neither operation consults whether the negative
mass reflects genuine non-Euclidean geometry or a data defect. They succeed identically either way,
by construction, which is exactly `02.1-RESEARCH.md`'s Pattern 1 and Pitfall 2 claim.

**Shown, not asserted.** Plan 02.1-03 ran both corrections against three spectra and reported the
result directly in `notebooks/.cache/geometry_probes_43cf438bc944c509.json`'s
`correction_blindness` block:

| Input | `m` before correction | `m` after clipping | `m` after Lingoes-type shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real spectrum (`eigvals_all`, the audited fit) | 0.412071 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| `synthetic_flat` (n=1,000, genuinely flat R^20 Euclidean sample; `m` is already ~4.05e-15, indistinguishable from zero) | ~0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| `synthetic_nonmetric` (n=1,000, random symmetric matrix with no metric structure at all) | 0.500649 | 0.0 | 0.0 |

A spectrum with 41% negative mass from a real curved geodesic metric, a spectrum that was already
flat, and a spectrum with no metric structure whatsoever — 50% negative mass and nothing geometric
underneath it — are all three corrected to the identical `0.000000`. That is the demonstration
`02.1-RESEARCH.md`'s Pattern 1 and Pitfall 2 describe from the literature, now measured on this
project's own data rather than cited from elsewhere: a 100% pass rate across correction methods is
what those methods are engineered to guarantee, not evidence about the geometry they were applied
to. Any method that hides the negativity rather than representing it is, on this evidence, a
method that cannot fail — diagnostic-blind by construction — and for a project whose stated
purpose is to *measure* curvature, adopting one silently would delete the very signal the project
exists to detect and manufacture a false PASS.

**The judgment, and the condition it is tested against.** A blanket rule that no correction could
ever be justified is not a judgment, it is a prohibition dressed as one — so the honest form of the
question is: under what condition *would* a correction be principled here? The defensible
condition is that a correction is principled when the negativity it removes is known, on
independent grounds, to be measurement error rather than geometry — a bad edge, a broken distance,
a sampling artifact — rather than a description of the underlying metric itself. Tested against
that condition, this data fails to meet it: `02-FINDINGS.md`'s four experiments (§4, §6, §7)
systematically tested and eliminated every measurement-error explanation available — numerical
error, kNN hop inflation with an active densification control, L2 normalization by exact
inversion, the specific survey column, and the specific 10,000 objects — and the negativity
survived every one of them at essentially constant magnitude (0.4064 to 0.4157 across a 6× range
of `k`; 0.4226 on the paired HSC column; 0.411948 on a ~90% disjoint resample). There is no
independent ground left on which to call this measurement error. **The judgment is retention, not
correction**: the negative 41% is treated as signal about the geometry, because every avenue by
which it could instead be an artifact has been tested and closed.

**The one legitimate role a correction keeps.** A correction earns a place only as a labelled
contrast baseline — reported together with the explicit statement that it cannot fail regardless
of input, so a reader can see directly what forcing flatness on this data would have looked like,
and why it was not adopted as the project's representation. The table above *is* that baseline,
stated for exactly that purpose and no other.

**Where this leaves the literature's own alternative.** The 2024 Neuc-MDS line of work
(bilinear-form optimisation that chooses which eigenvalues to keep by minimising stress, rather
than simply truncating each sign's top-`k`) is the articulated principled alternative to blanket
correction that the literature offers. `02.1-SURVEY.md`'s Pseudo-Euclidean/Krein-space subsection
records it at its actual evidence strength: abstract-level description only, no maintained Python
implementation found in this project's research session — treat as research-code-or-paper-only,
not as something this phase could have run. The practical fallback plan 02.1-03 actually measured
— simple top-`p`-per-sign, top-`q`-per-sign pseudo-Euclidean retention — is the lower-risk
implementation this survey's Standard Stack Alternatives table names, not a compromise forced by
missing tooling for a method this document was otherwise going to prefer.

**This section and the next answer each other, by construction.** `02.1-RESEARCH.md`'s
Entanglement item 4 states plainly that this is not two separable questions: recommending a
pseudo-Euclidean/Krein representation *is* the retention stance, stated in a different vocabulary.
The next section's recommendation is therefore not an independent choice layered on top of this
judgment — it is this judgment, applied. Neither section could be edited without breaking the
other.

## GEOM-04 — Recommendation

**This phase recommends pseudo-Euclidean / Krein-space retention**: for each of the 10,000
points, a coordinate vector in `R^(p+q)` built from the top-`p` positive and top-`q` most-negative
eigenpairs of the already-computed double-centred geodesic Gram matrix, with distances computed
under the indefinite bilinear form `sum_a sign(lambda_a) * (x_i,a - x_j,a)^2` rather than the
ordinary Euclidean sum of squares. This is `02.1-RESEARCH.md`'s Entanglement item 4 made concrete:
it is the retention judgment above, and not a second, independently-argued choice.

**Argued against the pre-registered Evaluation Criterion.** The relevant number is
`DISTORTION = median(|d2_rep - d2_geo| / d2_geo)` over the fixed 200,000-pair sample, with `q = 0`
in the same ladder as its own classical-MDS baseline:

| p | q | `DISTORTION` (median_abs_rel) | mass captured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0.750521 | 0.064269 |
| 8 | 0 | 0.121024 | 0.160774 |
| 18 | 0 (`q=0` best) | **0.079864** | 0.182637 |
| 25 | 0 | 0.099228 | 0.192361 |
| 40 | 0 | 0.179641 | 0.208089 |
| 25 | 8 | 0.069532 | 0.209715 |
| 40 | 25 (full-ladder best) | **0.065190** | 0.244267 |

Retaining negative directions produces a real, measured improvement: the full-ladder best
(`p=40, q=25`) is an **18.4% relative reduction** in median distortion from the `q=0` baseline's
own best (`p=18`) — `0.065190` vs `0.079864`, `reduction_fraction = 0.183742` in
`geometry_probes_43cf438bc944c509.json`'s `krein_falsifier_condition_b` block. This is modest in
absolute terms (6.5% vs 8.0% median relative distortion) and even the best-measured rung captures
only 24.4% of total eigenvalue mass at `p+q=65`, consistent with Phase 2's own finding of a long
diffuse tail rather than a handful of dominant negative directions. But it is a genuine,
non-cosmetic gain, unlike the correction methods above, which produce an identical `m = 0` on any
input and therefore no measurable geometric information at all. That contrast — a real, if modest,
distance-preservation benefit versus a guaranteed pass that means nothing — is what separates a
principled retention argument from a cosmetic one.

**The delta-hyperbolicity reading, applied to settle what single-curvature target the retained
directions are consistent with.** `02.1-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s Delta-Hyperbolicity Reading Rule,
applied verbatim: the real manifold's `delta_rel_max` at matched `n=2,000` is **0.386330**, against
a tree anchor of ~0 and a flat-20-dimensional-Euclidean anchor of **0.360433**, with a threshold of
`tree + 0.1*(euclidean - tree) = 0.036043`. The real value is nowhere near that threshold — it
does not merely fail to look tree-like, it **exceeds the flat-Euclidean anchor itself**
(0.386 > 0.360, and at `n=10,000` the same reading holds: 0.383921 vs the same 0.360433 anchor).
`single_curvature_defensible = False`. This geometry is *less* tree-like than flat Euclidean space
is under this estimator, which rules out a single constant negative curvature as a defensible
model of it — see GEOM-04's Alternatives Rejected section below for what this eliminates.
Pseudo-Euclidean/Krein retention makes no such single-curvature assumption at all: `p` and `q` are
free integers chosen from measured distortion, not a curvature constant fit to the whole manifold,
which is exactly why this candidate survives a result that eliminates the hyperbolic family.

**What this recommendation assumes that could be false.** Three things, stated so the
recommendation is falsifiable rather than merely justified: (1) that simple top-`p`/top-`q`
per-sign truncation is close enough to whatever an optimised bilinear-form selection (Neuc-MDS)
would achieve — untested, since no maintained implementation of that method exists to compare
against; (2) that the elbow-selected working dimension below is the right operating point for
Phase 3's decoder even though a further, real 18.4% distortion reduction is available at a much
higher total dimension — a tradeoff Phase 3 will need to weigh explicitly, not inherit silently
(see GEOM-05 below); and (3) that this document's own reading of the 18.4% reduction as real,
non-cosmetic signal is correct — if a stricter definition of "materially below" than this document
applies were used, the falsifier's condition (b) could be judged to have tripped after all, which
combined with condition (a) already tripping would overturn this entire verdict (see Evidence
below).

## GEOM-04 — Alternatives Rejected

Five candidates, one subsection each. None is rejected on difficulty; each is rejected on a
specific assumption the data contradicts, a Phase 3 requirement incompatibility, or a cost with a
number attached, cited to `02.1-SURVEY.md`.

### Hyperbolic (Poincare ball / Lorentz hyperboloid)

**Rejected on evidence, not preference.** Its defining assumption — one constant negative
curvature everywhere on the manifold — is directly contradicted by the delta-hyperbolicity result
above: the real manifold's `delta_rel_max` (0.386330 at n=2,000) does not merely fail to clear the
reading rule's threshold (0.036043), it sits *past* the flat-Euclidean anchor (0.360433) that the
threshold was built to be far below. A hyperbolic embedding assumes tree-like structure this data
positively does not have, on this estimator, at this sample size. `02.1-SURVEY.md`'s Hyperbolic
subsection records the cost this rejection also avoids: Riemannian SGD at a wall-clock class of
one or more full Isomap fits (78.5–104.5s each), against `geoopt` 0.5.1 (SUS, `unknown-downloads`,
`no-repository`), behind its own blocking `checkpoint:human-verify` gate. **What would change the
answer:** a different fit, sample, or architecture whose delta-hyperbolicity reading landed near
the tree anchor rather than past the Euclidean one — this is not a permanent verdict on hyperbolic
representations in general, only on this specific measured geometry.

### Mixed-curvature product manifold

**Rejected on the same delta-hyperbolicity evidence, plus an unresolved model-selection cost.**
`02.1-RESEARCH.md`'s stated preference for this family over pure hyperbolic was that curvature is
plausibly heterogeneous rather than a single global value — but a product of constant-curvature
factors that includes a hyperbolic factor inherits the same single-curvature-per-factor
commitment the delta result argues against for any one factor claiming to model this manifold's
tree-likeness. `02.1-SURVEY.md`'s subsection also records that this family substitutes a
model-selection problem — how many factors, which signature — for hyperbolic's single parameter,
a choice this survey never resolved even provisionally; it was never carried into this phase's
shortlist (`02.1-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s Shortlist Rule fixed three candidates before any evidence
existed, and this was not one of them). **What would change the answer:** a resolved
factor/signature choice plus a delta-hyperbolicity-style test applied per-factor rather than to
the whole manifold at once — neither exists yet.

### Diffusion maps

**Rejected on cost and on the plain fact that zero evidence was ever measured for it.** Its only
actively-maintained package (`datafold` 2.0.2) is Supporting-tier per `02.1-RESEARCH.md`'s
Standard Stack and strictly heavier than what the pre-registered shortlist needed, so
`02.1-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s Shortlist Rule explicitly excluded it from this phase's
evidence-gathering tasks. It is not eliminated by the delta-hyperbolicity result — it carries no
single-curvature assumption for that result to contradict — but this document's own prohibition
against presenting a claim above its evidence strength forbids recommending it: unlike
pseudo-Euclidean/Krein's measured 18.4% distortion reduction, no distortion number was ever
computed for diffusion maps against this manifold. **What would change the answer:** running its
own probe — a kernel build plus a leading-eigenvector solve, ~1 dense-eigensolve cost class per
`02.1-SURVEY.md` — and measuring where its distortion curve lands relative to the `q=0` baseline.
Absent that measurement, it remains the most plausible fallback if Phase 3 finds the recommended
representation wanting, not a second-ranked candidate this document can rank today.

### Ollivier-Ricci curvature

**Rejected on the fork test, a requirement-text incompatibility rather than a preference.**
`02.1-FORK.md`'s ratified verdict is structural: Ollivier-Ricci produces a scalar curvature value
per graph edge, no coordinate vector at all, so it cannot supply DEC-01's decoder with anything to
fit against — CURV-01 and CURV-02's batched Jacobian/Hessian path has no coordinate domain to
differentiate over. `02.1-SURVEY.md`'s cost section records what this rejection also avoids: three
SUS-flagged packages (`GraphRicciCurvature`, `POT`, `networkx`, the last not installed at all),
each behind its own blocking `checkpoint:human-verify` gate, a not-yet-built synthetic
tree-and-expander fixture, and a Sinkhorn-approximate optimal-transport solve over 150,000 edges.
**What would change the answer:** per the Falsifier's own symmetric framing (see Evidence below),
only both delta-hyperbolicity failing to distinguish this geometry from flat Euclidean space *and*
the pseudo-Euclidean ladder never dropping distortion materially below the `q=0` baseline would
argue for revisiting toward this branch. The measured 18.4% reduction is exactly the kind of
material improvement the falsifier's symmetric statement treats as evidence *against* moving to
the graph-native branch, not merely a failure to trigger a switch.

### Forman-Ricci curvature

**Rejected for the identical reason as Ollivier-Ricci** — graph-native, no coordinate vector,
same fork-test incompatibility, same bucketing consequence for DEC/CURV per `02.1-FORK.md`.
`02.1-SURVEY.md` records it as the lower-cost variant of the same graph-native slot (a closed-form
combinatorial formula rather than an optimal-transport solve, so no `POT` dependency), but that
cost advantage does not change the fork-test outcome, and it was not independently evaluated
because Ollivier-Ricci already covers this branch's cost/maturity/demand profile in full.
**What would change the answer:** the same condition as Ollivier-Ricci's — nothing specific to
Forman-Ricci alone, since the two share the branch's structural disqualification entirely.

## GEOM-04 — Evidence It Will Be Judged Against

**Checks Phase 3 can actually run.** (1) Held-out decoder reconstruction error (DEC-03/DEC-04) at
the elbow-selected working dimension should be roughly consistent with the ~12.1% median relative
squared-distance distortion measured directly on those coordinates (the `p=8, q=0` row of the
`krein_ladder`) — a trained decoder that reconstructs far worse than this baseline would indicate
the coordinate representation itself, not the decoder architecture, is the bottleneck. (2) Whether
choosing the higher-dimension `(p,q)=(40,25)` rung instead — which measures the 18.4% distortion
reduction to 6.5% — meaningfully improves decoder-side reconstruction or curvature-field stability
(CURV-04's conditioning, CURV-05's non-degenerate second derivatives); if Phase 3 finds no benefit
at the higher dimension either, that would undercut this document's reading of the 18.4% reduction
as real signal rather than sampling noise on the 200,000-pair distortion statistic. (3) Whether the
curvature field CURV-01..CURV-03 compute on the retained coordinates behaves sanely on the
CURV-06/CURV-07 synthetic controls before being trusted on the real fit — unaffected by this
phase's representation choice in kind, but the specific coordinate domain those controls must be
matched against is now this phase's, not Isomap's.

**The pre-registered falsifier, restated with its status.** `02.1-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s Falsifier
required *both* (a) the real manifold's `DELTA_REL` sitting far from the tree anchor —
indistinguishable from, or closer to, the flat-Euclidean anchor than the reading rule's threshold
allows — *and* (b) the pseudo-Euclidean ladder never dropping distortion materially below the
`q=0` classical-MDS baseline at any rung, in order to overturn the coordinate-producing verdict.
**Condition (a) trips, and by a wide margin**: `delta_rel_max = 0.383921` (n=10,000) exceeds the
flat-Euclidean anchor (0.360433), not merely falls short of the tree anchor. **Condition (b) does
not cleanly trip**: the ladder does drop distortion below the `q=0` baseline, by a real, if modest,
18.4% relative amount (§GEOM-04 Recommendation above). Because the falsifier requires *both*
conditions and only one trips, **it does not fire — the coordinate-producing verdict stands, as
ratified in `02.1-FORK.md`.** This document reads that outcome as close enough to the edge of the
pre-registered logic to flag rather than treat as a clean pass: condition (a)'s margin eliminates
the hyperbolic branch specifically (see Alternatives Rejected above), and condition (b)'s modest
18.4% is the entire quantitative basis for preferring retention over the classical-MDS baseline
that already failed Phase 2's gate. If Phase 3's own evidence (check (2) above) does not
corroborate that 18.4% as meaningful, this recommendation should be revisited at that point — a
dated, documented amendment, not a quiet reversal.

**Generality — architecture-specific, not yet shown to generalise.** Matching `02-FINDINGS.md`
§8's own framing: DINOv3 ViT-B/16 is the one variable never varied across every fit this milestone
has produced, and every measurement this document cites — the eigenspectrum, the delta-
hyperbolicity anchors, the `(p,q)` distortion ladder — was computed on that one architecture's
embedding of this one astronomical population. This document's recommendation is therefore
**architecture-specific as stated**, not yet shown to generalise, and should be read as a decision
for this fit rather than a general claim about deep vision embedding geometry. The packaged
35-model cross-architecture sweep (`sweep/`, `02-MODEL-SWEEP-PREREGISTRATION.md`) is prepared but
not yet run; its result bears directly on this question. If the sweep shows other architectures
pass Phase 2's eigenspectrum gate cleanly (small or no negative mass), that would mean this
pseudo-Euclidean/Krein recommendation is specific to DINOv3's embedding geometry rather than a
general decoder-input strategy for this kind of pipeline. If the sweep instead shows a similar
diffuse-negative-mass pattern across architectures, that would support the representation
generalising and would strengthen the case, deferred in `REQUIREMENTS.md`'s Future Requirements
under LIB-02, for eventually promoting this representation strategy into `src/effdim/`. Leaving
this question implicit is exactly what `02.1-RESEARCH.md`'s Open Question 3 asks this phase not to
do.

## GEOM-05 — Working Dimension

**The number the recommendation implies.** Re-derived from the recommended representation's own
distortion-versus-dimension curve, under the identical kneedle criterion `gate_verdict_43cf438bc944c509.json`
records verbatim (`elbow_criterion`): the pseudo-Euclidean best-per-total-dimension frontier's
elbow lands at **`(p, q) = (8, 0)`** — total dimension 8, zero negative directions retained at this
specific budget. The same criterion applied to the `q = 0` classical-MDS curve alone gives the
identical `classical_p = 8`. Reported as a pair per the Working-Dimension Rule's explicit
prohibition on collapsing a pseudo-Euclidean dimension into one integer — here that pair happens to
have `q = 0`, which is itself the finding worth stating plainly rather than glossing (see below).

**Placed beside `D_FROZEN = 5` and the other four estimates:**

| Estimate | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Residual-curve elbow, frozen by Phase 2 | **`D_FROZEN = 5`** | `02-FINDINGS.md` §5, Tenenbaum residual curve elbow |
| Eigenvalue-based residual-curve elbow (Phase 2, second curve) | 8 | `02-FINDINGS.md` §5, `CURVE_DIVERGENCE_MAX = 0.697664` at `d=5` between this curve and the one above |
| This phase's classical (`q=0`) distortion-curve elbow | **8** | `geometry_probes_43cf438bc944c509.json`, `working_dimension.classical_p` |
| This phase's pseudo-Euclidean frontier elbow | **(8, 0)** | `geometry_probes_43cf438bc944c509.json`, `working_dimension.krein_p`/`krein_q` |
| Median of 8 geometric `compute_dim` estimators (Phase 1) | 18 (`n_components` used for every fit) | `02-FINDINGS.md` §6.4 |
| TwoNN maximum-likelihood estimator | ~19.5 (measured 19.48) | `02-FINDINGS.md` §6.3 |
| Local PCA, 90% local variance | ~25 (median 25.0, std 2.01, range 19–29) | `02-FINDINGS.md` §6.3 |

**Two readings that pull in opposite directions, both stated rather than averaged away.** First,
`02-FINDINGS.md` §6.4's reading: with 41% of mass negative, the Tenenbaum residual curve saturates
early not because 5 is the intrinsic dimension, but because the flat embedding fails at every
dimension — adding coordinates past ~5 buys little when the target space is the wrong kind of
space, consistent with `CURVE_DIVERGENCE_MAX = 0.697664` between the two residual curves at exactly
`d=5`. On this reading the elbow of 5 plausibly measured the failure rather than the geometry.
Second, `02.1-RESEARCH.md`'s Entanglement item 2: a curved or indefinite target space can represent
the same structure in *fewer* dimensions than a flat one needs, so the 18–25 local
intrinsic-dimension cluster does not, on its own, refute 5 either — a striking example RESEARCH.md
found is a hyperbolic embedding reaching MAP = 0.989 in two dimensions where 200-dimensional
Euclidean embeddings did worse.

**What this phase's own re-derivation actually settles, and what it does not.** The re-derived
elbow — 8, on *both* the classical `q=0` curve and the pseudo-Euclidean frontier — is not the
5-versus-18-25 question restated a third way. It is a distortion-versus-dimension curve computed
directly from the 200,000-pair geodesic sample under the identical elbow criterion applied to a
different, non-`R^2` statistic than the one that produced 5. It lands closer to 5 than to the
18–25 cluster, and it corroborates the *other* curve Phase 2 itself computed (the eigenvalue-based
residual curve, elbow 8, `CURVE_DIVERGENCE_MAX = 0.697664` against the Tenenbaum curve's elbow of
5) rather than the curve that produced 5. Two independently-computed curves — Phase 2's
eigenvalue-based residual curve and this phase's distortion-vs-dimension curve on the classical
baseline — now agree on 8; only the one curve `02-FINDINGS.md` §6.4 already flagged as most likely
distorted by the negative mass (the Tenenbaum `1-R^2` curve) produced 5. **`D_FROZEN = 5` is
therefore REVISED, not inherited and not discarded**: superseded by a directly comparable
re-derivation under the identical pre-registered criterion, corroborated by an independent Phase 2
curve, rather than declared wrong on the 18–25 cluster's say-so alone (which this document does not
do, per the Working-Dimension Rule's explicit prohibition). The working dimension of record for
this recommendation is **`(p, q) = (8, 0)`**, not 5 and not 18–25.

The 18–25 cluster is not thereby refuted — it answers a different question. Those are *local*
intrinsic-dimension estimates (TwoNN, local PCA) of the data manifold's own tangent-space
dimension in ambient space; 8 is a *global*, diminishing-returns budget on a specific pairwise
distortion statistic under a curvature criterion designed to find where adding more coordinates
stops paying for itself. `02-FINDINGS.md` §6.4 records that `n_components = 18` — already below the
measured local intrinsic dimension — left 100% of neighbourhoods needing more than 18 dimensions
for 90% of local variance: every Phase 1/2 fit was dimension-starved relative to the manifold's own
local structure. That fact is unaffected by this section's dimension re-derivation and is not
resolved by it; it constrains what any fixed-dimension embedding, including this phase's
recommended `(8, 0)`, can represent of the manifold's fine local structure, even where the elbow
criterion says 8 total dimensions is where this specific global distortion statistic's returns
diminish.

**The `(8, 0)` versus `(40, 25)` tension, stated plainly rather than split silently.** The
elbow-selected working dimension retains *zero* negative directions — the pre-registered rule,
applied without exception, says the minimal working budget for this distortion statistic does not
need them. The best *measured* distortion, `0.065190` at `(p,q)=(40,25)`, is real and available,
but it sits far past the elbow, in the flat tail of the curve where the kneedle criterion — by its
own definition — does not select it: 57 additional total dimensions purchase a further 1.5
percentage points of absolute median distortion reduction (8.0% → 6.5%), which is exactly the kind
of point the elbow rule exists to reject as diminishing returns. **The pre-registered
Working-Dimension Rule governs here, not the point of lowest absolute distortion**, because
`02.1-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s Prohibited Moves section explicitly forecloses revising a rule — or
picking whichever point on a curve is convenient — after the evidence exists; the rule was fixed
before this ladder was computed for exactly this reason. **This does not weaken the GEOM-03/GEOM-04
retention judgment.** The representation itself still does not force the Gram matrix to be
positive-semidefinite, and a future user with a larger dimension budget remains free to recover the
measured 18.4% improvement by choosing `(40, 25)` instead — the elbow's `(8, 0)` is this phase's
answer to "what is the minimal working dimension," not a ceiling on what the representation can do
or a retraction of the retention argument. Phase 3 should read `(8, 0)` as the default working
dimension this recommendation freezes, and `(40, 25)` as a documented, available alternative if
Phase 3's own decoder-side evidence (Evidence check (2) above) shows the extra dimensions are worth
their cost.

**Chosen representation.** Pseudo-Euclidean / Krein-space retention: coordinates in `R^(p+q)` from
the top-`p` positive and top-`q` most-negative eigenpairs of the double-centred geodesic Gram
