## GEOM-01 — Flat-Target Class Membership

**What the flat-target assumption is, precisely:** a manifold-learning method's objective or
solver seeks an embedding into a Euclidean space and treats the input dissimilarities (distances,
neighbourhood weights, or probabilities) as if they were exactly realisable in that flat target —
i.e. as if the input distance matrix were Euclidean-embeddable at some finite dimension. Isomap
and classical MDS make this assumption explicitly, via the double-centred Gram matrix they
eigendecompose. Phase 2 measured that assumption failing on this data: 5,029 of 10,000
eigenvalues of that Gram matrix are strictly negative, carrying `m = 0.412071` — 41% — of total
absolute eigenvalue mass, with `r = 0.052419` passing its own bound (no single dominant outlier;
a long, diffuse negative tail instead) and every negative eigenvalue's magnitude sitting 10–11
orders of magnitude above the float64 noise floor. That is not exploratory uncertainty — it is a
measured, reproduced-four-ways fact about this manifold's geodesic metric.

**The finding this section exists to establish:** the flat-target assumption is shared far more
widely across manifold-learning methods than the diagnostic that exposed it. LLE, Hessian LLE,
LTSA, Laplacian eigenmaps, MVU, t-SNE and UMAP all target a flat Euclidean output space — the same
assumption Isomap makes — but none of their solvers can produce a computable negative-eigenvalue
statistic, for one of two distinct mechanisms. Their silence about this data's non-Euclidean
geometry would therefore be structural, not evidential.

### Class-membership table

| Method | Defining objective / solver | Target space flat Euclidean? | Produces a computable Euclidean-validity diagnostic? | Why not, where it cannot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Isomap | Eigendecomposes the double-centred **graph geodesic** distance matrix `B = -1/2 J D_geo^2 J` via classical MDS on top of shortest-path distances | Yes | **Yes** — `B`'s eigenvalues are not forced positive by double-centring; they are negative exactly when `D_geo` is not itself Euclidean-embeddable | — (this is the method whose failure this milestone measured) |
| Classical MDS | Eigendecomposes the double-centred **input** distance matrix `B = -1/2 J D^2 J` directly | Yes | **Yes** — identical mechanism to Isomap, one layer up (no geodesic step) | — |
| LLE (Locally Linear Embedding) | Minimises reconstruction error of each point from a locally-linear combination of neighbours, then finds the low-dimensional embedding whose local reconstruction weights match; the embedding step solves an eigenproblem on a **sparse, PSD-by-construction** matrix `(I-W)^T(I-W)` | Yes | No | `(I-W)^T(I-W)` is a Gram-type product of a real matrix with its own transpose — positive-semidefinite by construction, for any input `W`, regardless of whether the underlying geometry is flat |
| Hessian LLE | Same local-linear-reconstruction family as LLE, but estimates the local Hessian of the embedding via each neighbourhood's local tangent coordinates, then solves an eigenproblem on the resulting quadratic form's null space | Yes | No | The local Hessian estimator's quadratic form is a sum of squared local curvature estimates — positive-semidefinite by construction, same mechanism family as LLE, applied locally rather than globally |
| LTSA (Local Tangent Space Alignment) | Fits a local PCA / tangent space at each neighbourhood, then aligns those local coordinate systems into one global embedding by minimising a global alignment error, itself a PSD quadratic form | Yes | No | Local PCA covariance matrices are Gram matrices of real data — positive-semidefinite by construction; the global alignment matrix built from them inherits the same property |
| Laplacian eigenmaps | Solves a generalized eigenproblem on the graph Laplacian `L = D - W` (or its normalized form), retaining the eigenvectors of smallest eigenvalue | Yes | No | The graph Laplacian is **positive-semidefinite by construction** for any graph with non-negative edge weights (`x^T L x = sum_edges w_ij (x_i-x_j)^2 >= 0`); no input geometry can make an eigenvalue negative |
| MVU (Maximum Variance Unfolding) | Solves a semidefinite program (SDP) that maximises the trace of a kernel/Gram matrix subject to local-distance-preservation constraints **and an explicit PSD constraint on the Gram matrix itself** | Yes | No | MVU's SDP constrains the Gram matrix to the positive-semidefinite cone as a hard optimisation constraint, not a soft regularizer — a negative eigenvalue is not merely unobserved in an MVU solution, it is **infeasible under the solver's own constraint set** [CITED — the semidefinite-program formulation is carried from Weinberger & Saul's original construction via RESEARCH.md's WebSearch-strength sourcing; not independently re-verified against the primary paper in this session, no browsing tool available] |
| t-SNE | Minimises KL divergence between a Gaussian-in-high-dimension neighbourhood probability distribution and a Student-t-in-low-dimension one | Yes | No | Never forms a Gram, Laplacian, or covariance matrix at all — it optimises a divergence between two probability distributions over neighbourhoods via gradient descent; there is no matrix whose eigenvalues could be inspected for a sign |
| UMAP | Minimises a fuzzy-set cross-entropy between a high-dimensional weighted neighbourhood graph and a low-dimensional one, via stochastic gradient descent | Yes | No | Same mechanism family as t-SNE — a probability/fuzzy-membership divergence optimised directly, no Gram-type object is ever constructed |

### The two mechanisms, separated

Two distinct routes to the same consequence (no negative-eigenvalue statistic exists to
inspect), and conflating them would understate how structural the blindness is:

1. **PSD-constrained by construction.** MVU's SDP constrains the Gram matrix to the
   **positive-semidefinite** cone directly; Laplacian eigenmaps' graph Laplacian is
   positive-semidefinite by construction for any non-negative edge weighting; LLE, Hessian LLE
   and LTSA's local Gram/covariance eigenproblems are positive-semidefinite by construction because
   they are built as products of a real matrix with its own transpose (or as local PCA covariance
   matrices). In every one of these five methods, the object the solver eigendecomposes cannot
   carry a negative eigenvalue **regardless of what the input geometry actually is** — flatness is
   built into the solver's own algebra, not discovered by it.
2. **Never forms a Gram matrix at all.** t-SNE and UMAP optimise a divergence between probability
   distributions (or fuzzy-set memberships) over neighbourhoods, via gradient descent on that
   divergence directly. There is no eigendecomposed matrix at any point in either pipeline — the
   question "is this matrix's spectrum partly negative" cannot even be posed against their solvers.

Isomap and classical MDS are the only two methods in this table whose solver forms a
double-centred Gram matrix **without** constraining it to be positive-semidefinite as part of the
optimisation — `B = -1/2 J D^2 J` is PSD if and only if `D` is exactly a Euclidean distance matrix,
which is precisely the property under test. That is the structural reason only these two methods
can expose the failure: they are the only ones whose solver's own algebra does not force the
answer before the input geometry is consulted.

### A second, within-family instance of the same blindness

Even within the one method family that *can* show the failure, the attribute a caller would reach
for by default cannot. `Isomap.kernel_pca_.eigenvalues_` — sklearn's own cached spectrum from its
internal `KernelPCA` step — is truncated to `n_components` (18, in this fit) and sorted by
eigenvalue descending, so it structurally cannot contain a negative eigenvalue either: Phase 2's
audit computed the **full** 10,000-value spectrum by hand from `dist_matrix_` for exactly this
reason (`02-FINDINGS.md` §3.2), because reporting the truncated attribute would have begged the
question. A reader who inspects only `kernel_pca_.eigenvalues_` on an Isomap fit — even this exact
fit — would see nothing wrong, for the same class of reason UMAP and t-SNE see nothing wrong: the
object being inspected has already discarded the part of the spectrum that would show it.

### Reading

Anchored to the measured numbers: of the full 10,000-eigenvalue spectrum, **5,029 are strictly
negative**, carrying `m = 0.412071` of total absolute eigenvalue mass — comparable in magnitude to
the positive part, not a small correction to it — while `r = 0.052419` passes its own bound,
meaning this is not one dominant outlier but a long, diffuse negative tail. Every negative
eigenvalue's magnitude sits roughly ten orders of magnitude above the float64 noise floor
(`7.174e-09` at k=15), so the negative mass is real structure, not rounding. This result was
reproduced across `k in {5, 10, 15, 30}`, across the paired HSC survey column, and across a ~90%
disjoint resample under a new seed with identical positive/negative eigenvalue counts. Only
classical MDS and Isomap — and only when the full spectrum is computed by hand rather than read
from a truncated attribute — can expose that this failure exists at all.

### Consequence

A clean-looking UMAP or t-SNE plot of this data — or a clean-looking `kernel_pca_.eigenvalues_`
read off this exact Isomap fit — would not be evidence that the flat-target problem is resolved.
It would mean the same failure had become **invisible rather than absent**: seven of the nine
methods in this table structurally cannot report a negative-eigenvalue statistic, whether or not
one is present in the underlying geometry, because their solvers are either **positive-semidefinite
by construction** or never form the kind of matrix whose sign could be inspected in the first
place. "Does not show negative eigenvalues" must be read as "does not measure the problem," never
as "does not have the problem." The honest test of any candidate representation, GEOM-02 below
included, is therefore not whether its output looks clean, but whether it carries a validity
diagnostic of its own that could have failed and did not — silence from a method with no such
diagnostic is not reassurance.
