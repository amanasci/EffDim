<objective>
Resolve the one fork that gates every other decision in this phase, and fix in advance every rule
the later plans will judge candidates by.

Purpose: RESEARCH.md's Entanglement section is explicit that this is not one decision among five.
Coordinate-producing candidates (hyperbolic/Lorentz, mixed-curvature product manifolds,
pseudo-Euclidean/Krein, diffusion maps) re-specify the *input* to Phase 3 and leave its DEC and CURV
requirement shape intact. Graph-native candidates (Ollivier-Ricci, Forman-Ricci) produce no
coordinates at all, cannot feed a decoder, and compute an *intrinsic* Ricci quantity where
CURV-01..CURV-03 specifically ask for an *extrinsic* mean-curvature vector — choosing that branch
re-opens Phase 3's requirements rather than re-specifying their input. Everything the remaining
plans do — which candidates the survey emphasises, which diagnostics are worth computing, what the
recommendation can even mean — hangs off which side of that fork this phase lands on. Resolving it
first, on one commit, is the analogue of a tracer slice for a decision phase: it is the thinnest
path that touches every layer of this phase's stack (the frozen Phase 2 evidence, the criterion, the
judgment, and the downstream Phase 3 consequence) and it is the piece whose failure would invalidate
everything built on top of it.

The second reason this plan exists is ordering. This project's established discipline is that
decision rules are committed to git *before* the evidence they will judge — `02-REFIT-PREREGISTRATION.md`
was committed at 057b084 before a single re-fit ran, and Phase 2's report treats that ordering as
what separates a k-sweep from retrying until something passes. RESEARCH.md's Open Question 2 asks
explicitly whether a single evaluation criterion is even fair across the two branches, and
recommends deciding that explicitly rather than letting it be settled by whichever comparison table
gets built first. This plan settles it, in writing, first.

Output: `02.1-PREREGISTRATION.md` and `02.1-FORK.md`, in two separate commits whose ordering is
proved by git ancestry rather than asserted, plus a blocking human ratification of the verdict
before wave 2 builds on it.

Task budget: 2 tasks. The tracer (~35% context) writes both documents across two commits; the
checkpoint carries no context cost. Roughly 35-40% total.
</objective>

<constants_to_pre_register>

These are fixed here, by the planner, before any evidence in this phase has been examined. Task 1
transcribes them into `02.1-PREREGISTRATION.md` with the reasoning for each. They are not open
items for the executor to re-choose; they are open only to being *justified more fully* in prose.

### The fork test (RESEARCH.md Entanglement item 1)

A candidate is **coordinate-producing** iff it yields, for each of the 10,000 points, a finite
real coordinate vector from which a twice-differentiable map into R^768 can be fitted — which is
precisely what DEC-01's C2-smooth decoder and CURV-01..CURV-03's batched Jacobian/Hessian path
consume. A candidate that produces only per-node or per-edge scalars, with no coordinate vector to
differentiate a decoder against, is **graph-native**.

Failing the test does not disqualify a candidate. It relocates it: a coordinate-producing winner
re-specifies Phase 3's input and leaves DEC-01..DEC-05 and CURV-01..CURV-08 intact; a graph-native
winner replaces the extrinsic mean-curvature vector with an intrinsic Ricci quantity and therefore
re-opens that requirement set. The fork verdict is a statement about which of those two futures the
milestone is in, and nothing else.

### The evaluation criterion (RESEARCH.md Open Question 2, resolved explicitly)

**Two arguments, not one score.** Coordinate-producing candidates are compared on a single
computable number: relative squared-geodesic-distance distortion over the fixed 200,000-pair sample
already cached by Phase 2 at `r2_pair_seed = 20260731`. Graph-native candidates are **not** scored
on that axis at all, because they do not attempt to preserve distances and any number produced for
them on that axis would be a category error dressed as a comparison. They are judged on two
qualitative grounds instead: whether they answer the question CURV-01..CURV-03 poses, and the shape
of their curvature distribution.

Registering the incommensurability in advance is the point. It removes the option of inventing a
unified score after seeing which branch it would flatter.

### The distortion statistic

`DISTORTION = median( |d2_rep − d2_geo| / d2_geo )` over the 200,000-pair sample, reported together
with the **signed** median relative deviation `median( (d2_rep − d2_geo) / d2_geo )` so that
systematic over- or under-estimation is visible and not hidden inside an absolute value. Both are
computed on squared distances, because squared distance is what classical MDS and its
pseudo-Euclidean generalisation reconstruct exactly; comparing unsquared distances would silently
discard the sign information that is the entire subject of this phase.

### The working-dimension rule (GEOM-05)

The working dimension is re-derived **per candidate**, from that candidate's own
distortion-versus-dimension curve, using the identical kneedle criterion Phase 2 recorded verbatim
in `gate_verdict_43cf438bc944c509.json`'s `elbow_criterion` field: both axes range-normalised to
[0, 1], elbow at the point of greatest perpendicular distance from the chord joining the first and
last normalised point, ties to the lower index.

For a pseudo-Euclidean candidate the dimension is a **pair (p, q)** — positive and negative
directions — reported as a pair and never collapsed into a single integer, because collapsing it
would discard exactly the distinction the representation exists to make.

`D_FROZEN = 5` is neither inherited nor declared wrong. It is re-derived under this rule and the two
numbers placed side by side. RESEARCH.md is explicit on why the naive move is unsafe: a curved
target space can represent the same structure in *fewer* dimensions than a flat one needs, so the
18-25 local intrinsic-dimension estimates do not refute 5 on their own.

### The delta-hyperbolicity reading rule (RESEARCH.md Pitfall 3, Assumption A7)

Statistic: `DELTA_REL = 2 * delta / diam_sample`, the standard Gromov relative four-point delta,
where `delta` is taken at the maximum and the 95th percentile over sampled quadruples and
`diam_sample` is the maximum pairwise geodesic distance in the same sample.

Constants: `DELTA_QUADRUPLES = 200_000`, `DELTA_SEED = 20260801`.

Reading rule, fixed before the number is computed and calibrated rather than thresholded in the
abstract: the real manifold's `DELTA_REL` is reported next to two calibration anchors computed by
the *identical* estimator at matched n — a synthetic weighted tree metric, where the four-point
condition gives `delta` identically zero, and a synthetic sample from a flat 20-dimensional
Euclidean space. If the real value sits at or below the tree anchor plus one tenth of the gap to the
Euclidean anchor, a single constant negative curvature is a defensible model and a pure hyperbolic
target is supported. Otherwise it is not, and the mixed-curvature/product or pseudo-Euclidean branch
is preferred. Calibrating against two anchors rather than asserting a bare threshold is the same
move Phase 2 made when it refused to attach pass/fail bounds to `GEO_AMBIENT_RATIO` after the k=15
value was already known.

### The (p, q) ladder

`P_LADDER = (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 18, 25, 40)`, `Q_LADDER = (0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 25, 40)`. Ceiling 40
on both, matching Phase 2's `d_sweep_max = 40` and the width of the cached `eigvecs_top` block, so
the ladder costs one bottom-40 eigenvector solve and nothing more. `q = 0` is retained deliberately:
it *is* the classical-MDS baseline, and having it in the same table is what lets a reader see what
retaining the negative directions buys.

### The shortlist rule

At most three candidates are carried into the evidence stage. They are named in the pre-registration,
before any evidence is examined, and chosen on the fork test plus implementation maturity as recorded
in RESEARCH.md's Standard Stack — never on how well a candidate scored, since no candidate has scored
anything yet.

### The scope constant on dependencies

No package outside the existing venv (numpy 2.5.1, scipy 1.18.0, scikit-learn 1.9.0, torch 2.13.0+cpu,
joblib, pytest 9.1.1) may be installed to satisfy this phase. All seven candidates in RESEARCH.md's
Package Legitimacy Audit returned SUS. If the phase's own evidence stage turns out to require one,
the phase halts at a blocking human-verify checkpoint and the install is ratified there — it is never
a quiet `pip install` inside a task. This is registered as a scope constant rather than left to
judgment so that the SUS gate is structural.

### The falsifier

The pre-registration states, before the verdict exists, what finding would overturn a
coordinate-producing verdict and what finding would overturn a graph-native one. A verdict without a
stated falsifier is a preference.
