# Phase 02.5 Stage-1 Pre-Registration — Amendment 1

**Ratified 2026-08-08, at the plan 02.5-07 Task 3 GO/NO-GO checkpoint, before the two
additional seeds it authorizes have been measured.**

This document is an **amendment**: it changes a rule. `02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` Section 14
states the consequence for exactly this case — "a changed constant requires a fresh,
separately-ratified amendment document, committed alone with its own ancestry proof, and a
full re-run of the stage-1 sweep — never a silent edit." That is what this is.

`eecdb15c70dc3f833e31c54ec7864543579dddb7` remains in the record, unmodified and
unsuperseded. `02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` itself is **not edited** by this amendment.

## 1. The trigger, stated with the numbers that produced it

`02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` Section 9 ratified "3 seeds at the base cell" as a **non-gating
sensitivity axis**, and defined `CURVATURE_VERDICT` at the base cell alone (seed `20260807`).
The seed axis then produced this:

| seed | `spearman_rho` | `quantile_bin_concordance` | cell verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `20260807` (the base cell) | 0.520469 | **0.444446** | **FAIL** |
| `20260808` | 0.562711 | 0.483705 | PASS |
| `20260809` | 0.609409 | 0.523633 | PASS |
| mean | 0.564196 | 0.483928 | |
| sd (`ddof=1`) | 0.044489 | 0.039594 | |

Nothing differs between these three cells except the fixture seed: same `d=20`, `D=768`,
`n=10000`, `k=30`, `n_bumps=8`, `density_correct=True`, `k_density=30`, `density_skew=0.0`,
same floors, same `REGION_PAIR_SEED`.

The base cell misses `REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR = 0.4750` by **0.0306**. The across-seed spread
is **0.0792**, or **2.6x that margin**. Two of the three seeds clear both gates.

**Therefore the sealed verdict rule is not identified by the data it consumes.** Reporting
`FAIL` from one draw, when the same configuration returns `PASS` on two other draws, would
present a coin-flip as a measurement. Reporting `PASS` would be the same error with the
opposite sign. The defect is in the verdict rule's *sample size*, not in the estimator, not
in the floors, and not in the choice of statistics.

## 2. What this amendment changes — exactly two things

**Change 1 — `SEEDS`: 3 → 5.** Section 9's "3 seeds at the base cell" becomes **5 seeds at
the base cell**. The seed *values* were never ratified (Section 9 fixes the count; the values
are the runner's own orchestration choice, so recorded in `notebooks/diagnostics/
curvature_feasibility_sweep_run.py`), and become:

```
SEEDS = (20260807, 20260808, 20260809, 20260810, 20260811)
```

**Change 2 — `CURVATURE_VERDICT` is computed from the 5 seed replicates, not from the base
cell alone.** For each of the two gating statistics `S`:

```
mean_S   = mean(S_1 .. S_5)
sd_S     = std(S_1 .. S_5, ddof=1)
se_S     = sd_S / sqrt(5)
lower_S  = mean_S - T_MULTIPLIER * se_S

PASS(S) iff  lower_S > max(floor_S, mean null threshold for S)
         AND every one of the 5 seed cells cleared its own permutation null

T_MULTIPLIER = 2.132       # one-sided t(0.95, df = 5 - 1 = 4)
floor_spearman_rho              = 0.50     (UNCHANGED)
floor_quantile_bin_concordance  = 0.4750   (UNCHANGED)

CURVATURE_VERDICT = PASS  iff  PASS(spearman_rho) AND PASS(quantile_bin_concordance)
```

`T_MULTIPLIER = 2.132` is tied to `n = 5` (`df = 4`). The runner **must assert
`len(SEEDS) == 5`** before applying it, so a later change to the seed count cannot silently
reuse a multiplier derived for a different degrees-of-freedom.

The strict comparison itself is still performed only inside `curvature_probe._apply_gates`,
via `verdict_from_stage1_metrics`, exactly as before — this amendment changes *what value is
fed to that comparison*, never how the comparison is done.

### 2a. Why a confidence bound rather than a median, a majority, or unanimity

Ratified reason, recorded because it is load-bearing for how a later reader should read a
stage-1 result: the question stage 1 exists to answer is whether Phase 3/4 can be **built on**
this estimator. That requires positive evidence the configuration clears the floor, not
evidence that it clears on a typical draw. A lower confidence bound is the standard rule for
exactly the failure this amendment responds to — a marginal single-draw result whose spread
exceeds its own margin — and it was selected for that structural reason.

It is also, by construction, **direction-neutral with respect to the three already-observed
values**: it tightens as the seeds agree and loosens as they disagree, regardless of where
their centre sits. A median rule would have been decided largely by the observed
`median(0.444446, 0.483705, 0.523633) = 0.483705 > 0.4750`; a unanimity rule would have been
decided entirely by the observed `0.444446 < 0.4750`. Both were live options at ratification
and both were declined for that reason, not for their outcome.

This preserves `02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` Section 3h's ratified posture verbatim — "sets a
deliberately high bar before Phase 3/4 is built on this estimator" — while removing the part
of that posture that was accidental rather than chosen: that the bar's height depended on
which single seed happened to be `BASE_CELL`'s.

## 3. Ordering disclosure — read this before reading any result

**Three of the five seed values were already measured, and their values were known to the
orchestrator, at the moment this amendment's aggregation rule was written and ratified.**
They are the table in Section 1. This is disclosed here, in the sealed document, rather than
discovered later from the git log.

What follows from that, stated plainly:

1. **Seeds `20260810` and `20260811` are unmeasured** at the time of this ratification. They
   have never been run at any configuration. They are the only genuinely blind inputs to the
   new rule.
2. **The observed three were kept rather than discarded** deliberately. Replacing them with
   five fresh seeds was the other live option at ratification and was declined: the three
   values are already in the record and in `notebooks/.cache/`, so dropping them would amount
   to choosing which measured data counts toward a verdict — a worse failure than the
   ordering problem it would purport to fix. Extending is the transparent option; it is not
   the flattering one.
3. **No floor moved.** `SPEARMAN_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR = 0.50` and `REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR = 0.4750`
   are byte-identical to Section 3g's derivation, which predates every number in Section 1.
   Neither was reconsidered here, in either direction.
4. **The rule can still return FAIL, and on the most favourable available extrapolation it
   does.** Substitute the three observed values and assume the two unmeasured seeds land
   *exactly at the observed mean* — the assumption most favourable to a PASS, since it adds
   zero to the sum of squared deviations while raising `df` from 2 to 4:

   ```
   quantile_bin_concordance:  mean 0.483928  sd(5) 0.027997  se 0.012521
                              lower = 0.483928 - 2.132 * 0.012521 = 0.457234
                              floor 0.4750                          -> FAIL
   spearman_rho:              mean 0.564196  sd(5) 0.031458  se 0.014069
                              lower = 0.564196 - 2.132 * 0.014069 = 0.534202
                              floor 0.50                            -> PASS
   ```

   So even under the best case for the two blind seeds, `quantile_bin_concordance` misses its
   floor by 0.0178 and `CURVATURE_VERDICT` is **FAIL**. On that arithmetic the amendment as
   ratified points at FAIL, not at PASS. It is ratified anyway, because the point is to make
   the verdict identified, not to change its sign.

## 5. Cost, measured rather than estimated

A non-base seed-replicate cell cost **33.6 s** in the sealed run (`20260808`: 33.6 s;
`20260809`: 33.6 s), since seed replicates carry `OTHER_CELL_QUADRIC_TIMEOUT_S = 30.0`, not
the base cell's extended budget. Two additional seeds therefore add **~67 s**.

Sealed run: 25 cells, **1544.9 s** (25.7 min). Amended run: 27 cells, projected **~1612 s
(~26.9 min)** against the unchanged 30-minute budget. **No budget amendment is required, and
none is made.** If the real run overruns anyway, Section 9's existing rule governs unchanged:
the cell is recorded as `timed_out` with its partial state and the absence is named, never
silently dropped.

`SEEDS` is a member of `SWEEP_CFG`, which `cache.config_key` hashes into `stage1_key`.
Changing it therefore produces a **new** `stage1_key` and forces the full re-run Section 14
requires — mechanically, not on trust. The sealed run's artifact
(`curvature_feasibility_12e5e16efd940369.json`) is not overwritten and remains readable.

## 6. Implementation constraint this amendment imposes

Following Section 9's own precedent — that a computation any part of a gate depends on must
be a **tested module function**, not runner-inline arithmetic — the lower-bound computation
must be added to `notebooks/pu_manifold/curvature_probe.py` with its own tests, not written
inline in `curvature_feasibility_sweep_run.py`. Its tests must pin, at minimum: the exact
`T_MULTIPLIER` for `n=5`; that a zero-variance replicate set returns the mean itself; that
the bound decreases as spread increases at fixed mean; that a non-finite or short input
raises rather than returning a value; and the reproduction of Section 3(4)'s worked
`lower_qbc = 0.457234` / `lower_rho = 0.534202` arithmetic from the three observed values
plus two at the mean.

## 7. Limitations of this rule, stated before the measurement

(a) **`n = 5` is a small sample.** The `t` interval is wide and the `sd` estimate is itself
noisy at `df = 4`. This rule makes the verdict *identified*; it does not make it *precise*.

(b) **It assumes across-seed variation is approximately symmetric.** Three points cannot test
that. If the five realized values are strongly skewed, the bound is conservative in an
uncontrolled direction, and that must be reported alongside the verdict rather than absorbed.

(c) **It does not address the estimator's magnitude error.** `median_relative_error` was
0.870441 / 0.854214 / 0.766985 across the three observed seeds — the estimator gets curvature
magnitudes ~77-87% wrong at every one of them. Both gating statistics are rank-based and
neither sees this. A PASS under this amendment would mean "the ordering clears with
confidence," never "the estimator is accurate."

(d) **A PASS here would still sit close to the floor.** Even the best observed seed
(`qbc 0.523633`) clears `0.4750` by 0.0486. Nothing in this amendment turns a marginal
estimator into a good one, and a PASS must not be read downstream as if it had.

## 8. Consequence of changing this rule

Same as Section 14, applied to this document: once this amendment is committed with ancestry
proof, `SEEDS`, `T_MULTIPLIER`, the aggregation rule, and both unchanged floors may not move
without a further separately-ratified amendment, committed alone with its own ancestry proof,
and another full re-run. Not a silent edit.
