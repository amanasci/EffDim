**`CURVATURE_VERDICT = FAIL`.**

```

                           lower = 0.484832 - 2.132 * 0.014357 = 0.454223
                           threshold 0.475000                     -> MISSES by 0.020777
spearman_rho:              mean 0.565454  sd 0.036837  se 0.016474

every replicate cleared its own permutation null:  True
```

Section 3h's option-scale-C requires both to clear. One does, one does not, so the verdict is
FAIL.

## 1. The disclosure that must come first: the rule decided the outcome

Four aggregation rules were live at the amendment's ratification checkpoint. Applied to the
five measured replicates, **they do not agree**:

| candidate rule | value | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **confidence-bounded mean (RATIFIED)** | `lower = 0.454223` | **FAIL** |
| median of 5 | `0.483705` | **PASS** |
| majority (≥3 of 5 cells clear) | 3 of 5 clear | **PASS** |
| unanimity (all 5 clear) | 2 of 5 fail | FAIL |
| base cell only (the original sealed rule) | `0.444446` | FAIL |

**Two of the four candidates would have returned PASS.** A reader is entitled to know that
before being told the answer, and to know that the rule was chosen — from those four, with the
trade-offs stated — *before* seeds `20260810` and `20260811` existed as numbers.

What makes that choice defensible is ordering, not confidence:

- The amendment was sealed at `12cca56eefcfa904af42ea43fbcf88a7279845a5`, **committed alone**
  (`git show --name-only`: one file, nothing under `notebooks/`), and the runner **proved**
  before computing anything that this commit is an ancestor of `HEAD` **and** a descendant of
  the pre-registration `eecdb15c70dc3f833e31c54ec7864543579dddb7` it amends — the second check
  being what stops a rule change from being back-dated ahead of the document it modifies.
- The amendment's own Section 3 discloses that three of the five seed values were already
  measured and known when its rule was written, and Section 3(4) commits, in the sealed text,
  to the arithmetic that rule implies: `lower_qbc = 0.457234` (FAIL) and
  `lower_rho = 0.534202` (PASS). **Realized: `0.454223` and `0.530332`.** The sealed
  prediction was 0.003011 from the measured value on the gate that decided the verdict.
- That arithmetic is pinned in
  `test_seed_replicate_lower_bound_reproduces_the_amendments_worked_arithmetic`, so the
  document's own prediction cannot drift away from what the code computes.

What that ordering does **not** buy: it does not make FAIL the objectively correct reading of
these five numbers. It makes FAIL the reading of a rule that was fixed before the deciding
data existed. Those are different claims and this document does not conflate them.

## 2. The five replicates

Identical configuration throughout — `d=20, D=768, n=10000, k=30, n_bumps=8,
density_correct=True, k_density=30, density_skew=0.0` — with **only** the fixture seed
differing.

| seed | `spearman_rho` | `quantile_bin_concordance` | cell verdict | `median_relative_error` | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `20260807` (BASE_CELL) | 0.520469 | 0.444446 | **FAIL** | 0.8704 | previously measured |
| `20260808` | 0.562711 | 0.483705 | PASS | 0.8542 | previously measured |
| `20260809` | 0.609409 | 0.523633 | PASS | 0.7670 | previously measured |
| `20260810` | 0.594335 | 0.508334 | PASS | 0.8307 | **blind** |
| `20260811` | 0.540348 | 0.464041 | **FAIL** | 0.8337 | **blind** |

**The two blind seeds bracketed the previously-observed range rather than extending it.**
`20260810` (0.508334) landed second-highest, `20260811` (0.464041) second-lowest. Consequences:

- The mean barely moved: `0.483928` (3 seeds) → `0.484832` (5 seeds).
- The spread **tightened**: `sd 0.039594` → `0.032103`.
- The interval narrowed accordingly, and the bound still missed. The FAIL is not an artifact
  of a wide interval produced by two wild draws; it is what a *tighter* estimate produced.

**3 of 5 cells clear both floors individually.** This is the same instability that triggered
the amendment, now measured at `n=5` instead of `n=3` rather than resolved by it. The
estimator clears the region-scale floor on most draws of the same configuration and misses it
on some, and the amended rule's answer is that "most draws" is not the standard for building
Phase 3/4 on an estimator.

## 3. The divergence between the two statistics is the diagnostic

`spearman_rho` clears its floor with room (`lower 0.530332` vs `0.500000`). `quantile_bin_
concordance` misses (`lower 0.454223` vs `0.475000`). That split is not noise about a single
underlying quality — Section 3h ratified option-scale-C precisely to expose it.

Reading it: the estimator retains usable **global** rank information across the whole point
cloud, while failing at the **region** scale that Phase 4's quantile partitioning actually
consumes. A statistic that only asks "does the estimate order two points from genuinely
different true-curvature buckets correctly" is the one it fails. This identifies the error as
**structural** rather than noise-shaped, which is what Section 3h said the divergence would
diagnose.

Corroborating, and unchanged by the amendment: `median_relative_error` is **0.767–0.870
across all five seeds** — the estimator gets curvature *magnitudes* 77–87% wrong at every
single replicate, including the three that clear both gates. Both gating statistics are
rank-based and neither sees this. **A PASS under any of the four candidate rules would have
meant "the ordering clears," never "the estimator is accurate."**

## 4. The boundary is unchanged from the sealed 3-seed run

Every non-seed cell reproduced bit-identically (same seeds, same fixtures, same estimator).
`density_correct=True` variant shown; `PASS` means both floors cleared at that cell.

| axis | values |
|---|---|
| **`d`** | 2 → 0.7292 PASS · 5 → 0.8022 PASS · 10 → 0.6558 PASS · **20 → 0.4444 fail** · 25 → 0.4418 fail |
| **`D`** | 28 → 0.4444 · 50 → 0.4444 · 200 → 0.4444 · 768 → 0.4444 — **bit-identical** |
| `n` | 1000 → 0.3118 · 3000 → 0.3941 · 10000 → 0.4444 — rising, never clears |
| `k` | 30 → 0.4444 · 50 → 0.4535 · 100 → 0.4666 — rising, never clears; 10/15/20 `infeasible_k_le_d` |
| `n_bumps` | 1 → 0.5135 PASS · 8 → 0.4444 fail |
| `density_skew` | 0.0 → 0.4444 · 3.0 → 0.4268 — skew hurts |
| `ladder_d` | 8 → 0.7512 PASS · 16 → 0.5225 PASS · **20 → 0.4444 fail** · **24 → 0.4814 PASS** · 32, 40 `infeasible_k_le_d` |

Four points, all carried forward from `02.5-FINDINGS.md` unmodified:

**(a) The crossing is in intrinsic `d`, between 10 and 20.** Not in ambient `D`.

**(b) Ambient dimension is irrelevant — bit-identical across a 27× change in `D`.** The
failure is driven entirely by the intrinsic dimension of the neighbourhood being sampled.
This confirms the mechanism named in `02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` Section 13b: the curse of
dimensionality against a second-order expansion is about sampling a `d`-dimensional
neighbourhood, which is intrinsic. **Section 13b's conclusion must not be carried across to
the decoder arm** — see `02.5-NOTE-randomized-trace.md`, which corrects exactly this framing
error.

**(c) `ladder_d` is non-monotone.** `d=24` clears (0.4814) while `d=20` fails (0.4444). That
is the same ~0.03–0.08 noise scale sloshing around the threshold that the seed axis exposed
directly. **Recorded as noise, not as structure in `d`.**

**(d) `k > d` is a hard structural floor**, not a tuning preference. `local_tangent_basis`
cannot extract a `d`-dimensional basis from `k ≤ d` neighbours, so `k ∈ {10,15,20}` at `d=20`
and `d ∈ {32,40}` at `k=30` are recorded `infeasible_k_le_d` — not measurable, neither PASS
nor FAIL. The point-cloud estimator is squeezed between needing `k > d`, needing `k` large
enough for statistics, and needing `k` small enough to stay local. **No `recommended k` is
emitted by this document or by the runner**, per D-04/Section 8's own prohibition.

## 6. Limitations

(a) **`n = 5` is a small sample.** The `t` interval is wide and the `sd` estimate is itself
noisy at `df = 4`. The amendment made the verdict *identified*; it did not make it *precise*.

(b) **Symmetry of the across-seed distribution is assumed and not tested.** Five points cannot
test it. The realized values are not obviously skewed (two above the mean, three below, range
0.0796 roughly centred), but this is an assumption, not a finding.

(c) **The verdict is close to the floor on the gate that decided it** — missing by 0.020777,
which is smaller than the across-seed `sd` of 0.032103. A sixth seed would move it.

(d) **Neither gating statistic sees magnitude error at all**, and the magnitude error is large
(Section 3). This limits how much a PASS could ever have meant.

(e) **Nothing here constrains stage 2.** Section 4(b)/(d)'s mechanisms — intrinsic-`d`
neighbourhood sampling and the `k > d` floor — are properties of the *point-cloud* estimator.
The decoder arm differentiates an analytic map instead of sampling a neighbourhood and is
subject to neither. `02.5-NOTE-randomized-trace.md` records this argument in full.

## 7. Routing

`02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` Section 10 is **unchanged by the amendment** and governs: the phase
**halts for a user decision with no auto-fallback**. Stage 2 does not run by default; a
stage-1 negative is a complete and reportable outcome for this phase.

Two live options, named without choosing between them:

- **Ollivier-Ricci / intrinsic curvature** — defined on the k-NN graph via optimal transport,
  never touches the ambient space, and so is not subject to the sampling constraint that just
  failed. Section 10 names this one explicitly.
- **Stage 2's Arm B (decoder / chart curvature)** — escapes both the intrinsic-`d` sampling
  constraint and the `k > d` floor, per Section 6(e).

No sealed verdict is reopened by any of this. No Phase 3 handoff was written, under either
verdict — stage 1 writes none under any circumstance.
