# Note — why the point-cloud estimator failed at `d=20`, and which approaches survive it

**Status:** analysis note. Records (a) the measured mechanism behind stage 1's
`CURVATURE_VERDICT = FAIL`, (b) an assessment of a user-supplied external agent's comparison
of local-quadratic vs autoencoder vs randomized-trace estimators, and (c) candidate approaches
for high intrinsic dimension. Written 2026-08-08, after the amended 5-seed run
(`stage1_key e71a4ea18050ea20`) and before any stage-2 work. **Gates nothing.** Companion to
`02.5-NOTE-randomized-trace.md`; neither note amends `02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md`.

## 1. The measured mechanism: locality is unattainable in a `d=20` point cloud

The centroid estimator inverts an identity that is exact only as the neighbourhood radius
`r -> 0`. The question is therefore not "is the formula right" (it is — verified to `8.9e-16`
against autodiff, exact on the sphere at `d in {2,3,5,8}`) but **how small `r` actually is at
each `d`**. Measured, `k=30`, `n=10_000`, median kNN ball radius against the manifold's own
median radius:

| `d` | `r_knn` | manifold `R` | **`r/R`** | `(r/R)^2` | measured `median_relative_error` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (Swiss roll) | 0.1674 | 1.4521 | **0.115** | 0.013 | 0.125 |
| 5 | 1.4433 | 3.6908 | **0.391** | 0.153 | 0.346 |
| 8 | 2.4018 | 4.1031 | **0.585** | 0.343 | 0.481 |
| 10 | 2.9274 | 4.3356 | **0.675** | 0.456 | 0.604 |
| 16 | 4.1803 | 4.9724 | **0.841** | 0.707 | 0.790 |
| **20** | 4.8565 | 5.3606 | **0.906** | 0.821 | **0.870** |
| 25 | 5.6020 | 5.8047 | **0.965** | 0.931 | 0.875 |

**At `d=20` the 30-nearest-neighbour ball has a radius equal to 91% of the manifold's own
radius.** It is not a local neighbourhood. It is most of the manifold. At `d=2` it is 11.5%,
which is genuinely local — which is exactly why the Swiss roll passes and `d=20` does not.

The cause is elementary and unfixable by tuning: filling a `d`-ball with `k` points requires

```
r / R  ~  (k / n)^(1/d)
```

The exponent `1/d` is what kills it. At `d=20`, cutting `r/R` in half requires `2^20 ≈ 10^6`
times more data. **This is why the `n` axis rose without ever clearing**: `n = 1000 -> 10_000`
is 10x the data and shrinks `r/R` by only `10^(-1/20) = 0.891`, an 11% reduction — and moved
`quantile_bin_concordance` only `0.3118 -> 0.4444`, exactly the small improvement the scaling
predicts. The sweep's `n` axis was not a weak trend; it was the `n^(-1/d)` law being measured.

### 1a. The resulting error is incoherent across points, not a clean scale bias

Measured at `n=4000`, `k=30`, `density_correct=True` — per-point ratio `||H_est|| / ||H_true||`:

| `d` | median ratio | **CV of the per-point ratio** |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.905 | **0.250** |
| 5 | 1.096 | 0.639 |
| 10 | 1.531 | 1.004 |
| 20 | 0.591 | **2.250** |

At `d=2` the failure mode is a mild ~9.5% underestimate with modest scatter — a *correctable*
signature. At `d=20` the scatter is **2.25x the mean**. The median ratio itself is not even
monotone in `d`, so there is no scale factor to calibrate out.

This is the expected consequence of `r/R = 0.91`: the `O(r^2)` truncation error is
deterministic given the sample but varies from point to point, because each "neighbourhood"
covers a large and differently-shaped chunk of the manifold. **It behaves like noise while
being bias.**

**Consequence for remedies:** smoothing the estimate over the k-NN graph does *not* help. The
error decorrelates at the same spatial scale as the signal, because the neighbourhoods
generating it are the size of the manifold. Averaging over a region averages the signal away
with the error. This rules out the cheapest candidate fix, and it is why the phase halts
rather than reaching for one.

### 1b. What this generalizes to

**Every estimator that defines locality by a k-NN ball in the ambient point cloud inherits
this**, regardless of what it does inside the ball. Centroid, local quadratic, local PCA,
kernel/diffusion with a shrinking bandwidth, random directional fits — all of them need a
neighbourhood that is simultaneously small relative to the curvature scale and populous enough
to average. At `d=20`, `n=10^4`, no such neighbourhood exists. The failure is a property of
the *sampling geometry*, not of the estimator built on top of it.

## 2. Assessment of the external agent's comparison

The user supplied an analysis comparing (A) local quadratic patch, (B) encoder-decoder
derivatives, (C) randomized directional trace. Recorded here with what our own measurements
say about each claim.

### 2a. Correct, and confirmed by our data

**Its Section 8 warning — fitting `d(d+1)/2` coefficients then discarding all but the trace —
is real, and we measured it collapse.** Our non-gating `quadric_mean_curvature` cross-check is
exactly that method:

| `d` | `k` | quadratic coefficients | short by | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 30 | 3 | 0 | completes, `quadric_spearman_rho = 0.99997` |
| 10 | 30 | 55 | 25 | underdetermined |
| 16 | 30 | 136 | 106 | underdetermined |
| **20** | **30** | **210** | **180** | underdetermined |
| 20 | 100 | 210 | 110 | still underdetermined |
| 25 | 30 | 325 | 295 | underdetermined |

At `d=20` the fit needs `k > 210` to be determined at all, and `k=210` is not a local
neighbourhood — so it trades an underdetermined system for a worse-biased one. At `d=2` it is
3 coefficients, fully determined, and recovers curvature at `rho = 0.99997`.

**Its "the curse is `d`, not `D`" correction is right, and we measured it directly:**
`D = 28, 50, 200, 768` give **bit-identical** gating values. 768-dimensional ambient space
costs nothing. Only the 20 intrinsic dimensions do. Section 1's table is the mechanism.

**Its bias-variance framing of neighbourhood size is the right frame** and maps onto our `k`
axis exactly.

### 2b. Aimed at a method this phase does not gate on

The analysis assumes the gating estimator is Method A (full quadratic patch, then trace). **It
is not.** The gate is the centroid / Laplace-Beltrami estimator, which never forms the second
fundamental form at all — it reads `tr(II)` directly out of the mean neighbourhood
displacement. Zero quadratic coefficients estimated, never underdetermined, ~30s per cell.

Its boxed conclusion — *"don't estimate `B` if you only need `tr B`"* — **is already what the
gate does.** The quadric fit exists only as the non-gating cross-check, and is non-gating
precisely because its cost was known in advance.

**Therefore Method C solves an efficiency problem this phase does not have.** Randomized
directional fitting would comfortably beat Method A. It does not beat the centroid estimator,
which is already cheaper and already trace-direct — and which still fails at `d=20` with an
87% magnitude error and a per-point CV of 2.25. Per Section 1b, Method C is a point-cloud
estimator and inherits the same `r/R = 0.91` wall. **Random directions do not add neighbours.**

### 2c. Convention error — the expensive kind

The analysis writes `H = (1/d) * sum_i P_N Q[e_i,e_i]`, the AVERAGED convention. This module
is pinned to `CURVATURE_CONVENTION = "trace"`, `H = tr(II)`, with a regression guard.
Transcribing its formulas verbatim introduces a **factor-of-`d` = 20 error** against every
fixture, every SUMMARY number, and the sealed gate. This codebase has already shipped and
fixed exactly one factor-of-`d` scale bug (`2*(d+2)/r2 -> 2*d/r2`). Same warning as
`02.5-NOTE-randomized-trace.md` Section 1; repeated because the source material repeats the
convention.

### 2d. The genuinely new item worth carrying into 02.5-08

Its Section 5 observation that a learned decoder **"can also smooth genuine curvature"** is a
real, specific, and testable failure mode that neither this phase's notes nor plan 02.5-08
currently guard against. A decoder trained to reconstruct will happily regularize the bumps
flatter than they are, producing a curvature field that is smooth, well-ordered, highly
rank-correlated with the truth — and systematically wrong in amplitude.

**Both stage-1 gating statistics are rank-based and are exactly blind to this.** A decoder
that compressed every curvature magnitude by a constant factor would score a perfect `1.0` on
both.

**Required check for plan 02.5-08, recorded here:** train the decoder on the
graph-of-function fixture (whose analytic `H` is known in closed form), then compare
`||H_decoder||` against `||H_true||` on **amplitude**, not only on rank — at minimum the
median ratio and the per-point CV reported in Section 1a's format, so a compressed-amplitude
decoder is visible rather than hidden behind a high Spearman.

## 3. Candidate approaches for high intrinsic dimension

Ordered by whether they escape Section 1's wall. **None is chosen here** — Section 10's
halt-for-user-decision governs, and this note gates nothing.

**(1) Learned parametrization, differentiated analytically (stage 2's Arm B).** The only
candidate that structurally escapes the wall. Locality is defined in the `d`-dimensional
*latent* space and the derivatives are taken by autodiff at a point, so the `r -> 0` limit is
achieved exactly rather than approximated by a ball of radius `0.91 R`. No neighbourhood is
ever formed, so `r/R` does not appear anywhere in its error. **Cost:** it substitutes model
bias for sampling bias — Section 2d's smoothing failure mode is the specific risk, and it is
testable against the same fixtures. This is what `02.5-NOTE-randomized-trace.md` argues, now
with a measured number behind it rather than an argument.

**(2) Intrinsic curvature on the graph (Ollivier-Ricci).** Changes the *quantity* rather than
the estimator: it is defined on the k-NN graph via optimal transport and never references the
ambient space or a tangent plane, so no `r/R` locality requirement exists. **Cost:** it is not
mean curvature. It answers a different geometric question, and whether that question serves
Phase 3/4 is a separate decision, not a substitution. Named as the live option in
`02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` Section 10.

**(3) Reduce `d` before estimating.** `r/R ~ (k/n)^(1/d)` is benign at `d <= 10` — our own
table shows `d=10` at `r/R = 0.675` and still clearing both gates. If the curvature of the PU
manifold is concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace, estimating on a `d <= 10` reduction is
feasible where `d=20` is not. **Cost:** requires establishing that the discarded directions
are near-totally-geodesic, which is itself a curvature claim — potentially circular, and would
need its own pre-registered check.

**(4) Ruled out by Section 1a: graph smoothing / region averaging of the pointwise estimate.**
The cheapest fix, and it does not work. The error decorrelates at the same spatial scale as
the signal, so averaging removes both. Recorded as a negative result so it is not re-proposed.

**(5) Ruled out by Section 1b: any other point-cloud second-order estimator.** Randomized
directional fits, local PCA variants, kernel/diffusion with shrinking bandwidth. All inherit
`r/R = 0.91` at `d=20`, `n=10^4`. Improving what happens *inside* the ball cannot fix a ball
that is the size of the manifold.

**The single sentence:** at `d=20` with `n=10^4`, no local neighbourhood exists in the point
cloud, so the remaining options are to get locality from a learned parametrization (1), to
stop asking for extrinsic mean curvature (2), or to lower the intrinsic dimension (3).
