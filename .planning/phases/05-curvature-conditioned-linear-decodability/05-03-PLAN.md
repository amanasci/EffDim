  </decision>
  <context>
This is a one-way door. The pooled field determines the bucket edges; the bucket edges are frozen
at `05-04`; and a pre-registration amended after a number exists is a rationalization, not a
pre-registration. Changing the pooling rule after the freeze invalidates the phase and costs a
fresh pre-registration plus a complete re-run.

D5-04 locks *that* the three seeds are pooled and rates the choice one-way. It does not lock *how*,
and the how turns out to matter more than the that. Present the developer with `05-02`'s measured
numbers before naming any option: the three per-seed `H_norm` medians, the three distinct-value
counts, the three pairwise Spearman correlations, and the three pairwise median direction cosines.
Quote the measurements you took, not `03-09-SUMMARY.md`'s — and if yours disagree with the recorded
1359.0 / 51437.9 / 70794.1 medians and the 4 / 3 distinct-value counts, say so, because that
changes what is being decided.

The reason a plain average is not on the table as a silent default: at the recorded spread, raw
`np.mean` weights the three seeds at roughly 1.1 percent, 41.6 percent and 57.3 percent. Two of
those three fields take only a handful of distinct values, one per surviving chart, so the pooled
field's per-point ordering would be a chart-index lookup wearing a curvature field's name. The one
seed whose field varies continuously would contribute almost nothing.

Phase 03.1 already found the curvature ordering was not seed-consistent under the `scale` prior.
If `05-02`'s pairwise Spearman values are near zero, then no normalization rescues the premise —
pooling would be averaging fields that share no signal, and D5-05 requires the record to say so
while still reporting the verdict the frozen rule produces. That outcome is not a reason to stop;
it is a reason to name it now, in the pre-registration, rather than discover it in the findings.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="per-seed-median-divide">
      <name>Per-seed median divide (RESEARCH's recommendation)</name>
      <pros>
Each seed's `H_norm` is divided by its own median before the three are averaged, so every seed
contributes at comparable scale and no seed can dominate by raw magnitude. It is a multiplicative
rescale, so it preserves each seed's own within-seed relative structure exactly — the continuously
varying seed keeps its continuous variation, and the piecewise-constant seeds keep theirs. It is
one line, it is trivially invertible, and it is the more conservative of the two in the sense that
it discards no information.
      </pros>
      <cons>
It equalizes central tendency, not spread. A seed whose field is near-constant still contributes a
near-constant term after the divide, so it still dilutes the varying seed's signal — just at 1/3
weight rather than at 98 percent. The pooled field will still be partly a chart-index lookup; the
rule bounds the damage rather than removing it.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="per-seed-rank-uniform">
      <name>Per-seed percentile rank to [0, 1], then average</name>
      <pros>
Every seed becomes exactly uniform on `[0, 1]` before averaging, so scale, spread and outliers are
all neutralized and the pooled field is a pure consensus of the three orderings. Since the phase's
headline statistic is a rank/bucket comparison anyway, working in rank space end to end is
internally consistent, and it makes the pooled field robust to the collapsed-metric magnitudes
entirely.
      </pros>
      <cons>
It discards magnitude information completely, including the distinction between a seed with real
continuous variation and a seed with four distinct values — a piecewise-constant field becomes four
flat rank plateaus with equal weight to the varying seed's full ordering. Ties inside the constant
seeds have to be broken by a stable argsort, which imposes an arbitrary within-plateau order that
then enters the average. The pooled field is no longer a curvature magnitude in any units, which
makes the `||H||` axis harder to describe honestly in the findings.
      </cons>
    </option>
    <option id="single-seed-headline">
      <name>Abandon pooling: use seed 20260813 alone as the verdict field</name>
      <pros>
The only seed measured to vary continuously per point becomes the field, with no averaging artifact
of any kind, and the other two seeds become pure diagnostics. If `05-02`'s pairwise Spearman values
are near zero this is arguably the honest reading: three fields that share no signal should not be
averaged into one that appears to.
      </pros>
      <cons>
It contradicts D5-04, which is a locked user decision, not a discretion item. Selecting it requires
the developer to explicitly amend D5-04 at this checkpoint, and the amendment must be recorded in
`05-PREREGISTRATION.md` and `05-FINDINGS.md` as a decision change with its reason. It also trades
the three-seed robustness argument for a single-seed result whose dispersion is unknown.
      </cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Select: per-seed-median-divide, per-seed-rank-uniform, single-seed-headline, or describe a different rule. The selected string becomes POOLING_METHOD and is frozen verbatim at 05-04.</resume-signal>
