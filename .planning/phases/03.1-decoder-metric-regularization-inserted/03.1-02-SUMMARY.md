## Accomplishments

- Two new tests in `test_decoder_priors.py` pin that nested `decoder_prior_active` blocks compose additively — the inner block's `cae.chart_loss` patch calls the outer's patched function rather than replacing it, both penalties land in `total`, `recon`/`xent` are untouched, and both `finally` blocks unwind LIFO on normal exit and on exit by exception. `decoder_priors.py` itself is unmodified: D-12 is discharged entirely by test.
- The D-14 checkpoint was resolved `full-scope`: the notebook covers `scale`, `christoffel`, and the nested `scale`-outer/`christoffel`-inner combination, against a `weight=0` CAE baseline and a matched `cae.PlainAutoEncoder` — five fits total.
- `christoffel` had never been timed on the Swiss roll before this plan. An initial calibration at the reference notebook's `batch=64` measured five-fit totals of 148-235s depending on epoch count — over the 180s acceptance bound. Raising `batch` to 240 cut christoffel's per-epoch cost sharply (`CHRISTOFFEL_MAX_ROWS_PER_CHART` caps rows evaluated per chart per batch, so fewer/larger batches means fewer capped Hessian evaluations per epoch), landing a 35-epoch, batch=240 run at 121.2s measured total wall-clock — comfortably under budget with margin for curvature diagnostics and plotting.
- Prior weights are derived at notebook init from a measured fraction (0.5) of the base `chart_loss` on one training batch, not guessed: `SCALE_WEIGHT ≈ 7.48e-3`, `CHRISTOFFEL_WEIGHT ≈ 3.81e-4`.
- Measured read-out: `scale` (mse_per_dim 0.0957) and the nested `combo` (0.0926) both beat the matched `PlainAutoEncoder` (0.1757) by a wide margin; `christoffel` alone (0.2037) does not. All four CAE arms retained 4/4 surviving charts. Structurally, `scale`'s `log10_det_g` moved toward 0 versus baseline (1.482 → 0.929) and `christoffel`'s `metric_condition_number` fell versus baseline (9.61 → 6.85) — both hold, even though `christoffel` alone does not win on reconstruction fidelity at this epoch budget.
- The notebook's own read-out states this plainly: at this epoch budget, `scale` is doing the work in the combination arm, and `christoffel`'s reconstruction contribution is not demonstrated here — a real, reportable result about the prior at this budget, not a hedge.

## Decisions Made

- **D-14 resolved `full-scope`** (checkpoint, user-selected): all three prior modes plus both baselines, not the narrower `scale`+`christoffel`-only option, because the combination cell runs on the saddle in `03.1-05` and dropping it here would leave that arm's composition proven by unit test alone.
- **Batch size raised to 240 to fit the wall-clock budget**, deviating from the 02.2 reference notebook's `batch=64`: `christoffel_penalty` subsamples at most `CHRISTOFFEL_MAX_ROWS_PER_CHART=8` rows per chart per batch regardless of batch size, so a larger batch means fewer batches per epoch and proportionally fewer (capped) Hessian evaluations, without changing `christoffel`'s row-coverage-per-batch. This was measured, not assumed: batch=64 configurations at 15-70 epochs put five-fit totals at 148-235s; batch=240 at 35 epochs measured 108-122s across repeated calibration runs.
- **Prior weights calibrated from a measured fraction of base loss** rather than reusing `decoder_priors.py`'s own docstring example weight (`1e-2` for both modes): at `weight=1e-2`, `christoffel`'s raw penalty was ~13x the base `chart_loss` at init — dominant enough to distort training away from reconstruction. Measuring each mode's raw (`weight=1`) penalty against base loss and targeting a stated 50% fraction gave `SCALE_WEIGHT≈7.48e-3` (close to the docstring's `1e-2`) and `CHRISTOFFEL_WEIGHT≈3.81e-4` (over 25x smaller than the docstring's `1e-2`), avoiding that distortion.
