## 4. Why the phase stopped

The screen ranks substrates by agreement between decoder-pullback curvature and analytic `H`.
That score is a composite of three separable properties:

1. **Did the architecture learn the right surface?** (the substrate question the phase asked)
2. **Are that trained net's second derivatives trustworthy?** (never tested)
3. **Is pullback the right curvature approximator?** (assumed)

A neural net fits values, not derivatives. A decoder can sit on the manifold at 14.7%
reconstruction error while its second derivatives are junk. The screen measures the composite
and attributes the result to (1). Nothing in the phase separates (1) from (2), so a low `rho`
is not evidence about substrate choice — it is evidence about the composite.

The user's judgement, on being shown this: the substrate question and the
curvature-approximation question are two separate tasks, and ranking architectures should be
done on how well they preserve the underlying topology/manifold, decided before and
independently of how curvature gets approximated.

### Recorded as an after-measurement change

`02.6-SCREENING-RULE.md` was ratified blind and committed **before** any 02.6 measurement
existed — verified at the time by `git log --grep=02.6-05` returning empty. The decision to
change the ranking axis was made **after** the plain-AE result was in hand and unfavourable.

The reasoning above is independent of which candidate lost, and would apply identically had the
plain AE won. The record cannot distinguish those two cases, so this is written down as what it
is: a criterion changed after seeing a result. A later reader should weigh it accordingly, and
should not read `02.6-SCREENING-RULE.md` as having constrained any published conclusion — it
constrained nothing, because no conclusion was published under it.

## 5. Correction — what the fixture pin does not establish

During execution it was asserted to the user that the `8.882e-16` fixture pin showed the tracer
reproduces a known answer, and therefore that the low scores could not be a broken tracer. That
attribution was wrong and is corrected here.

`test_swiss_roll_analytic_H_vector_norm_pins_sealed_module` compares `swiss_roll_analytic_H_vector`'s
norm against `curvature_probe.swiss_roll_analytic_H_scaled` — **two analytic formulas checked
against each other**. It exercises no pullback, no decoder, no autodiff.

The tests that do bear on the estimator are:

- `test_plain_decoder_curvature_sphere_known_answer` — analytic sphere decoder, `H_norm` within
  `1e-12` of `2/R`.
- `test_plain_decoder_curvature_flat_linear_decoder_is_exactly_zero` — exactly `0.0`.

These establish that the tracer is exact **on an analytic decoder**. They establish nothing
about its conditioning on a *trained* net, which is exactly the gap in §4. The conclusion
("this is not simply a broken tracer") survives, but on narrower evidence than was claimed.

## 7. What must NOT be inferred from this phase

- **No substrate was eliminated.** Neither plain AE nor TopoAE has been shown unfit as a
  manifold substrate. They scored poorly on a confounded composite.
- **No substrate was promoted.** Phase 02.5 stage 2 remains blocked; `02.5-10` still has no
  substrate decision to inherit.
- **Curvature-through-a-trained-decoder is not established as unworkable.** It is untested
  separately from substrate choice.
- Per `02.5-NOTE-substrate-selection.md` §4, still binding: Swiss roll topology does not
  transfer; reconstruction quality validates nothing about curvature; H0 preservation is not
  curvature fidelity.

The last of those cuts against the replacement axis too, and the replan must face it: ranking
substrates on topology preservation alone does not guarantee the winner has usable second
derivatives, which is the downstream need. Phase 02.1 has recorded precedent for this failure —
Ollivier-Ricci was selected on a distance criterion that later proved suspect.
