## Post-completion repair: the second instance of this class in this phase

**This is the second time in Phase 02.6 that a module cleared every one of its own
acceptance criteria and then failed at real scale.** The first was plan `02.6-11`'s
training-budget asymmetry (`decoder_substrate_ph_screen_run.py`'s `CFG_COMMON` trained
plainae/topoae under an unconverged budget for two rounds before a shared convergence rule
was reached). This is the second: plan `02.6-10`'s `derivative_bridge.py` passed 13 tests,
all of them at Swiss-roll dimensionality (`d=2`, 12 elements per point), and none at PU
dimensionality (`d=40`, up to 3201 elements per point before batching). `torch.quantile`'s
`2**24`-element cap is invisible below roughly 350,000 points at `d=2` but binding at 32
points at `d=40`. **Neither defect was a logic error in the tested code path; both were
tested-shape-does-not-match-deployed-shape gaps.** `02.6-15` should carry this as a finding
about the phase's testing practice -- test at the scale the module will actually run at, not
only at the toy fixture's scale -- rather than as two unrelated fixed bugs.

**What was fixed, concretely.** `_agreement_stats`'s two `torch.quantile` calls (`p90_abs`,
`p90_abs_relative`) are replaced by a new `_p90` helper: `torch.quantile` unchanged (bit-
identical) at or below `2**24` elements, `numpy.quantile`'s own default `method="linear"`
above it -- the SAME linear-interpolation formula `torch.quantile`'s default computes, pinned
equal below the cap by `test_p90_matches_torch_quantile_below_the_cap` rather than merely
asserted equivalent. `test_p90_matches_numpy_linear_interpolation_above_the_cap` confirms the
fallback branch is genuinely exercised (and that `torch.quantile` itself would refuse the
same input). `test_agreement_stats_handles_tensor_above_quantile_cap` reproduces the crash's
exact tensor shape (`32, 768, 40, 40`) synthetically, with no model fit needed, and is the
test that would have caught this before it ever reached a real run.

**Coordinator-approved scope note.** This is a documented reopening of plan `02.6-10`'s
sealed module -- explicitly lifted for this one function only, the same pattern as plan
`02.6-11`'s two protocol repairs. No other function in `derivative_bridge.py`, and no other
sealed module in this phase, was touched.

## The headline finding

**S1 (`02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md`) was ratified to compare BOTH the full `d x d` Hessian
tensor and the reduced `H_vec`/`H_norm`, reported separately, because "a reduction can agree
while the tensor it reduces disagrees." The measured reality is mostly the reverse of that
framing, and it is substrate-dependent** -- the ratified option group caught something real
about this instrument, just not quite the failure mode its own rationale anticipated.

**`max_abs`-level ratios (Hvec/H_norm relative to the full Hessian), computed explicitly:**

| arm | candidate | full Hessian max_abs | reduced H_vec max_abs | ratio (Hvec / full) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PU | plainae | 5.9955e-10 | 4.0923e-06 | **~6828x worse** |
| PU | topoae | 3.9670e-09 | 3.4569e-06 | **~872x worse** |
| roll (seed 0) | plainae | 2.3192e-06 | 1.9316e-04 | **~83.3x worse** |
| roll (seed 0) | topoae | 7.8054e-05 | 4.4736e-04 | **~5.73x worse** |
| roll (seed 0) | cae (chart 0) | 4.2965e-07 | 3.3135e-08 | **~13.0x BETTER (inverted)** |

**Do not read `max_abs` alone -- the median/p90 columns tell a materially different, and in
one case an opposite-direction, story.** Computed explicitly from the same rows (all figures
from the transcribed tables below):

| arm / candidate | statistic | full Hessian | reduced H_vec | ratio (Hvec / full) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PU plainae | median_abs | 6.3037e-12 | 2.5380e-07 | **~40,262x worse** (larger than the max-level ~6828x) |
| PU plainae | p90_abs | 1.9128e-11 | 7.8319e-07 | **~40,943x worse** |
| PU topoae | median_abs | 6.6873e-11 | 3.9135e-07 | **~5,853x worse** (larger than the max-level ~872x) |
| PU topoae | p90_abs | 2.2575e-10 | 9.8616e-07 | **~4,369x worse** |
| roll plainae (seed 0) | median_abs | 2.7462e-08 | 3.1424e-07 | **~11.4x worse** (SMALLER than the max-level ~83.3x) |
| roll plainae (seed 0) | p90_abs | 1.4017e-07 | 6.9779e-06 | **~49.8x worse** |
| **roll topoae (seed 0)** | **median_abs** | **1.8598e-06** | **7.1290e-07** | **~0.383x -- DIRECTION FLIPS: full Hessian's typical disagreement is LARGER than the reduced quantity's, opposite the max-level reading** |
| roll topoae (seed 0) | p90_abs | 9.7515e-06 | 7.7515e-06 | ~0.795x -- also below 1, same flipped direction |
| roll cae (chart 0, seed 0) | median_abs | 7.5912e-08 | 7.3745e-10 | ~0.0097x -- ~103x better (same direction as max, larger magnitude) |
| roll cae (chart 0, seed 0) | p90_abs | 1.7686e-07 | 1.1754e-08 | ~0.0665x -- ~15.0x better (same direction as max, close magnitude) |

**Three things this establishes, stated plainly:**

1. **For the PU regime (plainae, topoae), the reduced quantity is worse relative to the full
   tensor at every statistic level measured, and the gap WIDENS from max (~6800x/~870x) to
   median/p90 (~40,000x/~5,900x).** The full Hessian's own error distribution is heavily
   right-skewed (its median is 4-5 orders below its own max); the reduced quantity's is not.
   This is not a tail artifact -- it is the typical case.
2. **For the Swiss roll's `topoae` candidate specifically, the direction FLIPS between
   `max_abs` and `median_abs`/`p90_abs`.** At the max, the reduced `H_vec` looks ~5.7x worse
   than the full tensor; at the median and 90th percentile, the full tensor is itself worse
   (or comparable). A reader who looked only at `max_abs` would draw the wrong conclusion
   about which level is more trustworthy for this one candidate.
3. **For the CAE (per-chart), the reduced quantity is consistently and substantially better
   than the full tensor at every statistic level** (13-103x, direction never flips across the
   sampled charts/seeds inspected), the only candidate for which the two levels agree in
   direction across statistics.

**The consequence for `02.5-10`, stated as an operational implication and nothing more (D-18
binds -- no threshold is applied here):** the reduced `H_vec`/`H_norm` is the quantity any
downstream curvature claim actually consumes, and on the two free candidates it is 3-4
orders of magnitude worse-agreeing than the full tensor, at every statistic level, not just
the tail. **A threshold inferred from full-tensor agreement alone would be wrong by that
margin** if applied to the reduced quantity `02.5-10` would actually use. `02.5-10` should
read both levels, not one, before ratifying any bound.

## Honest limit (A-01) and non-promotion rule (D-19), stated once

**A-01, in `02.6-CONTEXT.md`'s own terms:** "Autodiff-versus-finite-difference agreement
(D-16) establishes that the derivative *computation* is stable. A net that learned a smooth
but *wrong* surface passes it cleanly. ... As composed, nothing in this phase demonstrates
that a winner's derivatives are *right* — only that they are *computable*." The full
tensor/reduced-quantity disagreement measured above is itself evidence of exactly this limit
in action: strong internal agreement (or disagreement) between two ways of computing the
SAME trained decoder's derivatives says nothing about whether that decoder learned the right
surface.

**D-19, stated once:** a substrate that tops the persistent-homology ranking but fails this
bridge is not promoted — 02.6 hands `02.5-10` both results and `02.5-10` decides what it can
use. The ranking is not walked to a runner-up under this bridge's result.

## PU seed asymmetry (stated, not glossed)

The PU arm's seed/fit availability is **whatever the cache holds**, and it is asymmetric
across candidates: `plainae40` = **1** cached fit, `topoae_d40` = **1** cached fit
(seed 20260806), `cae` = **3** cached fits (seeds 20260803, 20260804, 20260805) -- against the

undefined, not merely small. The CAE's 3 seeds give some per-seed variation to look at, but
still far short of the roll arm's 4-seed protocol. This is a real limit on what the PU side
of this bridge can support, not a gap in this runner's coverage of what exists.

## Calibrated step varies across three orders of magnitude (A-06, confirmed directly)

The calibrated finite-difference step is **not** universal -- it moved across three
different decades depending on regime and substrate, never trusted at
`derivative_bridge.DEFAULT_FD_STEP=1e-4` blind:

- Roll plainae, roll cae (all seeds/charts): `best_step=1.0e-04` -- matches the sealed
  default exactly.
- Roll topoae (all 4 seeds): `best_step=1.0e-05` -- one decade smaller than the default.
- PU plainae, PU topoae: `best_step=1.0e-03` -- one decade larger than the default.
- PU cae (most seed/chart combinations): `best_step=1.0e-02` -- two decades larger than the
  default (a handful of PU CAE chart/seed combinations calibrated to `1.0e-03` instead).

The PU regime's decoders never calibrated to the sphere-fixture-tuned default at all --
every PU candidate's optimal step sits one or two decades away from it. This directly
confirms assumption A-06: the finite-difference step is decoder-scale-dependent, and this
plan's discipline of calibrating per model rather than trusting the default is what made
that visible instead of silently degrading every PU-regime measurement.
