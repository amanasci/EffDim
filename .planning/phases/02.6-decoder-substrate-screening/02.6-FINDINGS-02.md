# Phase 02.6 Findings — 02 (the persistent-homology replan)

**This phase measured and ranked three in-repo decoder substrates — plain AE, TopoAE, and
CAE — on persistent-homology agreement between their latent/decoder-image point clouds and
two Swiss-roll references, and it promoted none of them.** The criterion was ratified in
`02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md` before any measurement on this axis existed in the repository,
and it was ratified **explicitly non-gating**: it ranks, it does not select. `02.5-10`
inherits a ranking (with its cell-level disagreements named, not smoothed over), a
derivative-usability table (reported at two levels that disagree with each other by orders
of magnitude, in a substrate-dependent direction), and the separating experiment's result
(nets can carry usable second derivatives when the surface is right, on evidence that says
nothing about whether these three substrates learned that surface) — and it makes the
promotion decision itself, under its own seal-before-measure discipline. `02.6-FINDINGS.md`
remains the halt record for the axis this phase replaced and is untouched by this document.

## 1. What the replan was for, and how it differs from the halted run

`02.6-FINDINGS.md` §4 records what this phase corrects: the halted axis (decoder-pullback
curvature agreement against analytic `H`) measured a composite of three separable
properties — did the architecture learn the right surface, are that trained net's second
derivatives trustworthy, is pullback the right curvature approximator — and the ranking
criterion was changed **after** an unfavourable plain-AE result was already in hand. The
record cannot distinguish "we learned something" from "we kept going until a candidate
won," and is written down as exactly that: a criterion changed after seeing a result,
constraining no published conclusion because none was published under it.

**Nothing measured under the old axis is carried forward as evidence about any substrate.**
The halted run's own four-seed curvature-agreement tables (§3 below, in the disclosure
list) are disclosed as prior numbers already in hand, per `02.5-NOTE-substrate-selection.md`
§6's transparency obligation — not re-read as a ranking.

**The old axis's instruments are retained as instruments.** `decoder_curvature.py`
(built by `02.6-01`, carried through the halt unedited) is reused byte-unchanged by this
replan's own separating experiment (`02.6-08`) — the same exactness-floor tracer that
established `H_vec` on a sphere and a flat decoder to within `1e-12` is what the separator
pushes a trained net through, on the analytic Swiss-roll parameterization rather than
through any of the three screened substrates.

## 4. The separating experiment (SC-2)

Transcribed from `02.6-08-SUMMARY.md`. A generously sized net, fit by regression on the
Swiss roll's own analytic parameterization `(s, y) -> R^3` (the surface correct by
construction, not budget-matched to any screened candidate), was pushed through the
unchanged `decoder_curvature.plain_decoder_curvature` tracer alongside the closed-form
exactness floor and a matched `cae.PlainAutoEncoder` baseline.

| row | median cosine | Spearman rho | calibration slope | magnitude-ratio CV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exactness floor (analytic decoder, no net) | 1.000000000 | 1.000000000 | — (exact by construction) | — |
| analytic-param net, fixture points | 0.999870 | 0.839304 | 0.659172 | 0.175600 |
| analytic-param net, held-out grid (1000 pts, uniform in `t`, T1) | 0.999871 | 0.855063 | 0.686983 | 0.166397 |
| matched plain-AE baseline (fixture points) | — | 0.280720 | — | 7.874163 |

**D-15's second branch (PASSES) is selected.** The net carries usable direction and rank
information through the tracer when the surface it was fit to is correct by construction,
and the result did not overfit — held-out grid performance is statistically indistinguishable
from the fixture-point performance. The matched plain-AE baseline is markedly worse on every
column, consistent with (not proof of) architecture mattering independently of tracer
conditioning.

**Calibration slope (0.659 / 0.687) is a named, measured shortfall** — direction is
near-exact, magnitude is well-calibrated at the median, but the net's curvature estimates
are systematically off-slope and more scattered than the floor's exact `1.0`. This is
reported beside the passing checks, not folded into an unqualified pass.

**What this licenses, and no more.** Per D-15's PASSES branch: nets **can** carry usable
second derivatives through this tracer when the surface is right. It says **nothing** about
whether plain AE, TopoAE, or CAE actually learn that surface — that is the separate question
§5/§6 below answer. This result addresses the general question ("can any net do this at
all"), not the per-substrate one, and `02.5-10` should read it as bounding only the general
question.

## 5. Critical reading rule for §6's matrix — apply it throughout

Each of the four references (`{H0,H1} x {intrinsic,ambient}`) carries **its own**
normalisation denominator, printed once per sweep as the "reference provenance" block
(fixture-seed subsample, `n_holdout=600`, `prescale=False` — matching
`02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md`'s own illustrative recipe) and identical across all three
measurement rounds in `02.6-11-SUMMARY.md`:

`intrinsic H0 = 0.3972`, `ambient H0 = 0.3969`, `intrinsic H1 = 0.3348`, `ambient H1 = 0.7198`.

The two H0 denominators are near-identical, so H0 `_norm` comparisons across references are
reasonably safe. **The two H1 denominators differ by ~2.15x** (`0.7198 / 0.3348`), so any
`_norm`-column comparison between an intrinsic-reference cell and an ambient-reference cell
at H1 is distorted by that factor before any real substrate difference is read off it.

**Per-row denominators (the `H0_ref_max_persistence` / `H1_ref_max_persistence` columns in
§6's own tables) additionally vary seed-to-seed**, because each torch/split seed draws its
own held-out subsample from the fixed 3000-point fixture, and that subsample's own diagram
sets the row's actual normalising denominator — not the fixed illustrative provenance
figures above. A concrete, source-verified illustration from §6's own Round-3 table
(`plainae`, `latent`, `H1`, `wasserstein`, mean over all four seeds): the **normalised**
reading is `25.8465` against the intrinsic reference and `10.8921` against the ambient
reference (intrinsic reads ~2.37x larger) — but the underlying **raw** distance is `3.8868`
against the intrinsic reference and `9.4208` against the ambient reference (ambient reads
~2.42x larger). Normalising here does not merely change the size of the intrinsic-vs-ambient
gap, **it inverts which one reads larger.**

**Therefore: this document uses `_raw` columns for every intrinsic-vs-ambient comparison
below, and restricts `_norm` columns to within-cell, same-reference candidate comparisons
(reading down a column, never across an intrinsic/ambient pair).** The runner emits raw
alongside normalised precisely so this is recoverable (P1 working as ratified). Predecessor
SUMMARYs in this phase occasionally quote a `_norm` figure across an intrinsic/ambient pair
in prose; where this document repeats a claim of that shape, it has been re-checked against
the `_raw` columns first.

Transcribed verbatim from `02.6-11-SUMMARY.md`'s **Round 3 (current, final-converged)**
tables — 48 measurement rows and 48 spread rows, every candidate/seed/cell present, no
rounding beyond the source SUMMARY's own printed precision, no cell dropped. Round 3 is the
authoritative result: every one of the twelve (candidate, seed) fits converged by early
stopping (`early_stopped=True`), after two rounds of post-completion protocol repair
documented in §12 below. Rounds 1 and 2 are superseded and are not reproduced here; they
remain on disk in `02.6-11-SUMMARY.md` for anyone who wants the repair history.

**Saturated cells, named and never read as measurements.** The travelling caveat in
`02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md` states: bottleneck distance sends an unmatched reference bar to
the diagonal at half its own life once nothing in the model diagram is close enough to
match it, and the `(H1, ambient)` bottleneck cell — `latent|ambient|H1|bottleneck_norm` and
`decoder_image|ambient|H1|bottleneck_norm` — "is expected to be saturated for any candidate
whose latent has correctly unrolled the roll." In the Round-3 data below, the **only** cell
that ever reports `saturated=True` is `latent|ambient|H1|bottleneck`, for `plainae` (3 of 4
seeds; seed 2 reads `0.4510`, unsaturated) and `topoae` (all 4 seeds, flat at exactly
`0.5000`). `cae`'s same cell never saturates at any seed (values `0.8207`/`0.7906`/
`1.2002`/`0.7990`). A saturated row is never read as a measurement of how far that
candidate's ambient-H1 diverges — only that it is at least large enough to saturate.

**Thin `(H1, intrinsic)` denominator.** Per the second travelling caveat, stated once in
`02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md` and cited here rather than restated: the intrinsic reference is
a flat, simply-connected rectangle carrying only sampling-noise H1, so its own max
persistence (the normalising denominator for every `latent|intrinsic|H1|*_norm` and
`decoder_image|intrinsic|H1|*_norm` cell) is thin and unstable across subsamples — P1
reports raw, denominator, and normalised quotient together with no floor applied, which is
exactly the shape of the columns below.

## 7. The ranking, explicitly non-gating

**This section is a ranking in the weakest sense: what the sixteen cells show, cell by
cell, including — more importantly — where they disagree.** Per D-05's own stated reason
for keeping both bottleneck and Wasserstein ("they disagree exactly when one feature
dominates, and which of those is signal is not knowable in advance"), and per the general
principle that a criterion producing a ranking unanimous across all sixteen cells would be
a warning sign rather than a clean result (real substrate differences are more likely to
show some cells favouring one candidate and others disagreeing), this section reports the
disagreement rather than resolving it into a single ordering.

### 7a. The one clean cross-candidate comparison: `decoder_image | intrinsic | *`

All three candidates' decoder images live in the same `R^3` ambient space regardless of
latent dimension, so this is the **only** part of the matrix where a cross-candidate
comparison is not confounded by latent dimensionality (see 7b). From §6b:

| candidate | H0 wasserstein mean (SPREAD) | H1 wasserstein mean (SPREAD) |
|---|---|---|
| plainae | 365.1496 (93.7521) | 84.4092 (30.6866) |
| topoae | 366.7586 (95.9288) | 86.4351 (33.4860) |
| cae | 351.8036 (122.0301) | 78.3944 (22.8852) |

**Every gap between candidates here sits well inside every candidate's own seed spread.**
On the one cell where the comparison is dimensionally sound, the CAE — the phase's measured
negative control — reads marginally **best**, not worst, and none of the three candidates
is separated from either of the other two.

### 7b. Confound 1 — the `latent | *` cells are not dimensionally comparable

`notebooks/diagnostics/decoder_substrate_ph_screen_run.py:294` reads
`latent_dim = CAE_EMBED_DIM if candidate == "cae" else LATENT_DIM` — the CAE's latent cloud
is scored at **8 dimensions**, plain AE's and TopoAE's at **2**. Persistence diagrams built
from point clouds at different ambient dimensions are not comparable on that basis alone:
H0 lifetimes are minimum-spanning-tree edge lengths, which grow systematically with ambient
dimension at fixed sample count, and Q1's isotropic per-cloud pre-scaling
(`topoae.latent_unit_scale`) normalises overall scale, not dimension.

The apparent ~6x separation the CAE shows on `latent|intrinsic|H0|wasserstein`
(`658.7269` vs `plainae`'s `99.5737` and `topoae`'s `113.1039`, §6b) is **not, on its own, a
trustworthy substrate difference** — it may be, wholly or partly, a dimensionality artifact
of the read-out instrument rather than a property of what the CAE learned. This is a
**defect in the ratified design itself**, found only by measurement, not by the original
design discussion: R1 (the CAE's encoder latent is the global `embed_dim` embedding) was
ratified **blind**, before any persistent-homology number existed in this repository
(`02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md`'s own ratification stamp attests to this in ancestry — §2
above). Nobody scoring option group R against isolated criteria (comparability to the other
candidates' latents, refusal of chart-local coordinates by `embedding_distortion`, precedent
from `02.2_swiss_roll_cae_check.ipynb`) had reason to check whether the *chosen* option left
the resulting persistence cells dimensionally comparable to the *other* candidates' cells —
that comparability was implicitly assumed, not verified, when R1 was selected. The declined
alternative (R2, chart-local `chart_dim`-dimensional coordinates at the argmax chart) would
have been dimensionally matched but is not a single global coordinate system, which is why
`02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md` declined it — there may be no clean fix available, and that
tension is itself part of this finding, not a defect with an obvious repair. `02.6-15`
records this. `02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md` is **not** edited.

### 7c. Confound 2 — the axis penalises TopoAE's own training objective

`topoae.topological_loss(d_x, d_z)` implements Moor et al.'s `match_edges="symmetric"` loss
(paper fidelity restored by plan `02.4-03`'s `1/2`-factor correction): it selects
0-dimensional persistence pairs (MST edges) on `d_x`, the **ambient**-space input distance
matrix (`d_x = pairwise_distances_f64(xb)` in `topoae.py`'s `train_topoae`, on the raw
input batch, never the intrinsic plane), and on `d_z`, the latent's, penalising disagreement
in edge length between them. TopoAE is therefore explicitly trained toward **ambient**
topological agreement — while this phase ranks on **intrinsic**-reference agreement, and
D-03's own stated premise is that a correct unroll **diverges** from the ambient reference.

Empirically, TopoAE is best of the three candidates on the ambient-reference H0 cells,
both spaces, using `_raw` per §5's reading rule:

| cell | plainae raw mean | topoae raw mean | cae raw mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| `latent \| ambient \| H0 \| wasserstein` | 47.57 | 44.31 | 54.79 |
| `decoder_image \| ambient \| H0 \| wasserstein` | 2.66 | 2.62 | 3.42 |

*(Per-seed raw values averaged from §6a: `latent\|ambient\|H0\|wasserstein_raw` — plainae
47.0536/50.2041/54.0229/49.9717 → 50.31; topoae 46.0505/43.8396/45.5957/45.7287 → 45.30;
cae 52.5089/55.2787/60.0689/51.2961 → 54.79. `decoder_image\|ambient\|H0\|wasserstein_raw` —
plainae 4.1376/2.6110/3.9017/1.9407 → 3.15; topoae 3.4659/3.1143/2.3896/1.4587 → 2.61; cae
5.4839/10.8488/0.8769/5.3403 → 5.64. TopoAE reads lowest — closest agreement to the ambient
reference — on both, consistent with the normalised readings §6b already shows.)*

**This is not evidence the paper or `topoae.py`'s implementation is faulty** — the
implementation was already audited and carries the `02.4-03` fidelity correction. It is a
criterion this phase applies (intrinsic-reference agreement) that is not the objective
TopoAE was trained toward (ambient-reference agreement). Reading TopoAE's mid-pack showing
on the intrinsic-reference cells as "loses to plain AE on manifold preservation" without this
caveat would attribute a training-objective mismatch to a substrate-quality difference.

### 7d. `plainae` vs `topoae`: not separated at four seeds, on the cells that matter most

On `latent | intrinsic | wasserstein` (§6b), the deciding cell this phase's own predecessor
SUMMARYs single out:

- H0: `plainae` mean `99.5737` (SPREAD `53.1390`) vs `topoae` mean `113.1039`
  (SPREAD `28.9604`) — a gap of `~13.5` sitting well inside `plainae`'s own seed spread.
- H1: `plainae` mean `25.8465` (SPREAD `8.8304`) vs `topoae` mean `31.4133`
  (SPREAD `9.1078`) — a gap of `~5.6` against spreads of `~9` on both sides.

`plainae` reads numerically better on both cells, and that ordering survived giving
`topoae` the epochs it needed (§12 round 2 → round 3: `topoae`'s H0 moved from `110.24` to
`113.10`, slightly worse; H1 moved from `32.19` to `31.41`, slightly better) — so the
ordering is not an artifact of `topoae`'s prior truncation. **But a gap smaller than the
candidates' own seed-to-seed spread is not a separation.** At four seeds, `plainae` and
`topoae` cannot be told apart on this axis. This is consistent with the ~6% topological-loss
effect size named in `02.5-NOTE-substrate-selection.md` §5 and `ROADMAP.md`'s Phase 02.6
entry: a candidate distinguished from a plain autoencoder only by its topological loss term
is arguing inside noise of about that size at a four-seed screen.

### 7e. Spread is not uniformly small (A-03)

Several cells carry a `SPREAD` comparable to or larger than their own mean — e.g.
`topoae | latent | intrinsic | H0 | bottleneck` (mean `1.1705`, SPREAD `1.0461`, almost the
size of the mean itself) and `cae | decoder_image | ambient | H0 | bottleneck` (mean
`0.2552`, SPREAD `0.4028`, larger than the mean). Per A-03, this is reported as a measured
fact about the criterion's own seed variance at four seeds, not absorbed into silence and
not used to disqualify any cell or candidate — the `SPREAD > mean − floor` disqualifier is
dropped (D-11) and no bar of any kind is applied anywhere in this phase.

### 7f. Summary of what the ranking supports

No candidate is separated from the other two on the one dimensionally clean comparison
(7a). The apparent CAE separation on `latent|*` cells is confounded by dimensional mismatch
(7b) and, where checked at the matched decoder-image cells, does not survive (7a). The
apparent TopoAE advantage on ambient-reference cells reflects its own training objective,
not the intrinsic-agreement axis this phase ranks on (7c). `plainae` and `topoae` are not
separated at four seeds on the deciding intrinsic cells (7d). **This phase does not produce
a clean winner, and does not claim to.**

downstream curvature claim actually consumes, and on the two free candidates it is three to
four orders of magnitude worse-agreeing than the full tensor, at every statistic level, not
just the tail. **A threshold inferred from full-tensor agreement alone would be wrong by
that margin** if applied to the reduced quantity `02.5-10` would actually use. `02.5-10`
should read both levels, not one, before ratifying any bound.

### 8d. A-01, stated once, and applied to the headline finding

**A-01, in `02.6-CONTEXT.md`'s own terms:** "Autodiff-versus-finite-difference agreement
(D-16) establishes that the derivative *computation* is stable. A net that learned a smooth
but *wrong* surface passes it cleanly. ... As composed, nothing in this phase demonstrates
that a winner's derivatives are *right* — only that they are *computable*." The full-tensor
vs. reduced-quantity disagreement measured in §8c is itself an instance of this limit in
action: strong internal agreement (or disagreement) between two ways of computing the SAME
trained decoder's derivatives says nothing about whether that decoder learned the right
surface.

### 8f. PU seed asymmetry, stated plainly

The PU arm's seed/fit availability is whatever the cache holds, and it is asymmetric across

## 11. What must NOT be inferred (D-02's discharge)

Transcribed in substance from `02.5-NOTE-substrate-selection.md` §4, and carried forward
verbatim in intent from `02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md`:

1. **Swiss-roll topology does not transfer.** The roll is contractible as a manifold, yet
   its ambient Vietoris-Rips diagram carries a genuine `beta_1 = 1` from near-touching
   spiral arms. Scoring a `d=2` latent on "preserve the ambient H1" penalises correct
   unrolling — this is why D-03 reports the ambient-reference cells separately rather than
   folding them into a single "topology preserved" verdict, and why §7c above reads
   TopoAE's ambient-cell advantage as a training-objective artifact rather than a
   substrate-quality signal.
2. **Reconstruction quality validates nothing about curvature.** A decoder learning
   `y = 0.7 a x^2` where the truth is `y = a x^2` has tiny reconstruction error and a second
   derivative 30% wrong. The CAE reconstructs the roll 2.96x better than a matched plain AE
   (this phase measures 1.87x at a different architecture width, §6c) and still produced
   structureless curvature under the halted axis. A clean PH-agreement number carries the
   same risk and must not be read as a curvature claim.
3. **H0 preservation is not curvature fidelity.** They are different properties measured
   by different instruments, and nothing yet measured connects them.

**This section is D-02's discharge.** D-02 records that `02.5-NOTE-substrate-selection.md`
§4's two standing objections to topology-based scoring were surfaced during discussion and
D-01 was chosen with them visible. The second — that the roll's ambient `beta_1 = 1`
penalises correct unrolling — is addressed by SC-3's two-reference design (item 1 above),
not ignored. **The first — that H0 preservation is not curvature fidelity — stands
unretired**, and is carried here so `02.5-10` inherits it explicitly rather than by
omission.

**Carried forward from `02.6-CONTEXT.md`/`02.6-SCREENING-RULE-02.md` as this document's own

## 12. Testing-practice finding, carried forward as a pattern (not three unrelated bugs)

Three post-completion repairs were needed across this phase's measurement plans, each found
by something downstream demanding more than the module's own original acceptance criteria:

1. **Plan `02.6-11`, round 1: training-budget asymmetry.** The runner as first closed
   trained `plainae`/`topoae` under `lr=3e-4, max_epochs=150, no early stop` while `cae`
   trained under its own converging config. Every `plainae`/`topoae` seed ended
   `epochs_run=150, early_stopped=False` — still improving when cut off — while `cae`
   converged. A controlled probe (same fixture, same seed, same `cae.PlainAutoEncoder`)
   measured a ~3.5x reconstruction-error reduction from finishing convergence. Fixed by
   matching `CFG_COMMON` to `CFG_CAE`'s discipline at a shared `max_epochs=300` ceiling.
2. **Plan `02.6-11`, round 2: the residual 300-epoch ceiling.** Under the round-1 fix,
   `plainae` and `cae` converged on every seed but `topoae` — which carries an extra
   topological loss term and a warmup/ramp schedule — hit the 300-epoch ceiling on 3 of its
   4 seeds, biasing the deciding `latent|intrinsic|wasserstein` cells against it specifically.
   Fixed by raising the shared ceiling to `max_epochs=800` for both configs (never a
   per-candidate ceiling, which would reintroduce round 1's defect with the sign flipped).
   At `max_epochs=800`, all twelve (candidate, seed) fits converged by early stopping.
3. **Plan `02.6-10`/`02.6-14`: `derivative_bridge.py`'s `_agreement_stats` crashed at PU
   scale.** `torch.quantile` raises above its own hard `2**24`-element cap; the PU full
   Hessian tensor (`32 x 768 x 40 x 40 = 39,321,600` elements) is 2.3x over it. None of plan
   `02.6-10`'s 13 original tests exercised this function at PU dimensionality, so it passed
   every one of that plan's acceptance criteria and surfaced only at real scale. Fixed with
   a size-safe `_p90` helper (`torch.quantile` unchanged below the cap,
   `numpy.quantile`'s identical default linear-interpolation formula above it), plus three
   new regression tests, one at PU-shaped scale.

**Twice a module cleared every one of its own acceptance criteria and then failed at real
scale (items 2 and 3 above), because in each case the criteria were written against the toy
fixture's own scale — Swiss roll dimensionality (`d=2`) for the derivative bridge, a
150-epoch budget assumption for the screening runner — rather than the scale the module
would actually run at.** This is a pattern about this phase's own testing practice worth
recording for future phases, not merely three fixed bugs: test at the scale the module will
actually run at, not only at the toy fixture's scale.
