## Summary

The new axis (D-01) is **persistent-homology agreement between a model's diagram and a
reference diagram**, scored on the Swiss roll (absolute bar, D-07) and on PU (comparative,
D-07/D-17). It is genuinely new infrastructure to this project: `ripser`/`persim` have been
used exactly once before, in a **notebook-only, untested, unpromoted** quick task
(`notebooks/quick_topoae_vs_cae_persistence.ipynb`, `.planning/quick/20260809-topoae-vs-cae-persistence/`).
That prior use is the single most load-bearing precedent for this phase — it proves the
`ripser.ripser(D, maxdim=k, distance_matrix=True)["dgms"]` / `persim.bottleneck` /
`persim.wasserstein` API works in this venv, at these sample sizes, in these wall-clock budgets
— but it also surfaced two hazards that transfer directly onto this phase's design and that
`02.6-CONTEXT.md` does not itself resolve: **bottleneck distance saturates** at exactly half the
reference diagram's longest unmatched bar once a model diagram has nothing close enough to match
it (measured: 8 of 16 Swiss-roll model rows landed on the identical saturated value in the quick
task), and **an H1 reference diagram built from a contractible point cloud carries only
noise-level persistence**, which this session measured directly (below) and which threatens
D-05's "normalise by the reference diagram's own max persistence" rule specifically for the
`(H1, reference = intrinsic plane)` cell of the 16-number matrix.

Nothing in this phase's PH-agreement axis needs a new package: `ripser 0.6.15` and
`persim 0.3.8` are already venv-installed (confirmed by `pip show` this session) and have already
computed real diagrams on this exact fixture family. They remain **undeclared in
`pyproject.toml`** — a known, accepted reproducibility gap, unchanged by this phase, and this
phase inherits it rather than resolving it. No new PH-agreement code exists in
`notebooks/pu_manifold/` yet; the quick task's `persistence()`/`bnk()`/`wsr()`/`lives()`/`betti()`
helpers are notebook-cell functions with no test coverage and no home under `pu_manifold/` —
promoting them into a tested sibling module (this codebase's own established pattern:
`curvature_probe.py` beside `curvature.py`, `chart_curvature.py` beside `cae.py`,
`decoder_curvature.py` beside `chart_curvature.py`) is this phase's largest single piece of new
work.

The good news the new axis buys over the old one: **persistent homology needs no C2 guard, no
autodiff, and no chart-index care.** `assert_c2_decoder`/`decoder_curvature.py` were built
specifically because differentiating a decoder twice requires a smooth activation and a
chart-free map; a persistence diagram is built from point *coordinates* only (a Rips filtration
over pairwise distances). This means the CAE — disqualified from the old axis's Tier 0 entirely
because its chart-fragmented decoder cannot be safely differentiated in the old sense that
mattered there — is a fully valid PH-agreement candidate with **no special-casing required**:
`model.forward(x)["y"]` (CAE, TopoAE-trained PlainAutoEncoder, and plain PlainAutoEncoder all
expose this identical dict-returning interface) gives the decoder-image point cloud for any of
the three candidates, uniformly, and `model.encode(x)` gives the latent. D-20 retains the CAE
specifically as the negative control on this axis, unconditionally screened rather than
tier-gated — this drops the old axis's chart-fragmentation obstruction as a screening blocker,
though it says nothing about whether the CAE will actually rank well on PH agreement.

Reusable, verified-present PU-side plain-AE fits already exist for the D-17 bridge and D-21's
amended-fence PU comparison: `cae_ctrl_plainae40_43cf438bc944c509.npz` (Phase 02.2's
matched-capacity plain-AE control at `d=40`, full state dict present, single seed `20260803`,
`max_epochs=40`) and `topoae_baseline_43cf438bc944c509_amend01_seed{20260806,20260807,20260808}_d20.npz`
(Phase 02.4's matched baselines, `d=20`, up to three seeds at the primary rung). **This answers
assumption A-04 concretely: a reusable plain-AE PU fit exists, but only at a single seed for the
`d=40` control** (the `d=20` rung has up to three seeds via the TopoAE-baseline family). Whether
that is enough for D-17's bridge, or whether D-21's fence-amendment needs a fresh multi-seed
plain-AE PU fit, is a planner decision — flagged in Open Questions below.

The separator experiment (D-12..D-15) and the derivative-usability bridge (D-16..D-19) both need
genuinely new code, but neither needs a new package or a new architecture: the separator is a
supervised-regression variant of the exact same `PlainAutoEncoder`-shaped `.decode(z)` interface
`decoder_curvature.py` already differentiates, and this session confirmed the arc-length
parametrization it needs (`s(t)`) has an exact closed form, numerically verified to `~1e-13`
against `scipy.integrate.quad` in this session. The bridge needs a finite-difference Hessian
stencil; this session calibrated its step size directly against the existing sphere-decoder
known-answer fixture and found the classic U-shaped float64 error curve, with the error minimum
at `h ≈ 1e-4` (max abs error `4.5e-8` against the exact autodiff Hessian) — degrading by four
orders of magnitude one decade either side. This is a concrete, reusable default for D-16's
"finite-difference stencil choice and step size," calibrated on this project's own decoder shape
rather than assumed from generic numerical-analysis folklore.

**Primary recommendation:** build one new sibling module (e.g. `notebooks/pu_manifold/persistence_probe.py`)
that promotes the quick task's diagram/distance helpers into tested, reusable functions
generalized over reference-vs-model-diagram pairs (not the quick task's TopoAE-vs-CAE-specific
framing); compute the Swiss-roll 16-number matrix per candidate per seed from exactly two `ripser`
calls per seed per candidate (one on the 2-D latent, one on the decoder-image reconstruction —
the 16 numbers are a reporting decomposition of 2 diagrams × 2 references × 2 distances × 2
degrees, not 16 separate `ripser` calls); build the D-12 separator as a small supervised-regression
variant reusing `decoder_curvature.plain_decoder_curvature` unchanged (a `.decode(z)`-shaped
object is all it needs); and build the D-16 bridge as a new finite-difference Hessian function
calibrated at `h ≈ 1e-4` by default, verified against the same known-answer fixtures
`test_decoder_curvature.py` already has.

### Locked Decisions

- **D-01:** The ranking quantity is **persistent homology agreement** — the distance between a
  model's persistence diagram and a reference diagram. Chosen over isometry distortion against
  exact geodesics and over k-NN neighbourhood overlap. Reversibility: one-way — a second axis
  change for the same question would be the third criterion tried and the record cannot absorb
  it; whatever this measures is what the phase reports.
- **D-02:** `02.5-NOTE-substrate-selection.md` §4's standing objections to topology-based scoring
  were surfaced during discussion and D-01 was chosen with them visible. D-03 addresses the
  ambient-H1 objection; the "H0 preservation is not curvature fidelity" objection stands
  unretired — assumption A-01/A-02 below.
- **D-03:** Each model's diagram is scored against **two references, reported separately, never
  collapsed**: (a) the diagram of the true 2-manifold in its exact intrinsic coordinates
  `(arc-length s(t), y)`, and (b) the diagram of the ambient 3-D Swiss roll point cloud.
- **D-04:** Degrees **H0 and H1, reported separately.** H2 excluded — no power analysis exists
  for `beta_2`.
- **D-05:** Distances: **both bottleneck and Wasserstein**, each **normalised by the reference
  diagram's own max persistence**, scale-free and comparable across candidates.
- **D-06:** Scored on **both the encoder latent and the decoder image, reported separately.**
- **D-07:** Bars: **absolute on the Swiss roll, comparative on PU.**
- **D-08:** The already-built distortion instruments (`cae.embedding_distortion`,
  `cae.fit_global_scale`, `geometry_probes.distortion_stats`) are **not computed in this phase.**
  Reversibility: costly to add later, and adding it after seeing the PH ranking reproduces the
  §4 axis-switching failure.
- **Read-out matrix consequence:** D-03 × D-04 × D-05 × D-06 = 2 references × 2 degrees × 2
  distances × 2 spaces = **16 numbers per seed per candidate**, 64 across four seeds, 192 across
  three candidates — Swiss roll side only. The runner must emit this table.
- **D-09:** The criterion, bars, and read-out matrix are **written down and committed before any
  measurement on the new axis**, ratified as **explicitly non-gating**: it ranks, it does not
  promote. Reversibility: one-way — a pre-registration declared exploratory cannot later be
  claimed to have constrained a published conclusion.
- **D-10:** The phase **promotes no substrate.** Reverses ROADMAP.md's "promote at most ONE."
  `02.5-10` inherits a ranking, a derivative-usability table, and the separating experiment's
  result — not a substrate decision.
- **D-11:** Four seeds minimum, **spread reported**, and the `SPREAD > mean − floor`
  disqualifying rule from the old `02.6-RESEARCH.md` (flagged there as unratified proposal A4) is
  **dropped**.
- **D-12:** Fit a **single best-case net** — generously sized, not budget-matched — by regression
  on the analytic Swiss roll parameterization `(s, y) -> R^3`, then trace curvature through it
  with the existing `decoder_curvature` tracer.
- **D-13:** PASS is defined as **measured degradation from the analytic decoder's own
  exactness** — not a threshold set in the abstract. `decoder_curvature`'s sphere/flat known-
  answer tests are the exactness reference.
- **D-14:** The separator runs **first, and is non-blocking.** Its result is recorded; the screen
  proceeds regardless.
- **D-15:** **Separator FAILS →** the PH ranking continues and is reported, but the phase records

  were about architectures failing to learn the sheet, not net derivatives in general.
- **D-16:** The bridge instrument is **autodiff versus finite-difference agreement** on the
  trained decoder's second derivatives. Needs no ground truth, transfers to PU unchanged.
- **D-17:** The bridge runs on **both the Swiss roll and PU, reported separately.**
- **D-18:** 02.6 **measures and applies no bar.** `02.5-10` ratifies the threshold.
- **D-19:** A substrate that tops the PH ranking but fails the bridge is **not promoted** — 02.6

- **D-20:** Screen the **three in-repo candidates only**: plain AE, TopoAE (sealed 02.4 fits),

- **D-21:** "Screening only — no PU fits" is **amended** to permit new PU fits, for the D-17

  sealed 02.2 CAE and 02.4 TopoAE PU fits remain read-only.
- **D-22:** The Stage A (Swiss roll) / Stage B (PU) split is **kept, but admission is
  descriptive**: failing Stage A no longer bars a substrate from Stage B.

### Pattern 1: Persistence diagrams, generalized over reference-vs-model (new)

**What:** the quick task's `persistence()`/`lives()`/`bnk()`/`wsr()` helpers, promoted from
notebook cells into a tested module function, generalized so the same code scores **any**
model diagram against **any** reference diagram — not specific to "TopoAE vs CAE."

**When to use:** for every candidate x seed x space x reference combination in the 16-number
matrix.

**Example (adapted from the quick task's cell 30, verified working in this project already):**
```python
# Source: notebooks/quick_topoae_vs_cae_persistence.ipynb, cell 30 (committed, executed,
# 0 errors) -- the diameter-normalization convention shown here is the QUICK TASK'S OWN
# choice, included for provenance; 02.6-CONTEXT.md's D-05 instead normalizes by the
# REFERENCE diagram's own max persistence, which is a different, NEW rule -- see Pattern 2.
import numpy as np
from ripser import ripser
import persim

def _finite_pairs(dgm):
    """Filter H0's one infinite bar; persim's distances cannot consume +inf."""
    d = np.asarray(dgm, dtype=float).reshape(-1, 2)
    if d.size == 0:
        return np.zeros((0, 2))
    return np.ascontiguousarray(d[np.isfinite(d[:, 1])])

def persistence_diagram(D: np.ndarray, maxdim: int) -> list[np.ndarray]:
    """D: (n, n) square symmetric distance matrix. Returns [dgm_H0, dgm_H1, ...],
    each a (m, 2) array of (birth, death) pairs, RAW (not normalized) units."""
    D = np.asarray(D, dtype=float)
    dgms = ripser(D, maxdim=maxdim, distance_matrix=True)["dgms"]
    return [_finite_pairs(g) for g in dgms]

def max_persistence(dgm: np.ndarray) -> float:
    """The reference diagram's own scale, per D-05. Empty diagram -> 0.0, the caller must
    guard division (see Common Pitfalls: this is near-zero for a contractible reference's H1)."""
    if dgm.size == 0:
        return 0.0
    return float((dgm[:, 1] - dgm[:, 0]).max())

def bottleneck(model_dgm, ref_dgm) -> float:
    return float(persim.bottleneck(model_dgm, ref_dgm))

def wasserstein(model_dgm, ref_dgm) -> float:
    return float(persim.wasserstein(model_dgm, ref_dgm))
```

**Critical pitfall (confirmed by the quick task, and structurally unchanged by D-05's different
normalization rule):** bottleneck is a *max* over a matching. Once the model diagram has nothing
close enough to match the reference's longest bar, that bar goes to the diagonal at **exactly
half its own life**, and the distance stops responding to anything else in either diagram. The
quick task measured 8 of 16 Swiss-roll model rows landing on the *identical* saturated bottleneck
value. Report bottleneck, but do not rank on it alone if it is at or near the reference's
half-life saturation point — mirror the quick task's own `is_saturated(...)` check and print a
`sat` flag next to any saturated bottleneck cell.

### Pattern 2: The intrinsic-plane reference `(s(t), y)` — new, closed-form, verified this session

**What:** reference (a) from D-03 — the true 2-manifold's own intrinsic coordinates. Requires
converting `make_swiss_roll`'s own parameter `t` (which is **not** arc length — it is the raw
Archimedean-spiral parameter) into true arc length `s(t)`.

**Verification performed this session:** `s(t) = 0.5 * (t*sqrt(1+t^2) + asinh(t))` matches
`scipy.integrate.quad(lambda u: sqrt(1+u**2), 0, t)` to `< 9e-14` at `t in {0.1, 1.0, 3.0, 7.0,
10.5}` [VERIFIED: measured directly this session]. Derivation: `X(t,y)=(t cos t, y, t sin t)`
gives speed `|dc/dt| = sqrt((cos t - t sin t)^2 + (sin t + t cos t)^2) = sqrt(1+t^2)` (the exact
same `speed2` quantity `decoder_curvature.swiss_roll_analytic_H_vector` already computes for a
different purpose), and `s(t) = integral_0^t sqrt(1+u^2) du` is the standard closed form for that
integrand.

**Important correction to a plausible-sounding but wrong assumption:** the mean curvature vector
at intrinsic coordinate `s` is **identical** to `swiss_roll_analytic_H_vector(t, global_std)` at
the corresponding `t` — mean curvature is an intrinsic property of the surface's shape, invariant
under reparametrization by arc length. **No new curvature ground truth needs deriving** for
anything that reuses `H_vec`; only the *coordinates* `(s, y)` used to build reference (a)'s point
cloud are new.

**Example:**
```python
# NEW this phase -- not present anywhere in the repo yet. Verified against
# scipy.integrate.quad this session (max abs diff < 9e-14 at five test points).
import numpy as np

def swiss_roll_arc_length(t: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """Exact closed-form arc length of make_swiss_roll's own spiral parameter t,
    from the ruled-cylinder speed sqrt(1+t^2) already used by
    decoder_curvature.swiss_roll_analytic_H_vector's d1/speed2 computation."""
    t = np.asarray(t, dtype=np.float64)
    return 0.5 * (t * np.sqrt(1.0 + t**2) + np.arcsinh(t))

def swiss_roll_intrinsic_plane(t, y_raw, global_std: float) -> np.ndarray:
    """(n, 2) intrinsic-coordinate point cloud (s, y), CLAUDE.md-scaled by the SAME
    global_std the ambient cloud uses (both are lengths, both scale by 1/global_std --
    the opposite direction from curvature, which scales BY global_std)."""
    s = swiss_roll_arc_length(t) / global_std
    y = (y_raw - y_raw.mean()) / global_std
    return np.stack([s, y], axis=1)
```

**Where this belongs:** `decoder_curvature.py` already hosts `swiss_roll_analytic_H_vector` for
exactly this reason — `curvature_probe.py` is sealed (02.5) and never edited, and this is
fixture-adjacent, reused-across-notebooks Swiss-roll-specific math, not model code. Recommend
co-locating `swiss_roll_arc_length`/`swiss_roll_intrinsic_plane` there, or in the new
`persistence_probe.py` module if the planner judges PH-specific fixture code belongs beside the
PH-specific instrument rather than beside the curvature-specific one. Flagged as Claude's
Discretion (module layout is already named as such in `02.6-CONTEXT.md`).

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Reporting only Wasserstein or only bottleneck.** D-05 requires both, precisely because they
  disagree when one feature dominates a diagram — exactly the situation bottleneck saturation
  produces (Pattern 1's pitfall).
- **Normalizing by the *model's* diagram scale instead of the *reference's*.** D-05 is explicit:
  normalize by the reference diagram's own max persistence. Normalizing by the model's own scale
  would make every candidate's distance artificially comparable regardless of how badly it missed
  the reference — the opposite of what an absolute Swiss-roll bar needs.
- **Assuming the intrinsic-plane reference (a) has a genuine H1 feature.** It should not (it is
  a flat, simply-connected rectangle in `(s,y)`) — but measured this session (below), it does
  carry small noise-level H1 bars from finite-sample Rips-complex boundary effects. Treating its
  `max_persistence(H1)` as either "zero, so skip this cell" or "a real feature to match" are both
  wrong; see Common Pitfalls.
- **Building the D-12 separator's training pairs from `t` directly instead of `s(t)`.** D-12
  says the net regresses `(s, y) -> R^3`, not `(t, y) -> R^3`. A net trained on `t` instead of
  `s` is a different (still valid, but not what D-12 specifies) parametrization and would not be
  measuring what D-13's PASS criterion is framed against.
- **Reusing `chart_curvature.assert_c2_activation` on the CAE for the PH axis.** Unlike the old
  axis, the PH-agreement computation needs no C2 guard at all — applying one anyway would be a
  needless, incorrect gate (a ReLU-activated decoder still produces valid, differentiable-free
  point coordinates for persistence purposes).

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Bottleneck saturation (confirmed precedent, structurally unaffected by D-05's normalization change)

**What goes wrong:** a candidate's bottleneck distance to a reference stops moving once the
model's diagram has nothing close enough to match the reference's longest bar — the distance
locks at exactly half that bar's own life and further degradation is invisible to it.
**Why it happens:** bottleneck is a max over a matching; once the worst-matched pair is "send the
longest bar to the diagonal," no other pair can make the max any larger by getting worse, and no
pair can make it smaller either (the matching already chose the best available assignment).
**How to avoid:** compute and print a `saturated` flag (bottleneck within a small tolerance of
half the reference's own max persistence — the quick task's own `is_saturated(...)` helper),
alongside every bottleneck cell. Rank on Wasserstein where the two disagree; D-05 already requires
reporting both for exactly this reason, but the *reason* should be stated in the runner's own
output, not left implicit.
**Warning signs:** multiple candidates or seeds reporting the identical bottleneck value at the
same reference/degree/space cell.

### Pitfall 2 [NEW FINDING, VERIFIED THIS SESSION]: the intrinsic-plane reference's own H1 diagram is near-degenerate, threatening D-05's normalization for exactly one matrix cell

**What was measured this session** (n=600 holdout subsample of the standard `FIXTURE_SEED=20260807`,
`N_POINTS=3000` fixture, `maxdim=1`, `ripser 0.6.15`):

| reference | diameter | top H1 life (raw) | top H1 life / diameter |
|---|---|---|---|
| intrinsic plane `(s,y)` | 11.31 | **0.420** | 0.037 |
| ambient 3-D cloud | 3.98 | **0.714** | 0.179 |

The intrinsic-plane reference (a flat, simply-connected rectangle by construction — no true loop
should exist) does not return a literal `dgms[1]` of zero features; finite-sample Rips complexes
on a rectangle always produce some short-to-medium-lived noise bars near their boundary. Its
longest H1 bar has a life about **1.7x** the intrinsic plane's own median-nn-scale noise floor but
is **less than a fifth** the size, relative to its own diagram's typical scale, of the ambient
reference's genuine `beta_1=1` loop (life fraction 0.037 vs 0.179 — a **4.8x** gap in normalized
terms) [VERIFIED: measured directly this session against the standard fixture, not carried
forward from the quick task, which never built the intrinsic-plane point cloud].

**What goes wrong:** D-05 normalizes bottleneck/Wasserstein by "the reference diagram's own max
persistence." For the `(H1, reference = intrinsic plane)` cell specifically, that denominator is
small (this session measured `0.420` raw, well below the ambient reference's `0.714`) — so a
*model* diagram's H1 distance to this reference, divided by this small denominator, will read as
a *larger* normalized number than the identical raw distance would against the ambient reference.
A candidate could look far worse on this one cell purely from the denominator being thin, not
from genuinely worse topology preservation.
**Why it happens:** the intrinsic reference is (correctly) topologically trivial in H1 — that is
what "the roll unrolled correctly" should look like — so there is no genuine long-lived feature
to normalize against; the only available scale is sampling noise.
**How to avoid:** flag this cell explicitly in the runner's own output (the phase's own precedent
— `02.6-SCREENING-RULE.md`'s "travelling caveat" convention for the raw-point baseline's
imperfection is the right model to follow here: state the caveat once, cite it everywhere the
cell is invoked, never restate it with different wording). Consider whether the planner wants a
floor on the denominator for this one cell, or whether the raw (unnormalized) H1/intrinsic
distance should be reported alongside the normalized one so a reader can judge for themselves.
This is genuinely unresolved — see Open Questions.
**Warning signs:** the `(H1, intrinsic)` cell producing normalized distances an order of magnitude
larger than the `(H1, ambient)` cell for the same candidate, with no corresponding raw-scale
difference.

### Pitfall 3: PH agreement instruments can rank confidently while measuring the wrong thing (measured precedent, same project, different statistic)

**What goes wrong:** the quick task found that TopoAE's own T1 gate statistic
(`topological_fidelity`, a *different* PH-adjacent instrument, not `ripser`/`persim`-based)
"can rank a model best while its H0 merge structure is near-destroyed," because it sums *absolute*
squared edge-length differences and a compressed local latent scale earns a spuriously low
penalty. It also found bottleneck distance saturating in a way that looks like a meaningful
number but carries no further information (Pitfall 1). **This is now the second time this project
has measured a PH-family instrument that ranks confidently while measuring something other than
what it is read for.**
**Why it happens:** persistence-diagram statistics compress a great deal of structure into one
number, and the failure modes (scale artifacts, saturation, denominator degeneracy) are each
individually subtle and do not announce themselves.
**How to avoid:** report all four required axes (D-03..D-06) separately per D-09's own
instruction, never collapse them, and treat any single cell that looks unusually clean or
unusually bad as a prompt to check for a known failure mode (saturation, thin denominator,
scale mismatch) before reading it as a substrate finding.
**Warning signs:** a ranking that is unanimous across every one of the 16 cells (real substrate
differences are more likely to show up as some cells favouring one candidate and others
disagreeing, per D-05's own stated reason for keeping bottleneck and Wasserstein both).

### Pitfall 4: Comparing diagrams built in different ambient scales without a shared preprocessing convention

**What goes wrong:** the decoder-image diagram (3-D, in whatever scale the trained decoder's
output happens to sit at) and the intrinsic-plane reference (2-D, in arc-length units) are built
from genuinely different coordinate systems. Persistent homology is scale-*covariant*, not
scale-*invariant* — a global rescale of one diagram changes every birth/death value uniformly,
which is exactly what D-05's normalization exists to neutralize, but only if **both** the model
diagram and the reference diagram were built under a consistent preprocessing convention in the
first place (CLAUDE.md's centre-and-divide-by-one-global-std rule).
**Why it happens:** it is easy to build the intrinsic-plane reference from raw `t`/`y_raw` values
and forget to apply the same `global_std` divisor the ambient cloud and every trained model
already went through.
**How to avoid:** `swiss_roll_intrinsic_plane` (Pattern 2 above) divides both `s(t)` and `y` by
the same `global_std` the ambient fixture uses — confirm any new code follows this, and add a
regression test pinning it (the same discipline `decoder_curvature.py`'s own tests already use
for `swiss_roll_analytic_H_vector`'s scaling).
**Warning signs:** a reference diagram whose diameter is wildly different in scale from the model
diagrams it is compared against, with no accompanying normalization to make the comparison
meaningful.

### Pitfall 5: Finite-difference Hessian step size, measured this session

**What was measured:** central-difference Hessian of the existing `_SphereDecoder` fixture
(`test_decoder_curvature.py`) against the exact `torch.func.hessian` result, at `z0 = (0.3, -0.2)`,
`R = 1.5`, float64:

| step `h` | max abs error vs exact autodiff Hessian |
|---|---|
| `1e-2` | `4.5e-4` |
| `1e-3` | `4.5e-6` |
| `1e-4` | **`4.5e-8`** (minimum measured) |
| `3e-5` | `5.8e-7` |
| `1e-5` | `4.2e-6` |
| `1e-6` | `4.4e-4` |
| `1e-7` | `3.9e-2` |
| `1e-8` | `4.8` (worse than the signal itself) |

[VERIFIED: measured directly this session, standard central-difference stencil
`H_ii=(f(z+he_i)-2f(z)+f(z-he_i))/h^2`, `H_ij=(f(z+he_i+he_j)-f(z+he_i-he_j)-f(z-he_i+he_j)+f(z-he_i-he_j))/(4h^2)`.]

**What goes wrong:** the classic float64 U-shaped error curve — too large `h` gives truncation
error, too small `h` gives catastrophic cancellation. Picking `h` outside roughly `[1e-5, 1e-3]`
degrades the finite-difference side of D-16's comparison by orders of magnitude, which would make
the bridge's own "agreement" statistic measure the *stencil's* accuracy rather than the model's.
**How to avoid:** default to `h ≈ 1e-4` (this session's measured minimum for this decoder shape
and scale), but **do not treat this as universal** — the optimum depends on the local curvature
scale of whatever decoder is actually being tested (a differently-scaled trained decoder could
shift the optimum by an order of magnitude). Recommend calibrating `h` empirically against
`decoder_curvature.py`'s own sphere/flat known-answer fixtures at build time (pick the `h` that
minimizes error there, reuse it), exactly as this session did, rather than hard-coding `1e-4`
blind.
**Warning signs:** the bridge's agreement statistic being uniformly poor across every candidate,
in a way inconsistent with the autodiff side's own known exactness — a symptom of a bad `h`, not
necessarily a real derivative-usability finding.

### Pitfall 6: Cost of finite-difference Hessians at PU's latent dimension

**What goes wrong:** the standard central-difference Hessian stencil costs `O(latent_dim^2)`
decoder evaluations per point (`2*d` for the diagonal, `2*d*(d-1)` for cross terms). At
`latent_dim=2` (Swiss roll) this is trivial (~8 evaluations/point). At `latent_dim=40` (the
CAE's `embed_dim`, and the reusable plain-AE PU control) this is **~3,200 decoder evaluations per
point** — cheap per-evaluation (an MLP forward pass) but potentially slow if not batched.
**How to avoid:** vectorize with `torch.vmap` over points at minimum, and consider whether the
PU-side bridge needs the full `d x d` Hessian or only the diagonal/trace (D-16 does not specify;
the autodiff side already computes the full projected second fundamental form via a `d x d`
solve, so a like-for-like comparison plausibly needs the full FD Hessian too — flagged as an Open
Question), and whether it runs on the full PU holdout (383-2000 rows, per the existing evaluation-
set precedent) or a further subsample.
**Warning signs:** a PU-side bridge run taking materially longer than the Swiss-roll-side run
described in this phase's own wall-clock budget (CLAUDE.md's "under two minutes" applies only to
the mandated sanity notebooks, not to a `notebooks/diagnostics/` runner, but an unbounded runtime
is still worth flagging before committing to a full-`d x d` PU-side FD Hessian).

### Arc-length closed form, verified this session

```python
# Verified this session: max abs diff < 9e-14 against scipy.integrate.quad at
# t in {0.1, 1.0, 3.0, 7.0, 10.5}. See Pattern 2 above for full derivation.
import numpy as np

def swiss_roll_arc_length(t: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    t = np.asarray(t, dtype=np.float64)
    return 0.5 * (t * np.sqrt(1.0 + t**2) + np.arcsinh(t))
```

### Persistence diagram + reference-normalized distance, adapted from the proven quick-task pattern

```python
# Adapted from notebooks/quick_topoae_vs_cae_persistence.ipynb cell 30 (committed,
# executed, 0 errors), with D-05's reference-max-persistence normalization substituted
# for the quick task's own diameter normalization.
import numpy as np
from ripser import ripser
import persim

def _finite_pairs(dgm):
    d = np.asarray(dgm, dtype=float).reshape(-1, 2)
    return np.ascontiguousarray(d[np.isfinite(d[:, 1])]) if d.size else np.zeros((0, 2))

def persistence_diagram(D: np.ndarray, maxdim: int = 1) -> list:
    return [_finite_pairs(g) for g in ripser(np.asarray(D, dtype=float),
                                              maxdim=maxdim, distance_matrix=True)["dgms"]]

def max_persistence(dgm: np.ndarray) -> float:
    return float((dgm[:, 1] - dgm[:, 0]).max()) if dgm.size else 0.0

def ph_agreement(model_dgm: np.ndarray, ref_dgm: np.ndarray) -> dict:
    """D-05: bottleneck AND Wasserstein, both normalized by the REFERENCE's own max
    persistence. Caller must guard ref_max near zero (Pitfall 2) -- this function does
    not silently floor it."""
    ref_max = max_persistence(ref_dgm)
    b = float(persim.bottleneck(model_dgm, ref_dgm))
    w = float(persim.wasserstein(model_dgm, ref_dgm))
    return {"bottleneck": b, "wasserstein": w, "ref_max_persistence": ref_max,
            "bottleneck_normalized": b / ref_max if ref_max > 0 else float("nan"),
            "wasserstein_normalized": w / ref_max if ref_max > 0 else float("nan")}
```

### Finite-difference Hessian stencil, calibrated this session

```python
# Verified this session against decoder_curvature.py's own _SphereDecoder fixture:
# max abs error 4.5e-8 vs exact torch.func.hessian at h=1e-4 (the measured minimum;
# recalibrate per-decoder rather than trusting this value blind -- Pitfall 5).
import torch

def finite_difference_hessian(decode_one, z: torch.Tensor, h: float = 1e-4) -> torch.Tensor:
    """z: (latent_dim,) float64. Returns (out_dim, latent_dim, latent_dim)."""
    d = z.shape[0]
    f0 = decode_one(z)
    out_dim = f0.shape[0]
    H = torch.zeros(out_dim, d, d, dtype=z.dtype)
    for i in range(d):
        ei = torch.zeros(d, dtype=z.dtype); ei[i] = h
        H[:, i, i] = (decode_one(z + ei) - 2 * f0 + decode_one(z - ei)) / h**2
    for i in range(d):
        for j in range(i + 1, d):
            ei = torch.zeros(d, dtype=z.dtype); ei[i] = h
            ej = torch.zeros(d, dtype=z.dtype); ej[j] = h
            val = (decode_one(z + ei + ej) - decode_one(z + ei - ej)
                   - decode_one(z - ei + ej) + decode_one(z - ei - ej)) / (4 * h**2)
            H[:, i, j] = val
            H[:, j, i] = val
    return H
```

     identical question.

3. **Does the D-16 bridge need the full `latent_dim^2` finite-difference Hessian, or would a
   cheaper partial stencil (diagonal only, or the same `g`-trace quantity the autodiff side
   ultimately reduces to) serve the "agreement" comparison equally well?**

     solve to the trace-convention `H = tr_g(II)`; the *comparison target* could plausibly be
     just that final scalar/vector per point rather than the full intermediate Hessian tensor.

     reduced quantity.

     — cheaper, and it is literally what the separator (D-12/D-13) and the PH-ranking's own
     curvature-adjacent claims care about — and treat a full intermediate-tensor comparison as an
     enhancement if the reduced comparison proves inconclusive.

4. **Does the separator's regression net (D-12) get evaluated only at its own training points, or
   at a held-out set?**
   - What we know: D-12 says "fit a single best-case net... by regression," with no held-out
     split specified.
   - What's unclear: whether "best-case, not budget-matched" implies deliberately overfitting
     (since the goal is to test whether *any* net's second derivatives can be trustworthy, not
     whether this particular net generalizes) or whether a held-out check is still warranted.
   - Recommendation: since the analytic parametrization has a closed-form, noise-free ground
     truth everywhere (not just at sampled points), a dense held-out grid costs nothing extra and
     removes any ambiguity about whether the separator's PASS/FAIL reflects overfitting versus
     genuine second-derivative usability — recommend evaluating on both, reporting separately.
