### The FAIL-path test, answering CONTEXT.md's D-16 planner note

CONTEXT.md rejected "a test that exercises the FAIL path on a synthetic verdict" and then flagged that
rejection for a second look during planning, on the grounds that SPEC-07's halt is a branch which will
very likely never execute on real data, and that D-14's decision to keep the check in notebook prose
removed the other safety net.

**Answer: the test is included, inside the notebook, as three synthetic records exercised against the
enforcement function — not as a pytest addition to `notebooks/pu_manifold/tests/`.**

That placement is what reconciles the note with the decision it questions. D-14's reason for declining
a module was that a reader of the notebook alone should be able to see the guarantee; a pytest file
the notebook never mentions would restore exactly the invisibility D-14 objected to, and would also
require the module D-14 declined. Three synthetic dicts, a `pytest.raises`-free assertion block, and a
printed table of the three outcomes keep the demonstration where a reader will encounter it, cost
about fifteen lines, and mean the halt branch has provably executed at least once — every time the
notebook is run.

Scope is bounded deliberately: the synthetic records exercise `_require_gate`'s branching, not the
statistics. `_gate_classify`'s own boundaries are already asserted in `§6.3` on synthetic `(r, m)`
pairs, so the two tests together cover the rule and its enforcement without either duplicating the
other.
