<objective>
Turn the spectrum into the residual-variance curve SPEC-04 asks for, locate its elbow by a stated
deterministic criterion, and freeze the embedding dimension `d` that every remaining phase is defined
on.

Purpose: This is where the phase becomes irreversible. `d` is the decoder's input width in Phase 3,
the coordinate space the curvature field lives on, the support region CURV-08 bounds, and the basis
for Phase 4's region partition — changing it after Phase 3 invalidates the decoder, the curvature
field, and every MKNN number. It is also the one place in the phase where a plausible-looking
alternative rule is always available: several defensible elbow criteria exist, they disagree by a few
dimensions on a smoothly decaying spectrum, and one of the possible answers costs a fresh Isomap fit
while the others do not. Pre-registration in `§6.0` and a human gate before the freeze are the two
defences against choosing by convenience.

D-06 forces both curves rather than the cheap one alone: the Tenenbaum residual is the canonical
Isomap definition and the elbow is read from it, while the eigenvalue-based residual is free once the
spectrum exists and is carried as a cross-check. Where the two diverge is itself a non-Euclideanity
signal that reinforces the `r`/`m` gate, so the divergence is reported rather than merely drawn.

Output: sections 6.5 and 6.6 of `notebooks/01_manifold_and_gate.ipynb`, the cached
`mds_residuals_{fit_key}.npz`, and the frozen `D_FROZEN` with its coordinate slice `ISOMAP_COORDS_D`.

Task budget: 3 tasks. The curves and the elbow finder (~30% context), a zero-implementation decision
gate, and the freeze itself (~15%). Roughly 45% total.

### Open item 2 — the point-pair subsampling scheme and its stability argument

**Answer: `R2_PAIR_COUNT = 200_000` pairs drawn by the existing `_draw_geo_pairs` at
`R2_PAIR_SEED = SEED + 2`, with a second, independently seeded draw of the same size at
`SEED + 3` used to demonstrate the elbow is stable rather than to argue it.**

Both draws are already made in `§6.0` and both geodesic samples are already taken in `§6.1`, because
the geodesic matrix is unavailable once released. The count follows Phase 1's precedent — `§4.0` used
100,000 pairs for a Spearman correlation and stated that was far more than enough to distinguish 0.95
from 0.99 — and doubles it, because this curve is evaluated at up to 40 values of `d` and the quantity
that must be stable is not the R-squared at one `d` but the **`argmax` of a chord distance across the
whole curve**, which is a sharper demand than any single correlation.

The stability argument is a demonstration, not a sample-size assertion. The full curve is recomputed
on the disjoint second draw and the two elbows are compared. Agreement is the evidence; disagreement
halts, because an elbow that moves with the pair sample cannot support a one-way freeze, and quietly
taking the first draw's answer would be exactly the kind of unexamined choice this phase exists to
prevent. The remediation in the halt message is to raise `R2_PAIR_COUNT` and re-run from `§6.0` as a
new pre-registration — not to pick one.

Memory is bounded and worth stating because it is the one place this plan could surprise: the
vectorised evaluation builds a `R2_PAIR_COUNT x K_EFF` float64 array of cumulative squared coordinate
differences, which at 200,000 by 40 is about 64 MiB, computed once per draw and released.

### Open item 5 — plotting the elbow

**Answer: one figure, both curves on a single axis, with four annotations.**

CONTEXT.md's `<specifics>` asks for exactly this: the Tenenbaum curve, the eigenvalue curve, their
divergence, and the chosen `d` legible in a single figure. The four annotations are the kneedle point
on the Tenenbaum curve, the same `d` marked on the eigenvalue curve so the vertical offset between
them is visible, a shaded band or annotation at the point of maximum divergence, and a vertical line
at `n_components = 18` labelled as the D-08 branch boundary — so a reader sees immediately which side
of that line the elbow fell on and therefore which branch `§6.6` took.
