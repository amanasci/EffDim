\begin{abstract}
The Platonic Universe tested the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) by measuring the convergence of foundation-model representations of galaxy images with mutual $k$-nearest-neighbour agreement (MKNN). We ask whether that convergence is concentrated where the embedding manifold is flat. A pointwise curvature field, obtained by differentiating a trained decoder and validated against analytic truth in that regime, finds mean-curvature magnitude correlating negatively with cross-survey neighbour agreement at every latent dimension, and that local density removes most of it at $d=20$, half at $d=25$ and little of the already small $d=32$ value. A second-order expansion of a linear map restricted to the manifold explains why: a readout sees the second fundamental form contracted with its normal component, $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, and, at second order, an alignment's error splits into a connection mismatch and the relative second fundamental form projected into the target normal space; mean-curvature magnitude sees neither at large codimension. On a unit-normalized embedding that Hessian splits into a sphere term, proportional to the readout's own value, and a shape term. On a synthetic surface the sphere term has partial correlation $-0.74$ to $-0.80$ with local $R^2$ across samplings that swing the $\norm{H}$ partial from $-0.29$ to $+0.26$; on 86{,}471 labelled galaxies it is negative on every label, the mismatch between the label's own Hessian and the shape term is negative wherever the label has local structure, and the shape term alone takes the label-dependent sign that mismatch predicts. Mean curvature of a single representation is therefore not a viable explanation for weak Platonic convergence; the obstruction is relative and, for the fitted global linear alignment, already present at first order.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis~\citep{huh2024platonic} holds that models trained on different data converge toward a shared representation; Duraphe et al.~\citep{duraphe2025platonic} tested it on the sky: galaxies imaged by two surveys (Legacy Survey and Hyper Suprime-Cam, HSC) and embedded by a family of different vision models share only 0.3 to 2.3 percent of their nearest neighbours across the two surveys. We ask where on the manifold that agreement lives: if two models disagree most where the embedding surface bends sharply, local mean curvature should associate negatively with local cross-survey agreement. We test this claim using a pointwise mean-curvature estimation from a trained autoencoder on the embedding space.

We contribute (i) a pointwise mean-curvature and second-fundamental-form instrument from a trained decoder, validated against analytic truth in the production regime; (ii) a density-controlled test of the curvature--alignment prediction under two probes, which finds no effect free of density; (iii) the residual expansion of a linear map on a submanifold, which names the curvature such a map responds to and shows mean-curvature magnitude is blind to it; and (iv) known-surface and real-data tests on the readout side, where the expansion's terms behave as predicted.

\section{Pointwise curvature from a decoder}
\label{sec:instr}

\paragraph{Data.} DINOv3~\citep{simeoni2025dinov3} ViT-B/16 embeddings of 101{,}725 galaxies imaged by both HSC~\citep{aihara2018hsc} and the Legacy Survey~\citep{dey2019legacy}, (Platonic Universe release~\citep{duraphe2025platonic}); we draw 10{,}000 rows once with a recorded seed. A second set of 86{,}471 single-survey ViT-B embeddings with catalogue labels ($r$-band magnitude, photometric redshift, smooth fraction, stellar mass; the AstroPT galaxy set~\citep{smith2024astropt}) serves the readout experiment. Embeddings are unit-normalized (data on $S^{767}$). Persistent homology detects no one-dimensional cycles ($\beta_1 = 0$ under bootstrap bands~\citep{fasy2014confidence}); the auto-encoder is a computational chart, and curvature is evaluated only where $J$ has full rank.

\paragraph{Instrument.} Let $F:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^{768}$ be the decoder of a plain auto-encoder (three hidden layers of width 250, SiLU), fit on 80\% of rows; curvature is evaluated on held-out rows. With $J = DF(z)$, metric $g = J^{\top}J$, normal projector $P_N = I - Jg^{-1}J^{\top}$ and second fundamental form $\mathrm{II} = P_N D^2F(z)$, the mean-curvature vector is $H = \operatorname{tr}_g \mathrm{II}$, by automatic differentiation. On the unit sphere every $d$-submanifold carries a radial component of $H$ equal to $-d$ that says nothing about shape. To account for this, the map differentiated is $F/\norm{F}$ (after which $\Hrad = -d$ to $10^{-14}$) and $\norm{\Htan}$ carries the shape. Decoder curvature has served as a regularizer~\citep{lee2023curvature} and been read from templates~\citep{acosta2022templates}; here it is an instrument, validated against analytic fixtures.

\paragraph{Validation.} Scored against closed-form curvature on rank (Spearman), direction (cosine) and magnitude ratio. At $D=768$ an in-sphere surface $G(z) = Q\,\mathrm{normalize}([\phi(z);\,0.8\,h(z);\,0\ldots])$ ($\phi$ inverse stereographic $\mathbb{R}^{16}\to S^{16}$, $h$ a scalar sum of four Gaussian bumps, $Q$ a rotation; 18-dimensional span) at $n=86{,}471$, $k=2{,}048$ gives rank 0.94 against the true $\norm{\Htan}$ (equivalently $\norm{H}$, the radial part being fixed), direction cosine 0.999 and magnitude ratio 1.00 for the full vector, and rank 0.75 under noise at 25\% of patch radius.

\section{Curvature and cross-survey convergence}
\label{sec:align}

\paragraph{Probe.} Per-point mutual $k$-nearest-neighbour score $\mathrm{MKNN}_i = |N_k(z_1)_i \cap N_k(z_2)_i|/k$ at $k=20$ between the HSC and Legacy Survey embeddings, the Platonic Universe statistic made local, correlated with $\norm{H}$ over all 10{,}000 points at $d\in\{20,25,32\}$. Centered kernel alignment~\citep{kornblith2019similarity} is the second probe.

\paragraph{Result.} The raw association $\rho(\norm{H},\mathrm{MKNN})$ is $-0.112$, $-0.128$ and $-0.024$ at $d=20,25,32$ ($p<10^{-5}$, $<10^{-5}$, $0.009$), in the predicted direction. But $k$-NN statistics are density-sensitive~\citep{radovanovic2010hubs}: density correlates with agreement at $-0.21$ at every $d$ and with $\norm{H}$ at $+0.43$, $+0.32$ and $+0.01$, so residualizing on density leaves $-0.024$, $-0.066$ and $-0.022$: 78\%, 49\% and 8\% of the association removed. Under a permutation null within density strata only $d=25$ survives ($p<5{\times}10^{-5}$; replicating across three decoder seeds; the CKA tertile gap within strata agrees at $d=20$ and 25, $-0.05$ to $-0.08$ against a null band of $\pm0.017$). Replacing $\norm{H}$ by its sphere-tangential part $\norm{\Htan}$ (the field here is the unprojected decoder's, radial part not fixed; the two rank at $\rho = 0.89$--$0.96$) collapses the $d=25$ partial to $-0.023$, into the range of the two $d$ that fail. Convergence is not detectably concentrated by mean-curvature magnitude once density is controlled.

\section{What a linear map sees on a curved manifold}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $M\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ be a $d$-dimensional submanifold, $d<D$; at $x_0$ take an orthonormal tangent frame $J$, the second fundamental form $\mathrm{II}$ (normal-valued, symmetric $d\times d$), $H = \operatorname{tr}\mathrm{II}$, and for a vector $w$ its tangent and normal parts $w_T + w_N$. In normal coordinates $u$ at $x_0$, $M$ is $x_0 + Ju + \tfrac12\mathrm{II}(u,u) + O(|u|^3)$, so
\begin{equation}
[\nabla_M (w\!\cdot\! x)]_J = J^{\top}w,\qquad \operatorname{Hess}_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w_N, \mathrm{II}\rangle,\qquad \Delta_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w, H\rangle .
\label{eq:hess}
\end{equation}
To second order, a linear function is as non-linear on $M$ as $M$ bends in that function's normal direction~\citep{lee2018riemannian}. For $M\subset S^{D-1}$ the normal bundle splits into the sphere's radial direction and the rest, $\mathrm{II} = \mathrm{II}^{S} - g\,\hat x$, so
$\operatorname{Hess}_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w_N, \mathrm{II}^{S}\rangle - (w\!\cdot\!\hat x)\,g$ \refstepcounter{equation}(\theequation)\label{eq:split}: a linear readout of a unit-normalized embedding is bent by the manifold's shape and, whatever the manifold, by the sphere in proportion to the readout's own value $\hat y - b_0$. For a label $y$ on $M$, a global affine probe $\hat y = w\cdot x + b_0$ and a patch $u\sim N(0, s^2 I_d)$ in normal coordinates (the $\tfrac12$ below uses Gaussian fourth moments), with $\bar c$ the patch-mean residual, $\delta = [\nabla_M y]_J - J^{\top}w$, $\Delta = \operatorname{Hess}_M y - \langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{patch}}\big[(y-\hat y)^2\big] = \bar c^{\,2} + s^2\norm{\delta}^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}s^4\norm{\Delta}_F^2 + O(s^4\norm{\delta}) + O(s^6).
\label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
For two embeddings $F, G$ of the same objects in a common chart $u$ and a linear map $A$ with $AJ_F = J_G$, the same expansion gives $G - (G_0 + A(F - F_0)) = \tfrac12\big[J_G(\Gamma^G - \Gamma^F) + \mathrm{II}_G - A\,\mathrm{II}_F\big](u,u) + O(|u|^3)$, with $\Gamma$ the induced connections: the second-order obstruction to linear alignment is a connection mismatch plus the \emph{relative} second fundamental form, and reduces to the latter when the induced connections agree at $x_0$ (in particular under a local intrinsic isometry); the extrinsic term's normal part is $\mathrm{II}_G - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F$.

So the curvature a linear readout pays for is $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, and an alignment's second-order error has an extrinsic part whose normal component is $\mathrm{II}_G - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F$; $\norm{H}$ bounds only a trace and need not correlate with either at codimension 750; the term carries $s^4$, so patch radius, hence density, multiplies it; and when the label has intrinsic curvature of its own, $\Delta$ carries the residual and the curvature piece can take either sign.

\section{The probe-facing curvature, on a known surface and on galaxies}
\label{sec:real}

The readout side can be tested directly: $w$ is known and $\mathrm{II}$ comes from the decoder. Statistic: five-fold out-of-fold ridge probe~\citep{alain2017probes} at $\alpha=100$ from embedding to label; local $R^2$ inside each of 512 anchors' 2{,}048-neighbourhoods; rank-partial Spearman against local $R^2$ with Freedman--Lane $p$-values~\citep{freedman1983permutation}, controlling for log patch radius, local label variance and count and, as a stronger control, log radius at $k\in\{16,64,256,1024,2048\}$. Per anchor we compute $\norm{\Htan}$ and the probe-facing Hessian $\norm{\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle}_g$ with $w_N = w - Jg^{-1}J^{\top}w$, split by Eq.~\eqref{eq:split} into its shape and sphere terms.

\paragraph{Known surface.} The validation surface is resampled with weight $\norm{\Htan}^\gamma$, so only where the points sit changes (Figure~\ref{fig:main}a). The $\norm{\Htan}$ partial follows the sampling from $-0.29$ at coupling $+0.80$ through zero to a plateau near $+0.22$, and the decoder reproduces it. The probe-facing Hessian $\norm{\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle}$ has partial correlation $-0.74$ to $-0.80$ at every sampling. On this surface it is the sphere term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split}: rank $0.996$--$1.000$ with $\sqrt d\,|\hat y - b_0|$, the in-sphere part 20 to 40 times smaller. The fixture thus confirms the expansion's sphere term: an intrinsically linear label loses local $R^2$ where the readout's own value is large, whatever the sampling. With a nonlinear label the sphere term is indistinguishable from zero and $\norm{\Delta}$ carries the partial ($-0.2$ to $-0.6$), as Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid} predicts.

\paragraph{Galaxies.} Here the two terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split} trade places: the shape term dominates the probe-facing Hessian (rank $0.96$--$0.99$ with the full norm). To read it through Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid} we estimate $\operatorname{Hess}_M y$ per anchor by a least-squares quadratic of the label on the tangent-projected coordinates $u = g^{-1}J^{\top}(x - x_0)$ of its 2{,}048 neighbours, where, at the anchor, the Christoffel symbols vanish to the needed order and the quadratic coefficient is the covariant Hessian. Three regularities (Table~\ref{tab:real}). The sphere term is negative on every label and both $d$ ($-0.13$ to $-0.36$), as on the fixture. Wherever the label has local structure (magnitude, redshift, smooth fraction; stellar mass is indistinguishable from zero throughout) the mismatch is negative ($-0.09$ to $-0.45$) and the alignment $\cos_g(\operatorname{Hess}_M y,\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle)$ positive ($+0.07$ to $+0.43$): local $R^2$ is higher where the manifold bends in the readout's direction the way the label bends. The shape term alone is label-signed (positive for magnitude and smooth fraction, null to weakly negative for redshift), consistent with Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid}: it is one of three terms in $\norm{\Delta}^2 = \norm{\operatorname{Hess}_M y}^2 - 2\langle\operatorname{Hess}_M y,\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle\rangle + \norm{\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle}^2$, so raising it can shrink or enlarge the mismatch depending on its alignment with the label Hessian; empirically its sign follows the cross term's where that is nonzero and the remaining square where it is null. $\norm{\Htan}$ changes sign between $d=16$ and $20$ for magnitude: it sits on the curve of Figure~\ref{fig:main}a (coupling to radius $-0.60$). The same regression applied to the readout $w\cdot x$ returns the point cloud's own $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$ (median metric cosine with the decoder's $0.55$--$0.65$), and an independent split-half quadratic-chart estimator of $\mathrm{II}$ gives the same shape-term sign on three of four labels.

\paragraph{Across encoders.} The quadratic-chart estimator's mean-bending trace, run on five encoders at the same 512 anchors ($d=16$, $k=2{,}048$: ViT-B, DINOv3, CLIP-B, ConvNeXt-B, ViT-L), gives density-controlled associations with the magnitude probe's local $R^2$ of $-0.24$, $0.00$, $+0.27$, $+0.23$ and $-0.19$. A trace statistic has no encoder-stable sign, as Eq.~\eqref{eq:hess} predicts; a cross-encoder test should carry the terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid}.

\section{Discussion}

A decoder yields a pointwise second fundamental form at production scale whose readout-facing component agrees with an independent estimator and with the data. The residual expansion names what a linear map responds to, and on galaxies its terms behave as predicted: the sphere term and the Hessian mismatch carry the predicted negative sign, and the shape term is label-signed because it enters through its alignment with the label's own Hessian. The same expansion explains why mean-curvature statistics disagree across estimators and encoders: they are traces of a tensor that each map contracts differently. MKNN and CKA are invariant to rigid motions of either embedding, so if weak convergence is attributed to the failure of a linear alignment map, one embedding's mean curvature is not the relevant second-order quantity; after tangent alignment the relevant normal term is $\mathrm{II}_G - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F$, which needs both embeddings' second fundamental forms and a map between tangent frames. Our pilot at $d=20$ (2{,}048 anchors) supplies both and finds, for the fitted global ridge alignment, substantial mismatch already at first order: it carries HSC tangent frames into the Legacy embedding with 44\% residual, and the second-order term shows no density-free association yet ($+0.04$, n.s.; alternative estimators $-0.24$ to $+0.13$). In sum, mean curvature of a single representation does not explain weak Platonic convergence; the obstruction is relative and, for the fitted linear alignment, already first order; testing it means aligning tangent frames first and then the relative second fundamental form, for which the instrument is in hand.

\emph{Limitations.} One survey pair. The label's Hessian is a local estimate ($R^2$ gain over linear 0.10--0.17); a same-size fit in the quadratic-chart estimator's frame failed a split-half gate at cosine 0.20. The sphere term is not separated from ridge shrinkage of extreme predictions. Permutation $p$-values assume anchor exchangeability under overlapping patches.
