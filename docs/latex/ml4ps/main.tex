\begin{abstract}
The Platonic Universe study measured the convergence of foundation-model representations on galaxy images as one number per pair of embeddings. We ask whether that convergence is concentrated where the embedding manifold is flat. A pointwise curvature field, obtained by differentiating a trained decoder and validated against analytic truth in that regime, finds mean-curvature magnitude correlating negatively with cross-survey neighbour agreement at every latent dimension, with local density explaining 50 to 80 percent of it. A second-order expansion of a linear map restricted to the manifold explains why: a readout sees the second fundamental form contracted with its normal component, $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, and an alignment sees the relative second fundamental form of the two embeddings, neither visible to mean-curvature magnitude at large codimension. On a unit-normalized embedding that Hessian splits into a sphere term, proportional to the readout's own value, and a shape term. On a synthetic surface at production scale the sphere term reads $-0.74$ to $-0.80$ across samplings that swing the $\norm{H}$ partial from $-0.29$ to $+0.26$; on 86{,}471 labelled galaxies it is negative on every label, the mismatch between the label's own Hessian and the shape term is negative wherever the label has local structure, and the shape term alone takes the label-dependent sign that mismatch predicts. Convergence tests must condition on relative curvature, not on one model's.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis holds that models trained on different data converge toward a shared representation; Duraphe et al.~\citep{duraphe2025platonic} tested it on the sky: for each of a range of vision models, galaxies imaged by two surveys (Legacy Survey and Hyper Suprime-Cam, HSC) share only 0.3 to 2.3 percent of their nearest neighbours across the two embeddings, a fraction that rises with model capacity, one number per model. We ask where on the manifold that agreement lives: if two models disagree most where the embedding surface bends sharply, local mean curvature should associate negatively with local cross-survey agreement. Testing it needs a pointwise curvature instrument at intrinsic dimension near 20 in ambient dimension 768, and a design that separates curvature from local density.

We contribute (i) a pointwise mean-curvature and second-fundamental-form instrument from a trained decoder, validated against analytic truth in the production regime; (ii) a density-controlled test of the curvature--alignment prediction under two probes, which finds no effect free of density; (iii) the residual expansion of a linear map on a submanifold, which names the curvature such a map responds to and shows mean-curvature magnitude blind to it; and (iv) a known-surface and a real-data test on the linear-readout side, where the expansion's terms behave as predicted and account for a label-dependent sign.

\section{Pointwise curvature from a decoder}
\label{sec:instr}

\paragraph{Data.} DINOv3 ViT-B/16 embeddings of 101{,}725 galaxies imaged by both HSC and the Legacy Survey~\citep{duraphe2025platonic}, from which we draw 10{,}000 rows once with a recorded seed. A second set of 86{,}471 single-survey ViT-B embeddings with catalogue labels ($r$-band magnitude, photometric redshift, smooth fraction, stellar mass) serves the readout experiment. Embeddings are unit-normalized (data on $S^{767}$); the manifold has trivial first homology ($\beta_1 = 0$ under bootstrap bands~\citep{fasy2014confidence}), so one Euclidean chart is appropriate.

\paragraph{Instrument.} Let $F:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^{768}$ be the decoder of a plain auto-encoder (three hidden layers of width 250, SiLU), fit on 80\% of rows; curvature is evaluated on held-out rows. With $J = DF(z)$, metric $g = J^{\top}J$, normal projector $P_N = I - Jg^{-1}J^{\top}$ and second fundamental form $\mathrm{II} = P_N D^2F(z)$, the mean-curvature vector is $H = \operatorname{tr}_g \mathrm{II}$, by automatic differentiation checked against finite differences. On the unit sphere every $d$-submanifold carries a radial component of $H$ equal to $-d$ that says nothing about shape, so the map differentiated is $F/\norm{F}$ (after which $\Hrad = -d$ to $10^{-14}$) and $\norm{\Htan}$ carries the shape. Decoder curvature has served as a regularizer~\citep{lee2023curvature} and been read from templates~\citep{acosta2022templates}; here it is an instrument, validated against closed-form truth.

\paragraph{Validation.} Scored on surfaces with closed-form curvature on four axes: rank (Spearman against true $\norm{H}$), direction (cosine of $H$ vectors), magnitude ratio and calibration. At $D=768$ an in-sphere surface $G(z) = Q\,\mathrm{normalize}([\phi(z);\,0.8\,h(z);\,0\ldots])$ ($\phi$ the inverse stereographic map $\mathbb{R}^{16}\to S^{16}$, $h$ four Gaussian bumps, $Q$ a fixed rotation, span 18 dimensions) at $n=86{,}471$, $k=2{,}048$ gives rank 0.94, direction cosine 0.999, magnitude ratio 1.00, and 0.75 rank under noise at 25\% of patch radius.

\section{Curvature and cross-survey convergence}
\label{sec:align}

\paragraph{Probe.} Per-point mutual $k$-nearest-neighbour score $\mathrm{MKNN}_i = |N_k(z_1)_i \cap N_k(z_2)_i|/k$ at $k=20$ between the HSC and Legacy Survey embeddings, the Platonic Universe statistic made local, correlated with $\norm{H}$ over all 10{,}000 points at $d\in\{20,25,32\}$. Centered kernel alignment~\citep{kornblith2019similarity} is the second probe.

\paragraph{Result.} The raw association $\rho(\norm{H},\mathrm{MKNN})$ is $-0.112$, $-0.128$ and $-0.024$ at $d=20,25,32$ ($p<10^{-5}$, $<10^{-5}$, $0.009$), in the predicted direction. But density correlates with agreement at $-0.21$ at every $d$ and with $\norm{H}$ at $+0.43$, $+0.32$ and $+0.01$, so residualizing on density leaves $-0.024$, $-0.066$ and $-0.022$: 78\%, 49\% and 8\% of the association removed. Under a permutation null within density strata only $d=25$ survives ($p<5{\times}10^{-5}$; replicating across three decoder seeds; the CKA tertile gap within strata agrees at $d=20$ and 25, $-0.05$ to $-0.08$ against a null band of $\pm0.017$). Replacing $\norm{H}$ by $\norm{\Htan}$ collapses the $d=25$ partial to $-0.023$, into the range of the two $d$ that fail. Convergence is not detectably concentrated by mean-curvature magnitude once density is controlled.

\section{What a linear map sees on a curved manifold}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $M\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ be $d$-dimensional; at $x_0$ take an orthonormal tangent frame $J$, the second fundamental form $\mathrm{II}$ (normal-valued, symmetric $d\times d$), $H = \operatorname{tr}\mathrm{II}$, and for a vector $w$ its tangent and normal parts $w_T + w_N$. Writing $M$ locally as $x_0 + Ju + \tfrac12\mathrm{II}(u,u) + O(|u|^3)$,
\begin{equation}
\nabla_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = J^{\top}w,\qquad \operatorname{Hess}_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w_N, \mathrm{II}\rangle,\qquad \Delta_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w, H\rangle .
\label{eq:hess}
\end{equation}
A linear function is exactly as non-linear on $M$ as $M$ bends in that function's normal direction. For $M\subset S^{D-1}$ the normal bundle splits into the sphere's radial direction and the rest, $\mathrm{II} = \mathrm{II}^{S} - g\,\hat x$, so
$\operatorname{Hess}_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w_N, \mathrm{II}^{S}\rangle - (w\!\cdot\!\hat x)\,g$ \refstepcounter{equation}(\theequation)\label{eq:split}: a linear readout of a unit-normalized embedding is bent by the manifold's shape and, whatever the manifold, by the sphere in proportion to the readout's own value $\hat y - b_0$. For a label $y$ on $M$, a global affine probe $\hat y = w\cdot x + b_0$ and a Gaussian patch with isotropic second moment $s^2$ in the induced metric, with $\bar c$ the patch-mean residual, $\delta = \nabla_M y - J^{\top}w$, $\Delta = \operatorname{Hess}_M y - \langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{patch}}\big[(y-\hat y)^2\big] = \bar c^{\,2} + s^2\norm{\delta}^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}s^4\norm{\Delta}_F^2 + O(s^4\norm{\delta}) + O(s^6).
\label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
For two embeddings $F, G$ of the same objects and a linear map $A$ aligning their tangent spaces, the same expansion gives $G - (G_0 + A(F - F_0)) = \tfrac12(\mathrm{II}_G - A\,\mathrm{II}_F)(u,u) + o(|u|^2)$: the obstruction to linear alignment is the \emph{relative} second fundamental form.

So the curvature a linear map pays for is $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$ or $\mathrm{II}_G - A\,\mathrm{II}_F$; $\norm{H}$ bounds only a trace and need not correlate with either at codimension 750; the term carries $s^4$, so patch radius, hence density, multiplies it; and when the label has intrinsic curvature of its own, $\Delta$ carries the residual and the curvature piece can take either sign.

\section{The probe-facing curvature, on a known surface and on galaxies}
\label{sec:real}

The readout side can be tested directly: $w$ is known and $\mathrm{II}$ comes from the decoder. Statistic: five-fold out-of-fold ridge at $\alpha=100$ from embedding to label; local $R^2$ inside each of 512 anchors' 2{,}048-neighbourhoods; rank-partial Spearman against local $R^2$ controlling for log patch radius, local label variance and count and, as a stronger control, log radius at $k\in\{16,64,256,1024,2048\}$. Per anchor we compute $\norm{\Htan}$ and the probe-facing Hessian $\norm{\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle}_g$ with $w_N = w - Jg^{-1}J^{\top}w$, split by Eq.~\eqref{eq:split} into its shape and sphere terms.

\paragraph{Known surface.} The validation surface is resampled with weight $\norm{\Htan}^\gamma$, so only where the points sit changes (Figure~\ref{fig:main}a). The $\norm{\Htan}$ partial follows the sampling from $-0.29$ at coupling $+0.80$ through zero to a plateau near $+0.22$, and the decoder reproduces it. The probe-facing Hessian $\norm{\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle}$ reads $-0.74$ to $-0.80$ at every sampling. On this surface it is the sphere term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split}: rank $0.996$--$1.000$ with $\sqrt d\,|\hat y - b_0|$, the in-sphere part 20 to 40 times smaller (one in-sphere normal direction) and, where resolvable, following $\norm{\Htan}$. The fixture thus confirms the expansion's sphere term: an intrinsically linear label loses local $R^2$ where the readout's own value is large, whatever the sampling. With a nonlinear label the sphere term reads null and $\norm{\Delta}$ carries the partial ($-0.2$ to $-0.6$), as Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid} predicts.

\paragraph{Galaxies.} Here the two terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split} trade places: the shape term dominates the probe-facing Hessian (rank $0.96$--$0.99$ with the full norm). To read it through Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid} we estimate $\operatorname{Hess}_M y$ per anchor by a least-squares quadratic of the label on the tangent-projected coordinates $u = g^{-1}J^{\top}(x - x_0)$ of its 2{,}048 neighbours, where the quadratic coefficient is the covariant Hessian. Three regularities (Table~\ref{tab:real}). The sphere term is negative on every label and both $d$ ($-0.13$ to $-0.36$), as on the fixture. Wherever the label has local structure (magnitude, redshift, smooth fraction; stellar mass is null throughout) the mismatch is negative ($-0.09$ to $-0.45$) and the alignment $\cos_g(\operatorname{Hess}_M y,\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle)$ positive ($+0.07$ to $+0.43$): local $R^2$ is higher where the manifold bends in the readout's direction the way the label bends. The shape term alone is therefore label-signed (positive for magnitude and smooth fraction, null to weakly negative for redshift) because it is one of three terms in $\norm{\Delta}^2 = \norm{\operatorname{Hess}_M y}^2 - 2\langle\operatorname{Hess}_M y,\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle\rangle + \norm{\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle}^2$: its sign follows the cross term's where that is nonzero and the remaining square where it is null. $\norm{\Htan}$ changes sign between $d=16$ and $20$ for magnitude: it sits on the curve of Figure~\ref{fig:main}a (coupling to radius $-0.60$). The same regression applied to the readout $w\cdot x$ returns the point cloud's own $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$ (median metric cosine with the decoder's $0.55$--$0.65$), and an independent split-half quadratic-chart estimator of $\mathrm{II}$ gives the same shape-term sign on three of four labels.

\paragraph{Across encoders.} The quadratic-chart estimator's mean-bending trace, run on five encoders at the same 512 anchors ($d=16$, $k=2{,}048$: ViT-B, DINOv3, CLIP-B, ConvNeXt-B, ViT-L), gives density-controlled associations with the magnitude probe's local $R^2$ of $-0.24$, $0.00$, $+0.27$, $+0.23$ and $-0.19$. A trace statistic has no encoder-stable sign, as Eq.~\eqref{eq:hess} predicts for a quantity the readout does not see; the terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid} are what a cross-encoder test should carry.

\section{Discussion}

The design of Section~\ref{sec:align} could not have found curvature-concentrated convergence: MKNN and CKA are invariant to linear maps that leave the relative second fundamental form unchanged, so a per-point convergence score can be conditioned on curvature only through $\mathrm{II}_G - A\,\mathrm{II}_F$, which needs both embeddings' second fundamental forms and a map between tangent frames. A pilot at $d=20$ (2{,}048 anchors) finds a global ridge map carries HSC tangent frames into the Legacy embedding with 44\% residual and no density-free association of $\norm{\mathrm{II}_G(L\cdot,L\cdot) - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F}$ with MKNN ($+0.04$, n.s.; a locally fitted tangent map gives $-0.18$ to $-0.24$, a decoder-free estimate $+0.13$): the obstruction is already first order. On the readout side, where the map is known, the sphere term and the Hessian mismatch carry the predicted negative sign on the known surface and on galaxies, and the shape term is label-signed because it enters through its alignment with the label's own Hessian.

\emph{Limitations.} One survey pair; the label's Hessian is a noisy local estimate ($R^2$ gain over linear 0.10--0.17; a 136-coefficient fit of the same kind failed a split-half reliability gate); the sphere term is not separated from ridge shrinkage of extreme predictions; permutation $p$-values assume anchor exchangeability under overlapping patches.
