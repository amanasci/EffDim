\begin{abstract}
The Platonic Universe tested the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) by measuring the convergence of foundation-model representations of galaxy images with mutual $k$-nearest-neighbour agreement (MKNN). We ask whether that convergence is concentrated where the embedding manifold is flat. A pointwise curvature field from a trained decoder, validated against analytic truth, finds mean-curvature magnitude correlating negatively with cross-survey neighbour agreement at every latent dimension, and that local density removes most of it at $d=20$, half at $d=25$ and little of the already small $d=32$ value. A second-order expansion of a linear map restricted to the manifold explains why: a readout sees the second fundamental form contracted with its normal component, $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, and, at second order, an alignment's error splits into a connection mismatch and the relative second fundamental form projected into the target normal space; mean curvature retains only the metric trace of $\mathrm{II}$ and determines neither. On a unit-normalized embedding that Hessian splits into a sphere term, proportional to the readout's own value, and a shape term. On a synthetic surface the sphere term holds at $-0.74$ to $-0.80$ across samplings that swing the $\norm{\Htan}$ partial from $-0.29$ to $+0.26$; on 86{,}471 labelled galaxies the mismatch between the label Hessian and the full probe-facing Hessian is negative wherever the label has local structure and survives cross-fitting, the sphere term is negative on two of four labels under a weak-ridge sensitivity, and the shape term alone takes a label-dependent sign consistent with that decomposition. Mean curvature of a single representation is therefore not a viable explanation for weak Platonic convergence; the obstruction is relative and, for the fitted global linear alignment, already present at first order.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis~\citep{huh2024platonic} holds that models trained on different data converge toward a shared representation; Duraphe et al.~\citep{duraphe2025platonic} tested it on the sky: galaxies imaged by two surveys (Legacy Survey and Hyper Suprime-Cam, HSC) and embedded by a family of different vision models share only 0.3 to 2.3 percent of their nearest neighbours across the two surveys. We ask where on the manifold that agreement lives: if two models disagree most where the embedding surface bends sharply, local mean curvature should associate negatively with local cross-survey agreement.

We contribute (i) a pointwise mean-curvature and second-fundamental-form instrument from a trained decoder, validated against analytic truth in the production regime; (ii) a density-controlled test of the curvature--alignment prediction, which finds no effect free of density; (iii) the residual expansion of a linear map on a submanifold, which names the curvature such a map responds to and shows mean-curvature magnitude is blind to it; and (iv) known-surface and real-data tests on the readout side, where the expansion's terms behave as predicted.

\section{Pointwise curvature from a decoder}
\label{sec:instr}

\paragraph{Data.} DINOv3~\citep{simeoni2025dinov3} ViT-B/16 embeddings of 101{,}725 galaxies imaged by both HSC~\citep{aihara2018hsc} and the Legacy Survey~\citep{dey2019legacy}, (Platonic Universe release~\citep{duraphe2025platonic}); we draw 10{,}000 rows once with a recorded seed. A second set of 86{,}471 single-survey ViT-B embeddings with catalogue labels ($r$-band magnitude, photometric redshift, smooth fraction, stellar mass; the AstroPT galaxy set~\citep{smith2024astropt}) serves the readout experiment. Embeddings are unit-normalized (data on $S^{767}$). Curvature is evaluated only where $J$ has full rank.

\paragraph{Instrument.} Let $F:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^{768}$ be the decoder of a plain auto-encoder (three hidden layers of width 250, SiLU), fit on 80\% of rows; curvature is evaluated on held-out rows. With $J = DF(z)$, metric $g = J^{\top}J$, normal projector $P_N = I - Jg^{-1}J^{\top}$ and second fundamental form $\mathrm{II} = P_N D^2F(z)$, the mean-curvature vector of the raw decoder surface is $\Hraw = \operatorname{tr}_g \mathrm{II}$, by automatic differentiation. On the unit sphere every $d$-submanifold carries a radial component of $H$ equal to $-d$ that says nothing about shape. To isolate shape, the map differentiated is $F/\norm{F}$, whose $H$ splits exactly as $-d\,\hat x + \Htan$ ($\Hrad = -d$ to $10^{-14}$); $\Htan$, normal to $M$ within the sphere, carries the shape. Section~\ref{sec:align} uses the pre-registered $\norm{\Hraw}$; Sections~\ref{sec:theory}--\ref{sec:real} use the projected decoder. Decoder curvature has served as a regularizer~\citep{lee2023curvature} and been read from templates~\citep{acosta2022templates}; here it is a validated instrument.

\paragraph{Validation.} Scored against closed-form curvature on rank (Spearman), direction (cosine) and magnitude ratio. At $D=768$ an in-sphere surface $G(z) = Q\,\mathrm{normalize}([\phi(z);\,0.8\,h(z);\,0\ldots])$ ($\phi$ inverse stereographic $\mathbb{R}^{16}\to S^{16}$, $h$ a scalar sum of four Gaussian bumps, $Q$ a rotation; 18-dimensional span) at $n=86{,}471$, $k=2{,}048$ gives rank 0.94 against the true $\norm{\Htan}$ (equivalently $\norm{H}$, the radial part being fixed), direction cosine 0.999 and magnitude ratio 1.00 for the full vector (rank 0.75 under 25\% noise).

\section{Curvature and cross-survey convergence}
\label{sec:align}

\paragraph{Probe.} Per-point mutual $k$-nearest-neighbour score $\mathrm{MKNN}_i = |N_k(z_1)_i \cap N_k(z_2)_i|/k$ at $k=20$ between the HSC and Legacy Survey embeddings, the Platonic Universe statistic made local, correlated with $\norm{\Hraw}$ over all 10{,}000 points at $d\in\{20,25,32\}$. Centered kernel alignment~\citep{kornblith2019similarity} is the second probe.

\paragraph{Result.} The raw association $\rho(\norm{\Hraw},\mathrm{MKNN})$ is $-0.112$, $-0.128$ and $-0.024$ at $d=20,25,32$ ($p<10^{-5}$, $<10^{-5}$, $0.009$), in the predicted direction. But $k$-NN statistics are density-sensitive~\citep{radovanovic2010hubs}: local density (the $k=30$ nearest-neighbour estimate $k/(nV_d r_k^d)$ on the Legacy embedding, $d=20$) correlates with agreement at $-0.21$ at every $d$ and with $\norm{\Hraw}$ at $+0.43$, $+0.32$ and $+0.01$, so residualizing on density leaves $-0.024$, $-0.066$ and $-0.022$: 78\%, 49\% and 8\% of the association removed. Under a permutation null within density strata only $d=25$ survives ($p<5{\times}10^{-5}$; three decoder seeds; the CKA tertile gap within strata agrees at $d=20$ and 25). Removing the radial component of $\Hraw$ (the raw surface is not on the sphere, so the two rank at $\rho = 0.89$--$0.96$ rather than identically) collapses the $d=25$ partial to $-0.023$, into the range of the two $d$ that fail. Convergence is not detectably concentrated by mean-curvature magnitude once density is controlled.

\section{What a linear map sees on a curved manifold}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $M\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ be a $d$-dimensional submanifold, $d<D$; at $x_0$ take an orthonormal tangent frame $J$, the second fundamental form $\mathrm{II}$ (normal-valued, symmetric $d\times d$), $H = \operatorname{tr}\mathrm{II}$, and for a vector $w$ its tangent and normal parts $w_T + w_N$. In normal coordinates $u$ at $x_0$, $M$ is $x_0 + Ju + \tfrac12\mathrm{II}(u,u) + O(|u|^3)$, so
\begin{equation}
[\nabla_M (w\!\cdot\! x)]_J = J^{\top}w,\qquad \operatorname{Hess}_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w_N, \mathrm{II}\rangle,\qquad \Delta_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w, H\rangle .
\label{eq:hess}
\end{equation}
To second order, a linear function is as non-linear on $M$ as $M$ bends in that function's normal direction~\citep{lee2018riemannian}. For $M\subset S^{D-1}$ the normal bundle splits into the sphere's radial direction and the rest, $\mathrm{II} = \mathrm{II}^{S} - g\,\hat x$, so
$\operatorname{Hess}_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w_N, \mathrm{II}^{S}\rangle - (w\!\cdot\!\hat x)\,g$ \refstepcounter{equation}(\theequation)\label{eq:split}: a linear readout of a unit-normalized embedding is bent by the manifold's shape and, whatever the manifold, by the sphere in proportion to the readout's own value $\hat y - b_0$. For a label $y$ on $M$, a global affine probe $\hat y = w\cdot x + b_0$ and a patch $u\sim N(0, s^2 I_d)$ in normal coordinates, embedding and label locally $C^4$ (the $\tfrac12$ is the Gaussian constant; real $k$-NN patches share only the tensor structure), with $\bar c$ the patch-mean residual, $\delta = [\nabla_M y]_J - J^{\top}w$, $\Delta = \operatorname{Hess}_M y - \langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{patch}}\big[(y-\hat y)^2\big] = \bar c^{\,2} + s^2\norm{\delta}^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}s^4\norm{\Delta}_F^2 + O(s^4\norm{\delta}) + O(s^6).
\label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
For two embeddings $F, G$ of the same objects in a common chart $u$ and a linear map $A$ with $AJ_F = J_G$, the same expansion gives $G - (G_0 + A(F - F_0)) = \tfrac12\big[J_G(\Gamma^G - \Gamma^F) + \mathrm{II}_G - A\,\mathrm{II}_F\big](u,u) + O(|u|^3)$, with $\Gamma$ the induced connections: the second-order obstruction to linear alignment is a connection mismatch plus the \emph{relative} second fundamental form, and reduces to the latter when the induced connections agree at $x_0$ (in particular under a local intrinsic isometry); the extrinsic term's normal part is $\mathrm{II}_G - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F$.

So the curvature a linear readout pays for is $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, and an alignment's second-order error has an extrinsic part whose normal component is $\mathrm{II}_G - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F$; $\norm{H}$ contains only the norm of the metric trace of $\mathrm{II}$ and need not correlate with either; the second-order term scales as $s^4$, and with fixed-$k$ neighbourhoods the sampling density sets $s$, so curvature effects scored on patches are density-sensitive; and when the label has intrinsic curvature of its own, $\Delta$ carries the residual and the curvature piece can take either sign.

\section{The probe-facing curvature, on a known surface and on galaxies}
\label{sec:real}

Statistic: five-fold out-of-fold ridge probe~\citep{alain2017probes} at the pre-registered $\alpha=100$ from embedding to label; local $R^2$ inside each of 512 anchors' 2{,}048-neighbourhoods; rank-partial Spearman against local $R^2$ with Freedman--Lane $p$-values~\citep{freedman1983permutation}, controlling for log patch radius, local label variance and count and, stronger, log radius at $k\in\{16,\ldots,2048\}$ (five scales). \paragraph{Known surface.} The validation surface is resampled with weight $\norm{\Htan}^\gamma$, so only where the points sit changes (Figure~\ref{fig:main}a). The $\norm{\Htan}$ partial follows the sampling from $-0.29$ to $+0.22$, and the decoder reproduces it. The probe-facing Hessian $\norm{\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle}$ has partial correlation $-0.74$ to $-0.80$ at every sampling. On this surface it is the sphere term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split}: rank $0.996$--$1.000$ with $\sqrt d\,|\hat y - b_0|$, the in-sphere part 20 to 40 times smaller. With a nonlinear label the sphere term is indistinguishable from zero and $\norm{\Delta}$ carries the partial ($-0.2$ to $-0.6$), as Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid} predicts.

\paragraph{Galaxies.} Here the two terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split} trade places: the shape term dominates the probe-facing Hessian (rank $0.96$--$0.99$ with the full norm). We estimate $\operatorname{Hess}_M y$ per anchor by a least-squares quadratic of the label on the tangent-projected coordinates $u = g^{-1}J^{\top}(x - x_0)$ of its 2{,}048 neighbours (tangent-plane projection is a local Monge chart, in which the first metric derivatives and hence the Christoffel symbols vanish at the anchor, so the quadratic coefficient is the covariant Hessian there). Three regularities (Table~\ref{tab:real}; other decoder seeds and widths in Appendix~A). The sphere term is negative on every label and both $d$ ($-0.13$ to $-0.36$) under the sealed probe; with a weak-ridge probe ($\alpha=1$, Appendix~B) it stays negative for magnitude and smooth fraction ($-0.21$, $-0.12$) and vanishes for redshift and stellar mass, where the sealed-probe sign came with ridge shrinkage of extreme predictions. Wherever the label has local structure (magnitude, redshift, smooth fraction; stellar mass is indistinguishable from zero throughout) the mismatch is negative ($-0.09$ to $-0.45$; unchanged under cross-fitting, Appendix~B) and the alignment $\cos_g(\operatorname{Hess}_M y,\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle)$ positive ($+0.07$ to $+0.43$): local $R^2$ is higher where the manifold bends in the readout's direction the way the label bends. The shape term alone is label-signed (positive for magnitude and smooth fraction, null to weakly negative for redshift), consistent with Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid}: it is one of three terms in $\norm{\Delta}^2 = \norm{\operatorname{Hess}_M y}^2 - 2\langle\operatorname{Hess}_M y,\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle\rangle + \norm{\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle}^2$, so raising it can shrink or enlarge the mismatch depending on its alignment with the label Hessian; empirically its sign follows the cross term's where that is nonzero and the remaining square where it is null. $\norm{\Htan}$ changes sign between $d=16$ and $20$ for magnitude: it sits on the curve of Figure~\ref{fig:main}a (coupling to radius $-0.60$). The same regression applied to the readout $w\cdot x$ returns the point cloud's own $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$ (median metric cosine with the decoder's $0.55$--$0.65$), and an independent split-half quadratic-chart estimator of $\mathrm{II}$ gives the same shape-term sign on three of four labels.

\paragraph{Across encoders.} The quadratic-chart estimator's mean-bending trace on five encoders at the same anchors (ViT-B, DINOv3, CLIP-B, ConvNeXt-B, ViT-L; $d=16$, $k=2{,}048$) has density-controlled associations with the magnitude probe's local $R^2$ of $-0.24$, $0.00$, $+0.27$, $+0.23$ and $-0.19$: a trace statistic has no encoder-stable sign, consistent with Eq.~\eqref{eq:hess}.

\section{Discussion}

A decoder yields a pointwise second fundamental form at production scale that agrees with an independent estimator and with the data. The residual expansion names what a linear map responds to, and on galaxies its terms behave as predicted: the Hessian mismatch carries the predicted negative sign, robust to cross-fitting and probe strength; the sphere term does so on two of four labels under a weak-ridge sensitivity; and the shape term is label-signed because it enters through its alignment with the label's own Hessian. The same expansion explains why mean-curvature statistics disagree across estimators and encoders: they are traces of a tensor that each map contracts differently. If weak convergence is attributed to the failure of a linear alignment map, one embedding's mean curvature is not the relevant second-order quantity; after tangent alignment the relevant normal term is $\mathrm{II}_G - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F$, which needs both embeddings' second fundamental forms and a map between tangent frames. Our pilot at $d=20$ (2{,}048 anchors, two seeds; Appendix~C) finds the fitted global ridge map carries HSC tangent frames into the Legacy embedding with 44\% residual, a first-order mismatch, and no estimator-stable second-order association: the global-decoder, locally aligned and decoder-free estimates read $+0.04$ (n.s.), $-0.18$ to $-0.23$ and $+0.13$, disagreeing in sign. In sum: single-representation mean curvature is not the second-order statistic linear alignment depends on; a readout responds to $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, and alignment additionally to tangent mismatch, connection mismatch and relative extrinsic bending. On this survey pair, single-model mean curvature has no robust density-controlled association with cross-survey convergence.

\emph{Limitations.} One survey pair. The label's Hessian is a local estimate ($R^2$ gain over linear 0.10--0.17); its norm is reliably ranked (split-half $\rho$ 0.92) but its direction only moderately (tensor cosine 0.19--0.35). Stars are per cell; mismatch and alignment are the primary hypotheses, the rest exploratory. Permutation $p$-values assume anchor exchangeability under overlapping patches.
