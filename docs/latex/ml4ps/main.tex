\begin{abstract}
The Platonic Universe tested the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) by measuring the convergence of foundation-model representations of galaxy images with mutual $k$-nearest-neighbour agreement (MKNN). We ask whether that convergence is concentrated where the embedding manifold is flat. A pre-registered raw-decoder mean-curvature statistic is negatively associated with cross-survey agreement, but density removes most of the signal and the one residual case disappears once its radial component is removed. A second-order expansion of a linear map restricted to the manifold explains why: a readout sees $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, the second fundamental form contracted with its normal component; an alignment's second-order error is a connection mismatch plus a relative extrinsic term; mean curvature is only the metric trace of $\mathrm{II}$ and determines neither. On 86{,}471 labelled galaxies a linear readout gains accuracy where the manifold bends toward the label's own curvature and loses it where the manifold bends away. In a counterfactual local second-order surrogate of the readout, the manifold's bending in the readout's normal direction raises local $R^2$ at 79--99\% of anchors, its sign reversal lowers it at 97--100\%, and a random orientation of matched strength shows no such asymmetry and is typically harmful under either sign, on five encoders; across anchors, the mismatch between the label Hessian and the probe-facing Hessian is negative wherever the label has local structure and survives cross-fitting and a change of encoder. Mean curvature of a single representation does not explain weak Platonic convergence; the obstruction is relative and, for the fitted global linear alignment, present at first order.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis~\citep{huh2024platonic} holds that models trained on different data converge toward a shared representation; Duraphe et al.~\citep{duraphe2025platonic} tested it on galaxy images: galaxies imaged by two surveys (Legacy Survey and Hyper Suprime-Cam, HSC) share only 0.3 to 2.3 percent of nearest neighbours across surveys under a family of vision models. We ask where on the manifold that agreement is concentrated: if two models disagree most where the embedding surface bends sharply, local mean curvature should associate negatively with local cross-survey agreement.

We contribute (i) a pointwise mean-curvature and second-fundamental-form instrument from a trained decoder, validated against analytic truth; (ii) a density-controlled test of the curvature--alignment prediction, which finds no effect free of density; (iii) the residual expansion of a linear map on a submanifold, which names the curvature such a map responds to; and (iv) known-surface, cross-encoder and interventional tests on the readout side: a linear readout gains local accuracy where the manifold bends toward the label's curvature and loses it where the manifold bends away, at nearly every anchor on five encoders.

\section{Pointwise curvature from a decoder}
\label{sec:instr}

\paragraph{Data.} DINOv3~\citep{simeoni2025dinov3} ViT-B/16 embeddings of 101{,}725 galaxies imaged by both HSC~\citep{aihara2018hsc} and the Legacy Survey~\citep{dey2019legacy} (Platonic Universe release~\citep{duraphe2025platonic}); we draw 10{,}000 rows once with a recorded seed. A second set of 86{,}471 single-survey galaxies embedded by a supervised ImageNet-21k ViT-B/16 (``ViT-B'' below; not DINOv3) with catalogue labels ($r$-band magnitude, photometric redshift, smooth fraction, stellar mass; the AstroPT galaxy set~\citep{smith2024astropt}) serves the readout experiment. We unit-normalize embeddings (data on $S^{767}$).

\paragraph{Instrument.} Let $F:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^{768}$ be the decoder of a plain auto-encoder (three hidden layers of width 250, SiLU), fit on 80\% of rows; we evaluate curvature on held-out rows. With $J = DF(z)$, metric $g = J^{\top}J$, normal projector $P_N = I - Jg^{-1}J^{\top}$ and second fundamental form $\mathrm{II} = P_N D^2F(z)$, the mean-curvature vector of the raw decoder surface is $\Hraw = \operatorname{tr}_g \mathrm{II}$, by automatic differentiation. On the unit sphere every $d$-submanifold has radial mean curvature $-d$, which says nothing about shape; differentiating $F/\norm{F}$ instead splits $H$ exactly as $-d\,\hat x + \Htan$ ($\Hrad = -d$ to $10^{-14}$), and $\Htan$ carries the shape. Section~\ref{sec:align} uses the pre-registered $\norm{\Hraw}$; Sections~\ref{sec:theory}--\ref{sec:real} use the projected decoder. Decoder curvature has been a regularizer~\citep{lee2023curvature} and an estimator~\citep{acosta2023extrinsic}.

\paragraph{Validation.} Against closed-form curvature, at $D=768$ an in-sphere surface $G(z) = Q\,\mathrm{normalize}([\phi(z);\,0.8\,h(z);\,0\ldots])$ ($\phi$ inverse stereographic $\mathbb{R}^{16}\to S^{16}$, $h$ a scalar sum of four Gaussian bumps, $Q$ a rotation; 18-dimensional span) at $n=86{,}471$, $k=2{,}048$ gives rank 0.94 against the true $\norm{\Htan}$, direction cosine 0.999 and magnitude ratio 1.00 for the full vector.

\section{Curvature and cross-survey convergence}
\label{sec:align}

We correlate the per-point mutual $k$-nearest-neighbour score $\mathrm{MKNN}_i = |N_k(z_1)_i \cap N_k(z_2)_i|/k$ at $k=20$ between the HSC and Legacy Survey embeddings with $\norm{\Hraw}$ at $d\in\{20,25,32\}$. The raw association $\rho(\norm{\Hraw},\mathrm{MKNN})$ is $-0.112$, $-0.128$ and $-0.024$ at $d=20,25,32$ ($p<10^{-5}$, $<10^{-5}$, $0.009$). But $k$-NN statistics are density-sensitive~\citep{radovanovic2010hubs}: local density ($k=30$ estimate on the Legacy embedding) correlates with agreement at $-0.21$ at every $d$ and with $\norm{\Hraw}$ at $+0.43$, $+0.32$ and $+0.01$, so residualizing on density leaves $-0.024$, $-0.066$ and $-0.022$. Under a permutation null within density strata only $d=25$ survives ($p<5{\times}10^{-5}$; CKA~\citep{kornblith2019similarity} agrees), and removing the radial component of $\Hraw$ collapses it to $-0.023$.

\section{What a linear map sees on a curved manifold}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $M\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ be a $d$-dimensional submanifold, $d<D$; at $x_0$ take an orthonormal tangent frame $J$, the second fundamental form $\mathrm{II}$ (normal-valued, symmetric $d\times d$), $H = \operatorname{tr}\mathrm{II}$, and for a vector $w$ its tangent and normal parts $w_T + w_N$. In normal coordinates $u$ at $x_0$, $M$ is $x_0 + Ju + \tfrac12\mathrm{II}(u,u) + O(|u|^3)$~\citep{lee2018riemannian}, so
\begin{equation}
[\nabla_M (w\!\cdot\! x)]_J = J^{\top}w,\qquad \operatorname{Hess}_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w_N, \mathrm{II}\rangle,\qquad \Delta_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w, H\rangle .
\label{eq:hess}
\end{equation}
For $M\subset S^{D-1}$, $\mathrm{II} = \mathrm{II}^{S} - g\,\hat x$ splits off the sphere's radial direction, so
$\operatorname{Hess}_M (w\!\cdot\! x) = \langle w_N, \mathrm{II}^{S}\rangle - (w\!\cdot\!\hat x)\,g$ \refstepcounter{equation}(\theequation)\label{eq:split}: a linear readout of a unit-normalized embedding is bent by the manifold's shape and, whatever the manifold, by the sphere in proportion to the readout's own value $\hat y - b_0$. For a label $y$ on $M$, a global affine probe $\hat y = w\cdot x + b_0$ and a patch $u\sim N(0, s^2 I_d)$ in normal coordinates, embedding and label locally $C^4$ (Gaussian constant $\tfrac12$; real $k$-NN patches share only the tensor structure), with $\bar c$ the patch-mean residual, $\delta = [\nabla_M y]_J - J^{\top}w$, $\Delta = \operatorname{Hess}_M y - \langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{patch}}\big[(y-\hat y)^2\big] = \bar c^{\,2} + s^2\norm{\delta}^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}s^4\norm{\Delta}_F^2 + O(s^4\norm{\delta}) + O(s^6).
\label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
For two embeddings $F, G$ of the same objects in a common chart $u$ and a linear map $A$ with $AJ_F = J_G$, the same expansion gives $G - (G_0 + A(F - F_0)) = \tfrac12\big[J_G(\Gamma^G - \Gamma^F) + \mathrm{II}_G - A\,\mathrm{II}_F\big](u,u) + O(|u|^3)$, with $\Gamma$ the induced connections: the second-order residual contains a connection mismatch and a \emph{relative} extrinsic term, whose target-normal component is $\mathrm{II}_G - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F$.

So $\norm{H}$, the metric trace's norm, need not correlate with either term; and since the second-order term scales as $s^4$ and fixed-$k$ neighbourhoods let density set $s$, patch-scored curvature effects are density-sensitive.

\paragraph{Galaxies.} Here the two terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split} trade places: the shape term dominates the probe-facing Hessian (rank $0.96$--$0.99$ with the full norm). We estimate $\operatorname{Hess}_M y$ per anchor by a least-squares quadratic of the label on the tangent-projected coordinates $u = g^{-1}J^{\top}(x - x_0)$ of its 2{,}048 neighbours (a Monge chart, so the coefficient is the covariant Hessian). Three regularities (Table~\ref{tab:real}; Appendix~A). The sphere term is negative on every label and both $d$ ($-0.13$ to $-0.36$) under the sealed probe; with a weak-ridge probe ($\alpha=1$, Appendix~B) it stays negative for magnitude and smooth fraction ($-0.21$, $-0.12$) and vanishes for redshift and stellar mass (ridge shrinkage of extreme predictions). Wherever the label has local structure (magnitude, redshift, smooth fraction; stellar mass is null throughout) the mismatch is negative ($-0.09$ to $-0.45$; unchanged under cross-fitting, Appendix~B) and the alignment $\cos_g(\operatorname{Hess}_M y,\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle)$ positive ($+0.07$ to $+0.43$). The shape term alone is label-signed (positive for magnitude and smooth fraction, null to weakly negative for redshift): with $K = \langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$, $\norm{\Delta}^2 = \norm{\operatorname{Hess}_M y}^2 - 2\langle\operatorname{Hess}_M y,K\rangle + \norm{K}^2$, so the cross term decides for infinitesimal scaling from zero and $\norm{K}^2$ is the finite-magnitude overshoot penalty. The same regression on the readout $w\cdot x$ returns the point cloud's own $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$ (median cosine with the decoder's $0.55$--$0.65$).

\paragraph{Bending toward helps, bending away hurts.} By Eq.~\eqref{eq:resid} curvature lowers the second-order residual relative to $K=0$ iff $2\langle\operatorname{Hess}_M y, K\rangle > \norm{K}^2$: the manifold must bend toward the label's own curvature, and not too far. (\citet{liu2023linear} derive the optimal normal weight of least squares on a hypersurface as a uniqueness and stability condition; manifold capacity~\citep{chung2016linear,slatton2026linear} uses dimension and radius, flattening~\citep{psenka2024flattening} treats curvature as an obstacle, latent curvature tracks generalization~\citep{kaufman2023latent} and biases local regression~\citep{cheng2013local}; none conditions on the sign of $\langle\operatorname{Hess}_M y, K\rangle$.) The partials compare anchors, and no anchor is flat, so we test the condition with a counterfactual local second-order surrogate of the readout. Writing $K = K_S - (w\!\cdot\!\hat x)g$ with $K_S = \langle w_S,\mathrm{II}^S\rangle$ the in-sphere shape term, each anchor's 2{,}048 neighbours are scored by $\hat y_t(u) = c + a^{\top}u + \tfrac12 K_{\mathrm{sph}}(u,u) + \tfrac t2 K_S(u,u)$ with $a$ the readout's tangent gradient, $K_S$ from the decoder and $c$ refit: $t=1$ is the manifold's bending as seen in the readout's normal direction (``bending toward the label'' means $\langle R, K_S\rangle > 0$ for the remaining label curvature $R = \operatorname{Hess}_M y - K_{\mathrm{sph}}$), $t=-1$ its sign reversal, $t=0$ shape-flat. In the Gaussian model $t=1$ beats shape-flat iff $2\langle R,K_S\rangle > \norm{K_S}^2$; on the actual neighbours, with $r_0$ the centred shape-flat residual and $q_c$ the centred quadratic $\tfrac12 K_S(u_i,u_i)$, the exact criterion is $2\langle r_0,q_c\rangle > \norm{q_c}^2$ with optimum $t^{*} = \langle r_0,q_c\rangle/\norm{q_c}^2$ (Appendix~E). On five encoders and all four labels, $t=1$ beats shape-flat at 79--99\% of anchors and $t=-1$ is worse at 97--100\%, while a random orientation with the same centred quadratic amplitude shows no such asymmetry and is typically harmful under either sign ($t=1$ beats shape-flat at 22--36\% of anchors, $t^{*}\approx 0$; Appendix~E). The probe-selected shape quadratic is locally error-reducing; under the second-order model that is the signature of bending toward the remaining label curvature. This within-anchor question differs from Table~\ref{tab:real}'s cross-anchor one (does variation in mismatch explain variation in local $R^2$) and can hold where that has no signal, as for stellar mass. $t^{*}$ sits at 1.5 to 3.6 throughout, consistent with the globally ridge-regularized probe under-using a locally beneficial second-order term.

\paragraph{Across encoders.} The quadratic-chart estimator's mean-bending trace at the same anchors of five encoders (the readout set's supervised ViT-B, DINOv3 ViT-B/16, CLIP-B, ConvNeXt-B, ViT-L; $d=16$) has density-controlled associations with the magnitude probe's local $R^2$ of both signs ($-0.24$ to $+0.27$), as Eq.~\eqref{eq:hess} allows. The decoder test repeated per encoder (Appendix~D) is encoder-stable where the expansion says it should be: the mismatch partial is negative for magnitude and redshift on all five encoders ($-0.32$ to $-0.61$; cross-fitted $-0.28$ to $-0.62$) and the alignment positive for magnitude on all five ($+0.13$ to $+0.53$) and for redshift on four; the shape term's sign varies with encoder as well as label; stellar mass stays null but for one mismatch cell ($-0.12$).

\section{Discussion}

A linear readout of a galaxy embedding gains accuracy where the manifold, seen in the readout's normal direction, bends toward the label's curvature and loses it where that bending is reversed, at nearly every anchor and on five encoders. The residual expansion names the quantity, $\langle w_N,\mathrm{II}\rangle$ against $\operatorname{Hess}_M y$, and a decoder's pointwise second fundamental form measures it at production scale, validated against analytic truth for $\Htan$ and in substantial agreement with the data for the contracted Hessian. For a linear alignment map, the mechanism recent accounts of alignment assume~\citep{bangachev2026linear}, the relevant second-order extrinsic normal term is $\mathrm{II}_G - P_N^G A\,\mathrm{II}_F$, not one embedding's mean curvature; the full residual also carries the connection mismatch; our pilot (Appendix~C) finds a 44\% first-order tangent-frame residual and no estimator-stable second-order association. On this survey pair, single-model mean curvature has no robust density-controlled association with cross-survey convergence.

\emph{Limitations.} One survey pair. The label's Hessian is a local estimate ($R^2$ gain over linear 0.10--0.17); its norm is reliably ranked (split-half $\rho$ 0.91--0.94) but its direction only moderately (tensor cosine 0.19--0.35). On a synthetic surface with known curvature the mismatch partial reproduces ($-0.14$ to $-0.88$) but the alignment partial has no stable sign; the intervention of Appendix~E carries the alignment claim. Mismatch and alignment are the primary hypotheses, the rest exploratory. Permutation $p$-values assume anchor exchangeability under overlapping patches.

\section*{Appendix E: intervening on the readout's normal component}
The partials of Table~\ref{tab:real} compare anchors with one another and cannot say whether reversing the probe-facing shape term hurts relative to removing it, since no anchor is flat. Here we score a counterfactual local second-order surrogate of the readout at a fixed manifold. At each anchor the fitted ridge weight is split into tangent, radial and in-sphere normal parts $w = w_T + w_{\mathrm{rad}} + w_S$; we score the anchor's 2{,}048 neighbours by the data-side tangent-plus-sphere readout $(w_T + w_{\mathrm{rad}})\cdot x$ plus $t\,q$ with the local intercept refit, where $q = \tfrac12\langle w_S,\mathrm{II}^S\rangle(u,u)$ is the decoder's in-sphere second-order term in the anchor's chart coordinates: $t=1$ is the manifold's bending as seen in the readout's normal direction, $t=-1$ its sign reversal, $t=0$ shape-flat (the sphere term $-(w\!\cdot\!\hat x)g$ is kept in the base). Because the intercept is refit for every $t$, the local sum of squares is exactly $\mathrm{SSE}(t) = \norm{r_0 - t\,q_c}^2$ with $r_0$ the centred residual of the shape-flat predictor and $q_c = q - \bar q\mathbf 1$ the centred quadratic on the anchor's neighbours; hence $t^{*} = \langle r_0,q_c\rangle/\norm{q_c}^2$ and $t=1$ beats shape-flat iff $2\langle r_0,q_c\rangle > \norm{q_c}^2$. This is the finite-sample counterpart of the tensor-level condition $2\langle R,K_S\rangle_F > \norm{K_S}_F^2$ of Section~5, not the same quantity: the tensor version needs the estimated label Hessian, the empirical one does not. Columns: fraction of anchors where $t=1$ beats shape-flat (help) and where $t=-1$ is worse than shape-flat (hurt), the median change in local $R^2$ at $t=\pm1$, the median $t^{*}$; then the same for a random in-sphere normal direction $v$ whose quadratic is rescaled to the same centred amplitude on the actual neighbours, $\norm{q_{v,c}} = \norm{q_c}$ (matching $\norm{v}$ to $\norm{w_S}$ instead leaves the contracted tensor at a fraction of the fitted one, since $\mathrm{II}^S$ spans at most $d(d+1)/2$ of the $\sim 750$ normal directions; matching $\norm{\langle v,\mathrm{II}^S\rangle}_g$ gives the same picture; Supplement 12). The anchors' neighbourhoods overlap (each point lies in about twelve of them), so the fractions are not counts of independent trials; a maximal set of anchors whose pairwise overlap is at most 5\% of $k$ has 19--21 members per run, on which $t=1$ beats shape-flat at 81--100\% (one-sided sign test $p \le 10^{-3}$ in every cell) and $t=-1$ is worse at 95--100\% ($p \le 10^{-5}$).
