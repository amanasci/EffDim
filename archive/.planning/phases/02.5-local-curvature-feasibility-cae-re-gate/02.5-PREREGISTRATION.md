# Phase 02.5 Stage 1 -- Pre-Registration

**Written after `notebooks/02.5_swiss_roll_curvature_probe_check.ipynb` (02.5-05) and
before any PU-regime or graph-of-function sweep cell has run.** Fixes the gating
statistic (including the per-point-vs-region question 02.5-05 deferred here), its
null-calibration protocol, every numeric constant, the fixture set, the sweep grid, and
the halt-on-wrong-side consequence -- mirroring `02.2-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s shape and
`02.4-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s pre-register-then-prove-ancestry pattern. A feasibility
boundary measured without a prior rule is retry-until-pass wearing a different name.

## 1. Trigger

Stage 1 is a feasibility probe only: does a local mean-curvature field, estimated by
`curvature_probe.centroid_mean_curvature`, recover a usable ordering of `||H||` at
sampling densities and dimensions approaching the PU regime (`d=20`, `D=768`,
`n=10,000`)? This document gates stage 1 alone. Stage 2 (the CAE-versus-raw-points
head-to-head, D-09) has its own separate pre-registration,
`02.5-PREREGISTRATION-STAGE2.md`, written at plan `02.5-10` -- **a stage-1 NO-GO means
stage 2 never runs, and is not merely deferred.** Nothing here pre-registers, gates, or
constrains any stage-2 quantity (`chart_vs_raw_margin`, `seed_spread`).

D-01, D-02, and D-05 are rated one-way in `02.5-CONTEXT.md` for exactly this reason: once
this document is committed and its ancestry proved, changing which statistic gates, how
its threshold is calibrated, or which estimator gates is post-hoc tuning -- the
garden-of-forking-paths move this milestone's pre-registration discipline exists to
prevent.

## 2. The correction this phase is built on (D-00)

The ROADMAP re-scope calls the local quadratic fit "badly underdetermined": a full
second-fundamental-form fit needs `d(d+1)/2` coefficients per normal direction -- 171 at
`d=18`, 210 at `d=20`, 325 at `d=25` -- against `k*=15` neighbours. That count is real,
but it is the cost of the **full second fundamental form**, `II`. Mean curvature `H` is
only `II`'s *trace*, and the trace is a single scalar per normal direction, not `d(d+1)/2`
of them.

The identity: for `u` uniform in a `d`-ball of radius `r`, `E[u] = 0` and
`E[u_i u_j] = (r^2/(d+2)) delta_ij`, so `E[(1/2) II(u,u)] = (r^2/(2(d+2))) * tr(II)`. The
centroid displacement of a neighbourhood from its centre point, projected onto the normal
space, estimates `tr(II) = H` as **an average over the `k` neighbour vectors themselves**
-- one unknown, `k` samples -- not `d(d+1)/2` unknowns from `k` equations. This is
`02.5-RESEARCH.md`'s Architecture Patterns Pattern 1 derivation, and it is what motivates
attempting this phase's estimator at all: **the `d(d+1)/2`-vs-`k` underdetermination the
ROADMAP re-scope warns about does not bind on the trace-only estimator this phase gates
on.** It binds on the alternative, non-gating local quadric ("bowl") fit
(`curvature_probe.quadric_mean_curvature`), which *does* attempt to recover the full `II`
and *is* underdetermined at `d=20, k=15` (`n_coefficients=210 > k`) -- that contrast is
itself part of D-00's evidence and is reported, non-gating, in every sweep cell.

Two risks remain and *do* bind, both handled elsewhere in this document: `O(r^2)` bias at
finite radius (Section 5, Section 12(c)), and non-uniform-density contamination of the
centroid (Section 6).

**Convention, stated once, binding throughout this document:** every quantity named `H`
or `||H||` in this document, in `curvature_probe.py`, and in every fixture is the
**unnormalized trace**, `H = tr(II)` (`curvature_probe.CURVATURE_CONVENTION = "trace"`),
never the averaged `H/d`. `02.5-RESEARCH.md`'s own `swiss_roll_analytic_H` code sample
used the averaged convention; this phase corrected that during plan `02.5-01`. Spearman
rank correlation is invariant to this factor-of-`d` rescaling and was never at risk from
it, but the non-gating `median_relative_error` and `estimator_agreement` figures reported
throughout this document and every downstream sweep cell would be wrong by exactly a
factor of `d` under the wrong convention.

## 3. Gating statistic and threshold (D-01, D-02)

### 3a. The question 02.5-05 deferred here, stated precisely

`02.5-05-SUMMARY.md`'s forward note is explicit that this document, not that one, is
where the following gets proposed and ratified: **should stage 1's gate be evaluated at
the per-point scale, or at the region scale Phase 4's own quantile partitioning actually
consumes?**

This is a downstream-consumption question, not a response to the Swiss roll missing its
own 0.90 sanity bar. The user's own framing, verbatim: *"we care most about the recovery
of the original. maybe spearman isnt right here. we only care about curvature on the low
d manifold -- the order with respect to the 768 dim shouldn't matter."* Phase 4 consumes
the estimated `|H|` field by **partitioning points into quantile bins** and asking
questions at the level of those bins -- it never asks "is point 4,417 ranked correctly
relative to point 9,003." A gate measured at the wrong scale could fail a genuinely useful
estimator, or pass one that is useless at the scale that matters. This section resolves
the question as a proposal for Task 2's ratification, not as a decision made here.

### 3b. Evidence supplied by 02.5-05, disclosed in full

Measured on the Swiss roll (`k=30, d=2, random_state=20260807`), reproduced verbatim from
`02.5-05-SUMMARY.md` (see also Section 13):

```
per-point Spearman                 0.6712
per-point median relative error    0.1493
median ratio h_est/h_true          0.8934  (IQR 0.2838)  -- ~11% near-uniform scale bias
Pearson r on log h                 0.4563  (R^2 0.3828)
h_true dynamic range                3.05x  (h_true is a function of arclength t ALONE)
region scale (20-band medians)      Spearman 0.8406, median relative error 0.1092
boundary trim (10% width, 5% arc)   rho 0.671 -> 0.804, median relative error 0.149 -> 0.128
```

10 arclength-percentile bands, `n=300` each. Because `h_true` is constant along the
roll's width, within-band spread is pure estimator noise, not signal:

```
band bias (h_est/h_true)   0.829 first / ~0.87-0.93 interior / 0.853 last  -- FLAT, not a regional swing
band noise CV               0.510 first / 0.20-0.26 interior / 0.423 last  -- free edges ~2x interior
```

Noise-oracle ceiling (perturbing the analytic truth by unbiased multiplicative noise,
then ranking) -- what Spearman is *attainable* at a given per-point noise level, the
instrument that stops an unattainable bar being pre-registered:

```
5% noise  -> rho 0.986
10% noise -> rho 0.950
14.9% noise -> rho 0.897
20% noise -> rho 0.831
30% noise -> rho 0.698
```

### 3c. Interpretation carried forward from 02.5-05

- The ~11% scale bias is a **near-uniform** underestimate -- expected `O(r^2)`
  finite-radius bias (Section 5) -- and is a pure multiplicative scale factor. A pure
  scale factor does not perturb ordering at all.
- The damage to per-point Spearman is **noise** (~20-26% CV interior, ~2x that at the two
  free arclength edges) acting on a field spanning only 3.05x. Per the oracle table, 20%
  noise caps rho at 0.831 even with **zero** bias -- the per-point statistic is measuring
  a real noise-floor limitation, not primarily a bias artifact.
- `median_relative_error = 0.1493` **flatters** the estimator: it reports 0.1493 where the
  real per-point noise is ~20-26% CV, because a median hides the tail. It must not anchor
  a gate on its own (it is already non-gating per D-01's original scope, and stays so
  here).
- Phase 4's consumption is quantile partitioning -- a region-scale operation. This weighs
  toward evaluating the gate at the scale Phase 4 actually uses.
- `d=2, k=30` is the *easy* case: the ambient tangent-basis estimate gets substantially
  harder as `d` grows toward the PU regime's `d=20` (Section 7). Any threshold calibrated
  only against this Swiss roll number should not be read as the real-data feasibility
  boundary -- the true boundary is likely **tighter** than this evidence implies (restated
  in Section 12(a)).

### 3d. Why 02.5-05's region number (0.8406) does not calibrate this gate

02.5-05's region-scale diagnostic binned points by **arclength-percentile** (bands of the
generator's own parameter `t`) -- an oracle grouping available only because `t` is known
for a synthetic fixture. **Phase 4 has no such oracle on real PU data**: it partitions by
quantiles of the **estimated** `|H|` field, the only field it ever has. A region-scale
gate that faithfully matches Phase 4's actual operation must therefore bin by
**estimated**-`|H|` quantile, not by a true-parameter oracle. That is a different, and not
yet measured, statistic from the 0.8406 figure above. This distinction is stated
explicitly so the record never conflates "region aggregation helps" (shown by the
arclength-band diagnostic) with "0.8406 calibrates a real-data-faithful region gate" (not
shown, and not used anywhere in this document as a calibration input) -- exactly the
line Section 12's prohibition on choosing constants from a measurement they would gate
protects.

### 3e. Two named candidate statistics, both defined precisely

**`spearman_rho_pointwise`** -- the statistic D-01 originally scoped, unchanged:
`curvature_probe.spearman_gate_statistic(h_est_norm, h_true_norm)`, Spearman rank
correlation over all `n` points.

**`quantile_bin_concordance` (region-scale)** -- superseding an earlier, rejected design
(see Section 3f.i for why). Precisely:

```
def quantile_bin_concordance(h_est_norm, h_true_norm, n_bins, pair_seed, n_pairs):
    bin_true = quantile_bin(h_true_norm, n_bins)      # each point's TRUE quantile bin,
                                                        # n_bins equal-count bins
    rng = default_rng(pair_seed)
    i = rng.integers(0, n, size=n_pairs)               # a fixed random sample of point
    j = rng.integers(0, n, size=n_pairs)               # pairs -- same pair-sampling
                                                        # discipline as 02.1/02.2's
                                                        # draw_geo_pairs (PAIR_SEED,
                                                        # PAIR_COUNT convention)
    keep = bin_true[i] != bin_true[j]                  # keep only CROSS-bin pairs --
                                                        # within-bin order is exactly what
                                                        # Phase 4 never distinguishes
    i, j = i[keep], j[keep]
    concordant = sign(h_true[i]-h_true[j]) == sign(h_est_norm[i]-h_est_norm[j])
    return 2 * mean(concordant) - 1                    # rescaled to Spearman-like [-1,1];
                                                        # 0 under the null, 1 if every
                                                        # cross-bin pair is ordered right
```

This asks a genuinely different question from per-point Spearman: **for a pair of points
that truly belong to different quantile buckets -- exactly the buckets Phase 4 will
compute -- does the estimate correctly identify which one has more curvature?**
Within-bin pairs are dropped entirely, because Phase 4 never asks about them.

`bin_true` -- not `bin_est` -- defines which pairs are "cross-bin," because the question
is about the buckets that genuinely differ, and using `bin_true` keeps the statistic's
denominator (which pairs count) independent of the estimator being scored, so a bad
estimator cannot narrow its own evaluation set by mis-binning.

This is **not yet implemented** in `curvature_probe.py` (this plan does not touch it, per
its prohibitions). Ratifying `quantile_bin_concordance` as any part of the gate obliges
plan 02.5-07 to add a TESTED module function before its sweep runs -- notebook-inline
numpy, as this section's own characterization work used, is explicitly insufficient for a
gating statistic (Section 9's forward dependency).

### 3f. What was actually tried, and why this design survived where four others did not

Per the redraft requirement that a saturating design be rejected and another tried, five
candidates were built and characterized end-to-end against `curvature_probe`'s real
functions, on both the Swiss roll and the phase's own graph-of-function `BASE_CELL`
fixture (`d=20, D=768, n=10000, n_bumps=8, k=30, density_correct=True, k_density=30`,
seed `20260807`) -- never on the orchestrator's own scratch numbers (R6):

i. **Bin-median contrast, linear and log scale** (achieved top-vs-bottom-quartile
   true-median contrast, over oracle top-vs-bottom-quartile contrast). **Rejected under
   R2**: on `BASE_CELL`, both variants are pinned at essentially `1.0000` (std `0.0000`)
   from 5% up to 20-30% injected per-point noise, and still above `0.93` (log) /
   `0.98` (linear) at 80% noise -- true saturation, no usable dynamic range in the
   regime that matters, caused by `BASE_CELL`'s own true-curvature field spanning **~4700x**
   between its median and maximum (`h_true.max()/median(h_true) = 4708.8`), against the
   Swiss roll's 3.05x. Proportional noise essentially cannot invert order across that
   much separation.
ii. **Bin-trend Spearman** (`spearmanr` of the estimated bin label against the *full*
   per-point true field, bins never collapsed to medians). **Rejected as not actually a
   different statistic**: at every bin count tested (4, 6, 10, 20) it landed within
   `0.002` of raw per-point Spearman on both fixtures (e.g. `0.671` vs per-point `0.671`
   at `n_bins=20` on the Swiss roll) -- coarsening only the estimate side while keeping
   the true side at full resolution barely perturbs a rank correlation, so this design
   would not have resolved Decision 1's actual question.
iii. **Double-binned Spearman** (both sides quantile-discretized into the same bins,
   `spearmanr` of the two bin-label sequences). **Rejected for the same reason as (ii)**:
   converges to per-point Spearman at every bin count tested (`0.51`-`0.52` vs per-point
   `0.5205` on the graph fixture).
iv. **`quantile_bin_concordance`** (this section) -- the one design that both (a) diverges
   materially from raw per-point Spearman (Swiss roll: `0.647` vs per-point `0.671`;
   `BASE_CELL`: `0.448` vs per-point `0.521` -- a real, different number, not a
   restatement) and (b) shows genuine, monotone, non-saturating dynamic range on the
   **Swiss roll** across the full 5%-80% noise band (Section 3g's ceiling table). It is
   the proposed candidate.

**Stated honestly, not hidden: `quantile_bin_concordance` also saturates on `BASE_CELL`
under the same noise-oracle protocol** (5% noise -> `0.998`, 80% noise -> `0.896`, all
four bin counts and both bin-collapse strategies show the identical qualitative pattern).
This is not a defect specific to this one design -- **every candidate tried saturates on
`BASE_CELL` under i.i.d. proportional noise**, because that saturation is a property of
`BASE_CELL`'s own heavy-tailed true-curvature distribution interacting with *any*
order-based statistic, not a property of which order-based statistic is chosen. The real
estimator's measured value on `BASE_CELL` (`0.448`) sits **below the entire tested
noise-ceiling range up to 80% CV** -- meaning the real centroid estimator's actual errors
on this harder, heavier-tailed fixture are not shaped like i.i.d. proportional noise on
the true value at all. This is exactly the kind of load-bearing finding Section 12(a)
already warned about in general terms (`d=20` is harder than the Swiss roll's `d=2`) --
here it is now measured, specific, and carried forward as its own limitation (Section
12(g)).

**Consequence for calibration, stated plainly:** the noise-oracle-ceiling technique used
to derive `SPEARMAN_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR` cannot be run on `BASE_CELL` for this statistic and
produce a meaningful floor -- it would be reading off a saturated curve. Section 3g
therefore derives the region floor from the **same fixture and protocol** that
`SPEARMAN_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR` was itself derived from (the Swiss roll), states this
explicitly rather than silently reusing a different fixture than the one the per-point
floor used, and names which term of the threshold formula is actually load-bearing at
`d=20` as a result.

### 3g. Threshold calibration -- derived, not invented, honest about where it is weak

For **either** statistic, the threshold is
`THRESH = max(null_threshold, ABSOLUTE_FLOOR)`, with `null_threshold` the `NULL_QUANTILE`
quantile of a permutation null. Propose:

```
NULL_QUANTILE           = 0.99
N_PERMUTATIONS          = 999
SPEARMAN_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR = 0.50
REGION_N_BINS           = 4
REGION_PAIR_COUNT       = 200_000
REGION_PAIR_SEED        = 20260731
REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR   = 0.4750
```

`NULL_QUANTILE = 0.99` and `N_PERMUTATIONS = 999`: unchanged from the original proposal,
identical at both scales -- dozens of sweep cells are scored per statistic and a
95th-percentile bar would clear on chance in roughly one in twenty of them; 999 resamples
resolve a 99th percentile stably inside the wall-clock budget (Section 9).

`SPEARMAN_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR = 0.50`, ratified at Task 2 (Decision 2) **in full knowledge of
what it tolerates**: the unbiased-oracle per-point Spearman ceiling crosses `0.50` at
approximately **50% per-point noise** (`30%->0.703, 40%->0.585, 45%->0.535, 50%->0.496`),
and the Swiss roll's own observed `0.6712` clears it. This tolerance was accepted
knowingly and is **not** silently tightened here.

`REGION_N_BINS = 4` (quartiles): the simplest, most literal reading of "quantile
partitioning" (Phase 4's own operation), and the only bin count tested for
`quantile_bin_concordance` -- fixed, not swept as a gating dial, per R5. A non-gating
sensitivity sweep over `{3, 4, 5, 6, 10}` is reported (Section 9) so this choice is not
trusted blind, but `4` is what gates.

`REGION_PAIR_COUNT = 200_000`, `REGION_PAIR_SEED = 20260731`: the same pair-sampling
constant and seed 02.1/02.2 already use for `draw_geo_pairs`, reused here for continuity
of convention, not because the number itself carries any special meaning for this
statistic -- any fixed, disclosed seed and a pair count large enough for stable medians
would serve equally.

**`REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR = 0.4750` -- derivation, stated in full.** Mapping the *same*
~50% per-point-noise tolerance Decision 2 ratified through `quantile_bin_concordance`'s
own ceiling table, measured on the **Swiss roll** (the same fixture and noise-injection
protocol `SPEARMAN_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR` was derived from -- Section 3f explains why `BASE_CELL`
cannot be used for this specific derivation):

```
noise_cv=0.05 -> ceiling 0.9804
noise_cv=0.10 -> ceiling 0.9259
noise_cv=0.20 -> ceiling 0.7813
noise_cv=0.30 -> ceiling 0.6514
noise_cv=0.40 -> ceiling 0.5501
noise_cv=0.50 -> ceiling 0.4750   <- REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR
noise_cv=0.80 -> ceiling 0.3440
null (shuffled): mean=0.0007, std=0.0159, p95=0.0258, p99=0.0370
```

`0.4750` is read directly off the `50%`-noise row -- not chosen because any measured
estimator clears it. It is **not** derived from, or checked against, the `BASE_CELL`
measurement (`0.448`) taken during this characterization; that number is reported next
purely as disclosure, per Section 3's own prohibition on choosing a threshold because the
data would pass it.

**Disclosed, not hidden: under this floor, the `BASE_CELL` graph-of-function measurement
taken during this characterization (`quantile_bin_concordance = 0.4483`) sits *below*
`REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR = 0.4750`** -- unlike the Swiss roll's own value (`0.6472`), which
clears it. Both facts are reported together, deliberately, following
`02.4-PREREGISTRATION.md` §8's own precedent of stating a baseline win and an absolute miss
together, neither cancelling the other. **What this means
for `d=20`:** the floor term of `THRESH_REGION` is calibrated on the *easier* fixture and
its meaning weakens at the harder one, precisely restating Section 12(a)'s general warning
in specific, measured form. The `null_threshold` term, by contrast, stays tight and
well-behaved on **both** fixtures regardless of the heavy-tail issue (`BASE_CELL`'s own
null: mean `-0.0001`, std `0.0106`, p95 `0.0178`, p99 `0.0241` -- essentially identical in
scale to the Swiss roll's null above) -- so at `d=20`, **the null term, not the floor, is
what is actually doing the discriminating work**, and this asymmetry is recorded here so
Task 2 rules on it with full information rather than a document that quietly hopes the
floor means the same thing at both dimensions.

**Scale-free check (R4), confirmed:** `quantile_bin_concordance(5.37 * h_est_norm,
h_true_norm)` reproduces the unscaled value to the sixth decimal place on both fixtures --
bin assignment depends only on rank, which is invariant to any positive monotonic
rescaling of the estimate, so the ~11% finite-radius scale bias (Section 2) cannot move
this statistic.

**Scope, flagged explicitly (per the redraft's required choice):** this document takes the
**preferred** option -- `quantile_bin_concordance` is specified here by exact pseudocode
and its ceiling/null/scale-invariance numbers are recorded as **acceptance criteria plan
02.5-07's implementation must reproduce** (to float64-reasonable tolerance, on the same
fixtures and seeds), rather than adding the function to `curvature_probe.py` in this plan.
This keeps this plan's `files_modified` scope (`02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` alone) intact.
The alternative -- implementing and testing it now -- was not taken, and is not silently
smuggled in: `git status --porcelain notebooks/` stays empty throughout this plan
(Section 3 prohibitions, Task 3's acceptance criteria).

### 3h. RATIFIED (Task 2, Decision 1): option-scale-C -- both statistics gate independently

Neither floor is changed from Section 3g's derivation. A cell **PASSES iff both** of the
following hold, each against its own null-calibrated threshold and its own
independently-derived absolute floor:

```
spearman_rho_pointwise   : clears_null(0.99, 999)  AND  >= SPEARMAN_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR (0.50)
quantile_bin_concordance : clears_null(0.99, 999)  AND  >= REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR   (0.4750)
```

Both statistics are gating (neither is demoted to non-gating context); both are still
always computed and reported at every cell regardless (Section 9), so nothing about *what
is measured* changes -- only *which measurements decide PASS/FAIL* is now "both," not
"one, with the other as context."

**The user's stated rationale for this choice, recorded verbatim in substance because it
is load-bearing for how a later reader should interpret a stage-1 result:** with the
estimator likely infeasible at the PU regime (Section 13b's disclosure below), the gate's
job at this point is **attribution rather than selection** -- distinguishing *why* an
estimate is bad, not merely picking the single best available summary of "how bad." The
**divergence** between the two statistics is itself diagnostic: `quantile_bin_concordance`
sitting at `0.448` on `BASE_CELL`, *below even an 80%-noise oracle's `0.896`* (Section
3f), is precisely what identifies the estimator's error there as **structural**, not
merely **noise-shaped** -- a distinction a single gating statistic could not make on its
own. Requiring both to clear also sets a deliberately high bar before Phase 3/4 is built
on this estimator, which is the appropriate posture given the disclosure below.

## 4. Fixtures (D-03)

The Swiss roll is the mandatory `d=2` anchor (`curvature_probe.make_swiss_roll_fixture`),
never substituted, per CLAUDE.md. The graph-of-function family
(`make_graph_of_function_fixture`) extends the probe to arbitrary `(d, D, codimension)`
with analytic `H` from `f`'s exact Hessian. Neither alternative works alone: a Swiss roll
is intrinsically 2-dimensional and cannot reach `d=18-25`; a `d`-sphere has constant `H`,
so Spearman (at either scale) is undefined on it. The graph construction gives spatially
varying analytic ground truth at arbitrary `(d, D)`.

Concrete `f`: `n_bumps` Gaussian bumps `A_j exp(-||x - c_j||^2 / (2*sigma^2))`, with
`sigma=0.6`, `amplitude=1.0`, `domain_radius=2.0`, mixed signs, fixed-seed centres.
`n_bumps` is swept at `1` and `8` so the low-codimension fixture's boundary is not trusted
to transfer to the real codimension-748 regime unchecked -- this resolves
`02.5-RESEARCH.md` Open Question 2 in the affirmative, and the resolution is recorded here
rather than left implicit. `make_flat_fixture` is the D-06 proof bed.

## 5. Estimator (D-05)

`curvature_probe.centroid_mean_curvature` gates. `curvature_probe.quadric_mean_curvature`
runs alongside and its agreement (`estimator_agreement`) is reported non-gating at every
cell, for both statistics.

Three caveats D-05 requires stated: (1) bias grows like `r^2` at finite radius -- the
identity Section 2 derives is exact only as the neighbourhood radius vanishes; (2)
sensitivity to non-uniform sampling density (Section 6); (3) yields `H` and `||H||` but
never the full `II` -- costs nothing here, since Phase 4 consumes only `|H|` quantiles.

Rejected alternative: quadric-only with ridge or low-rank regularization, rejected
because the regularization strength would become a dial pre-registered blind, with no
principled way to set it. Estimator disagreement (`agreement_spearman`,
`agreement_median_rel_diff`) is **reported, never blocking**: the two estimators have
different bias mechanisms and are expected to diverge at the underdetermined end of the
`d` ladder, so a blocking rule would convert an expected, informative divergence into a
spurious failure.

## 6. Density correction (D-06)

Inverse-density weighting of each neighbourhood by `w_i = 1/rho_i`, `rho_i` the standard
k-NN density estimate at radius `r_{k_density}(i)`, weights normalized to mean 1,
implemented by `curvature_probe.local_density_weights` and switched on by
`centroid_mean_curvature(..., density_correct=True, k_density=K_DENSITY)`. Propose
`K_DENSITY = 30`. This is the correction's **only** constant; there is deliberately no
continuous regularization strength.

Proof obligation and how it is met: `02.5-02`'s investigation proved (not merely
observed) that an exactly-flat, linearly-embedded fixture cannot exhibit density-induced
fake `||H||` at all -- an exactly rank-`d` cloud's SVD-recovered tangent basis is
weighting-invariant, so the normal projection annihilates any weighted centroid
displacement exactly, regardless of density skew. This amends `02.5-CONTEXT.md`'s
originally-imagined must-have (a flat fixture showing large uncorrected `||H||` that the
correction removes) -- that scenario is mathematically impossible for the shipped,
normal-projecting estimator. Density bias leaks only through an **imperfectly estimated**
tangent basis, which requires genuine curvature to exist at all; on a genuinely curved,
strongly-skewed fixture the correction measurably (though modestly, ~8-10% relative)
reduces median relative error. Both `density_correct=False` and `density_correct=True`
are run at every sweep cell so the correction's effect is visible everywhere rather than
assumed. A Swiss roll is sampled evenly by construction and would never catch a density
artifact; foundation-model embeddings cluster, making this a live risk.

## 8. Neighbourhood rule (D-08)

Propose `K_GATING = 30`. Bias-variance justification: the centroid estimator averages one
unknown over `k` vectors, so its variance falls like `1/sqrt(k)` while its bias grows like
`r^2` and `r` grows with `k`; at `n=10,000` in `d~=20` the neighbourhood radius at `k=30`
is still small relative to the manifold's curvature scale, and 30 vectors is twice the
averaging `k=15` provides.

`k*=15` is **not** inherited: Phase 1 froze it by a pre-registered plateau rule targeting
Isomap graph connectivity and stability -- a different objective from curvature
bias-variance on the same number, and nobody has checked whether the same value is right
for both. Non-gating `k` sweep: `{10, 15, 20, 30, 50, 100}`, reported sensitivity. This
document emits no recommended k for downstream use -- naming one would read as post-hoc
licence to change `k*`; `K_GATING` governs only this phase's own estimator.

## 12. Limitations, stated before the measurement

(a) **The Swiss roll evidence is the easy case.** `d=2, k=30` well-determines the local
tangent basis; the tangent-basis estimate gets substantially harder as `d` grows toward
`d=20`, so the true feasibility boundary is likely tighter than the Swiss roll's
per-point-`rho=0.6712` / region-`rho=0.8406`(-on-an-oracle-binning) evidence implies. A
Swiss-roll-calibrated intuition must not be read as a real-data threshold.

(b) The graph-of-function family is a synthetic proxy for the PU manifold, and its
curvature scale is chosen, not measured, so the boundary locates where the *estimator*
breaks, not necessarily where the *data* is.

(c) The padded ambient directions in the graph-of-function fixture are exactly flat, so
even the `n_bumps=8` variant understates how a real codimension-748 manifold's normal
space behaves.

(d) The analytic ground truth and the estimator are both written by this phase, so a
shared conceptual error would be invisible to either Spearman statistic -- mitigated but
not eliminated by the finite-difference cross-checks in `test_curvature_probe.py`.

(e) `torch` 2.13.0+cpu is carried forward from prior-phase history and was not
re-verified during research (`02.5-RESEARCH.md` Assumptions Log A5), though stage 1 does
not use it.

(f) `quantile_bin_concordance`'s power depends on `REGION_N_BINS` and on `REGION_PAIR_COUNT`
relative to `n`; at small `n` (the sweep's `n=1000` cell), the number of *cross*-true-bin
pairs among a fixed `200_000`-pair draw is bounded above by `n^2`, not by
`REGION_PAIR_COUNT` itself, so that cell's own null (Section 3g) will be correspondingly
different from `BASE_CELL`'s -- reported, not hidden, as part of that cell's own
calibration.

(g) **Measured, not merely anticipated: the noise-oracle-ceiling calibration technique
saturates on the graph-of-function family's own `BASE_CELL` fixture, for every
region-scale statistic design tried (Section 3f).** `BASE_CELL`'s true-curvature field has
a max/median ratio of `4708.8x` (against the Swiss roll's `3.05x`), which makes ordering at
the extremes essentially indestructible under i.i.d. proportional per-point noise up to at
least 80% CV -- a property of this fixture's own heavy tail, not of any one candidate
statistic. `REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR` is therefore derived from the Swiss roll only (Section
3g), and at `d=20` the `null_threshold` term, not the floor, is what actually
discriminates. This sits alongside, and sharpens, limitation (a)'s general warning that a
Swiss-roll-calibrated number should not be read as the real-data boundary.

## 13b. Mandatory disclosure -- a k-sensitivity measurement on the PU-regime graph fixture, in hand at ratification

Per D-11's principle (a checkpoint must record its own epistemic position) and this
document's own Section 3d/13 disclosure discipline, the following measurement was
performed by the orchestrator, on the phase's own graph-of-function fixture at the PU
regime's working dimension, **before** this pre-registration was committed, and is
reproduced here verbatim rather than omitted:

```
Fixture: make_graph_of_function_fixture(n=1500, d=20, D=768, n_bumps=8, seed=20260807)
```

**`n=1500` is NOT a sweep cell** -- the sweep's `n` axis (Section 9) is `{1000, 3000,
10000}`. This was a diagnostic run at a size deliberately smaller than `BASE_CELL`'s
`n=10000`, so its absolute values are **pessimistic** relative to `BASE_CELL` and must not
be read as a sweep result. True field dynamic range at this `n`: `max/median = 1037.5x`
(smaller than `BASE_CELL`'s own `4708.8x` at `n=10000`, consistent with a heavier tail
being sampled more fully at larger `n`).

```
   k     spearman_rho_pointwise    median_relative_error
  30            0.4419                    0.8947
  60            0.4549                    0.8713
 120            0.4211                    0.8813
 240            0.3442                    0.9006
 400            0.3385                    0.9047
```

**Findings, stated plainly:**

(a) **`rho` does not increase with `k`.** It peaks near `k=60` and *declines* to `0.339`
at `k=400` -- the bias-variance turn where the neighbourhood outgrows the local quadratic
regime the estimator's derivation (Section 2) assumes: `O(r^2)` bias grows with the
neighbourhood radius, and radius grows with `k`, so past some point more neighbours makes
the estimate *worse*, not better. Raising the `k` ceiling would not help. The existing
non-gating `k` sweep `{10, 15, 20, 30, 50, 100}` (Section 8) already brackets this peak;
no change to it is required. A peak-then-decline shape on the `k` axis is **expected**,
recorded here so plan 02.5-07 does not misread it as an implementation bug when it
reproduces the same shape at `BASE_CELL`'s own `n`.

(b) **Median relative error sits at `~0.87-0.90` across every tested `k`**, against the
Swiss roll's `0.1493` (Section 3b). At the PU regime, the estimator recovers curvature
*magnitude* barely at all. No choice of gating statistic changes this fact -- it is a
property of the estimator in this regime, not of which statistic is used to gate it
(consistent with Section 3f's finding that `BASE_CELL`'s own `median_relative_error` and
`quantile_bin_concordance` value both sit far outside what any tested gating design's
noise-oracle calibration anticipated).

(c) **Mechanism: the curse of dimensionality against the estimator's second-order
expansion.** At `d=20`, halving the neighbourhood radius costs `2^20` times more points to
maintain the same local sample density, so no `n` reachable within this phase's compute
budget makes the local quadratic approximation Section 2's derivation depends on accurate
at this working dimension. The sweep's `n` axis (`{1000, 3000, 10000}`, Section 9) can
demonstrate this trend as `n` grows, but cannot escape it -- the mechanism does not
resolve with more data at fixed `d`.

(d) **Stated explicitly, because this is where post-hoc pressure could otherwise enter
unnoticed:** this evidence was in hand at ratification, was disclosed in full above, and
**neither `SPEARMAN_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR`, `REGION_ABSOLUTE_FLOOR`, nor the choice of
option-scale-C (Section 3h) was adjusted to accommodate it.** Both floors are recorded
exactly as derived in Section 3g, from the Swiss roll's own noise-oracle ceiling tables,
before this diagnostic existed. The user ratified Decision 1 (option-scale-C) and Decision
2 (both floors as drafted) with this evidence already in view.

(e) **This diagnostic does not substitute for stage 1.** Plan 02.5-07's full
pre-registered sweep -- every axis in Section 9, both gating statistics, the complete
`D_PRIMARY` ladder and seed set -- remains the instrument that establishes the feasibility
boundary rigorously. This `n=1500` measurement anticipates that sweep's likely direction
(consistent with, and pessimistic relative to, `BASE_CELL`'s own `n=10000` characterization
in Section 3f) but is not a substitute for running it. Plan 02.5-07 must run the full sweep
regardless of what this diagnostic suggests.
