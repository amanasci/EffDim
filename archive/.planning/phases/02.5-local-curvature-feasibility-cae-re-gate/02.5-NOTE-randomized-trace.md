# Note — randomized-trace (Hutchinson) mean curvature, for plan 02.5-08

**Status:** accepted as a NON-GATING variant inside plan 02.5-08. Not a new plan, not a quick
task. Recorded 2026-08-08, before 02.5-08 has run.

**Origin:** user-supplied external analysis proposing that mean curvature through a decoder be
estimated by randomized directional second derivatives (a geometric Hutchinson trace estimator)
rather than by building the full second fundamental form.

## The proposal

For a decoder `F: R^d -> R^D` at latent `z`, with `J = DF(z)`, pullback metric `g = J^T J`, and
normal projector `P_N = I - J g^-1 J^T` applied via a `d x d` solve (never a `D x D` matrix):

    B(v,v) = P_N D^2F(z)[v,v]

Draw random directions and average. Two normalizations appear in the source material:

- `v = g^{-1/2} u`, `u` uniform on `S^{d-1}`: `E[u u^T] = I/d`, so the `1/d` is supplied
  IMPLICITLY and the estimator is `(1/K) sum_k P_N D^2F[v_k, v_k]`.
- `xi = g^{-1/2} eps`, `eps_i` Rademacher: `E[eps eps^T] = I`, so `E[xi xi^T] = g^-1` and the
  `1/d` must be written EXPLICITLY: `(1/(dK)) sum_k P_N D^2F[xi_k, xi_k]`.

Both are internally correct. **Mixing them costs a factor of `d`.**

## Three constraints this phase imposes on it

1. **CONVENTION — the load-bearing one.** The source analysis uses `H = (1/d) tr_g B`, the
   AVERAGED convention. This module is pinned to `CURVATURE_CONVENTION = "trace"`, `H = tr(II)`,
   with a regression guard (02.5-01 Task 2). Transcribing the source formulas verbatim
   introduces a **factor-of-`d` = 20 error** against every fixture, every 02.5 SUMMARY number,
   and the sealed stage-1 gate. Precedent: this codebase already shipped and then fixed exactly
   one factor-of-`d` scale bug (`2*(d+2)/r2` -> `2*d/r2`, 02.5-01). Under the trace convention
   the Laplace-Beltrami identity reads `Delta_g F = H`, not `Delta_g F = d H`.

2. **`K << d` barely holds at `d = 20`.** Exact `tr_g B` costs `d` directional second
   derivatives (eigenbasis of `g`), so exact = 20 HVPs against `K = 8` = 8 — a 2.5x saving
   bought with Monte-Carlo variance. The estimator's premise is `K << d`; at `d = 20` that is
   thin. It becomes decisive only at much larger `d`. **The exact path stays the gated one.**

3. **Smoothness is already satisfied.** The source flags that pure ReLU decoders have
   identically-zero second derivatives a.e. Not an issue here: `cae.py`'s `ACTIVATION` is
   `silu` (C2-smooth), and `activation_module` reaches `relu` only for the deliberate CAE-06
   ReLU control fit. 02.5-08 already plans a load-time guard that RAISES on such an activation
   rather than silently returning a zero second fundamental form.

## Why it is worth carrying anyway

Stage 1's runner crash exposed a hard structural floor on the POINT-CLOUD estimator:
`local_tangent_basis` requires `k > d`, so the sealed `k` axis `{10, 15, ...}` is not merely
inaccurate at `D_PRIMARY = 20` — it is mathematically impossible. The point-cloud arm is squeezed
between needing `k > d`, needing `k` large enough for statistics, and needing `k` small enough to
stay local.

**The decoder arm has no such constraint**, because it differentiates an analytic map instead of
sampling a neighbourhood. This is a sharper argument for Arm B than anything in the stage-1
numbers, and it is why stage 1's likely FAIL does not transfer to stage 2.

It also corrects a framing error made during 02.5-07: high AMBIENT dimension does not force a
curse of dimensionality for a decoder estimator. The curse-of-dimensionality finding recorded in
`02.5-PREREGISTRATION.md` Section 13b is about the point-cloud centroid estimator specifically —
it must sample a `d`-dimensional neighbourhood, and that genuinely does suffer. Different
estimators; both statements true. Do not carry 13b's conclusion across to Arm B.

## Addendum, 2026-08-09: two corrections after the stage-1 measurement

The source analysis was re-supplied unchanged. Two items were not in the original note.

### A. Antithetic directions give EXACTLY ZERO variance reduction here

The source suggests, if the estimator is noisy, to "use antithetic directions." For this
estimator that suggestion is not merely weak — it is void, and would silently halve the
effective probe count of anyone who followed it.

`B` is a symmetric BILINEAR form, so for any probe `v`:

    B(-v, -v) = (-1)(-1) B(v, v) = B(v, v)

The antithetic partner `-v` returns the **identical** value, not a negatively-correlated one.
Antithetic pairs are perfectly correlated at `+1`, so averaging `{v, -v}` has exactly the
variance of the single sample `v` while costing two directional second derivatives. Any
`K`-probe budget spent on antithetic pairing is halved for nothing.

Antithetic sampling reduces variance for estimators with an ODD-order dependence on the probe.
A quadratic form is even.

**Correction to this note's own first draft (2026-08-09):** that draft added "Hutch++-style
deflation is sound." **That was too casual and is retracted.** Hutch++ estimates the scalar
trace of a MATRIX. Here `II: T_zM x T_zM -> N_zM` is VECTOR-valued, so in codimension greater
than one there is no single scalar matrix whose trace it computes. Making Hutch++-style
deflation work would require applying it componentwise in a chosen normal basis, or to the
scalar shape operators of particular normal directions — and constructing a normal basis is
precisely what the `d x d` solve exists to avoid at `D = 768`. It is not plug-and-play here.
At `d = 20` this is a further reason to take the exact 20-direction trace. Probe reuse across
nearby points remains sound and is unaffected.

**02.5-08 must not implement antithetic probes.** If it wants variance reduction at fixed
cost, the honest lever at `d = 20` is to stop probing and take the exact `d`-direction trace,
which is only 20 Hessian-vector products.

### B. The real argument for the decoder arm is statistical, not computational

The source frames the decoder's advantage as **cost**: a trace instead of a `d(d+1)/2`
tensor, `K` probes instead of `d^2` second derivatives. At `d = 20` that framing is weak — the
exact trace is 20 HVPs against `K = 8`, a 2.5x saving on a computation that was never the
bottleneck.

Stage 1's measured mechanism gives a much stronger and completely different argument, and it
is the one 02.5-08 should carry (see `02.5-NOTE-high-d-curvature-approaches.md` Section 1):

    kNN ball radius / manifold radius, k=30, n=10_000:
      d = 2   ->  r/R = 0.115     (genuinely local; Swiss roll PASSES)
      d = 20  ->  r/R = 0.906     (the "neighbourhood" is 91% of the manifold)

    r/R ~ (k/n)^(1/d)  --  halving it at d=20 needs ~10^6 x more data

The point-cloud estimator failed because **no local neighbourhood exists** at `d = 20`, not
because a trace was computed inefficiently. The decoder arm's escape is that it forms no
neighbourhood at all: `r -> 0` is achieved analytically by autodiff, so `r/R` never enters its
error. That is a structural escape from the measured failure, and it holds for the EXACT trace
just as much as for the randomized one — which is further reason the randomized variant stays
non-gating. It is an optimization of something that is already affordable, on an arm whose
advantage lies elsewhere.

The source's own headline correction — "high ambient dimension alone does not force a curse of
dimensionality here" — is CONFIRMED by measurement, not merely argued: stage 1 returned
bit-identical gating values at `D = 28, 50, 200, 768`.

**Correction to this note's own first draft (2026-08-09):** that draft went on to say "ambient
dimension cost nothing," which OVERCLAIMS what the experiment shows and conflates two
different costs. Measured directly, `centroid_mean_curvature_both_densities` at
`d=20, n=4000, k=30`:

    D =  28   1.20 s   1.00x
    D =  50   1.41 s   1.18x
    D = 200   2.53 s   2.11x
    D = 768  10.75 s   8.98x     <- bit-identical gating values throughout

Ambient dimension cost **~9x in compute and exactly nothing in accuracy**. (The sweep
artifact's own `elapsed_s` column cannot show this: the `D=768` row IS the base cell, which
carries a 120 s quadric budget against every other cell's 2 s, so that comparison is
confounded. The numbers above are an unconfounded direct measurement.) The precise claim, and
the only one stage 1 demonstrates, is:

> **Ambient dimension created no statistical curse and did not alter the measured gating
> outcome.** It did cost wall-clock, roughly linearly in `D`.

The same distinction applies to a decoder `F: R^20 -> R^768`, whose output and derivative
vectors are length `D` and which will cost more memory and FLOPs than one emitting `R^28`.
Cheap statistically, not free computationally.

### C. What the source does not address, and 02.5-08 must

The decoder's curvature is `H_F`, the curvature of the LEARNED manifold `M_F = F(R^d)`, not
`H_true`, the curvature of the data manifold. A reconstruction objective asks `F(E(x)) ~= x`;
it never asks `D^2F ~= D^2F_true`, and second derivatives are far more sensitive than
position. Both stage-1 gating statistics are rank-based and exactly blind to the difference —
a decoder compressing every amplitude by a constant factor scores `1.0` on both.

The source, shown this, supplied the sharpest illustration of it, recorded here: if the true
local surface is `y = a x^2` and the decoder learns `y = 0.7 a x^2`, reconstruction error
stays tiny wherever the sampled `x` sit near zero, while the second derivative is `1.4a`
instead of `2a` — **curvature attenuated 30% with no reconstruction signal at all.**
Reconstruction quality can never validate a curvature estimate.

**Required checks for 02.5-08, refined by the source's Section 7 and adopted:** `H` is
vector-valued, so amplitude attenuation and orientation error are DISTINCT failure modes and
must be reported separately, never collapsed into one scalar:

1. **direction** — cosine similarity between `H_F` and the fixture's analytic `H_true`;
2. **magnitude** — the ratio `||H_F|| / ||H_true||`, reported as median AND per-point CV, in
   the format of `02.5-NOTE-high-d-curvature-approaches.md` Section 1a. That section shows why
   both numbers are needed: at `d=2` the point-cloud estimator gives a clean 0.905 median ratio
   at CV 0.250, while at `d=20` it gives CV 2.250 with a non-monotone median — the same pair
   separates "attenuated but calibratable" from "destroyed".
3. **calibration slope** — regress `||H_F||` on `||H_true||`, check `a ~= 1` and `b ~= 0`. A
   rank statistic cannot see `a = 0.5`; this can.

See also `02.5-NOTE-high-d-curvature-approaches.md` Section 2d.

## What 02.5-08 should do

- Keep the EXACT `torch.func` `g`-trace as the gating computation, as already planned (D-09).
  At `d = 20` this is **20 Hessian-vector products** — `H = sum_i B(e_i, e_i)` over a
  `g`-orthonormal frame, under this module's trace convention. Affordable, deterministic, and
  it removes Monte-Carlo variance from the arm's headline result entirely.
- Keep the randomized K-probe estimator, but **demoted in purpose**: it is a CONVERGENCE CHECK
  ON THE EXACT PATH, not a candidate estimator. The randomized estimate must converge to the
  exact one as `K` grows; that agreement is evidence the exact implementation is right, which
  is worth having for the same reason the sphere known-answer test is. Report `K = 4, 8, 16`
  as recorded context, gated on nothing.

  (The source's own revised recommendation was to drop the stochastic estimator altogether at
  `d = 20`, on the grounds that it muddies the research question. That reasoning is accepted
  for its role as an ESTIMATOR — hence the demotion above — but the convergence check costs
  almost nothing and validates the exact path, so it is retained in that reduced role rather
  than deleted.)
- Do not let the randomized path touch any gated number.
- Do NOT implement antithetic probes: zero variance reduction, twice the cost (Addendum A).
- Do NOT reach for Hutch++ here without first confronting the vector-valued `II` problem
  (Addendum A's correction).
- Report direction, magnitude, and calibration slope SEPARATELY against the fixture's analytic
  `H` (Addendum C). Rank agreement alone can never validate this arm.

## The research question this arm actually tests

Stated here because both the source and this note converged on it, and it is sharper than
anything either started with:

> **Can learning a global smooth manifold overcome the locality breakdown of nonparametric
> curvature estimation at moderate intrinsic dimension, WITHOUT distorting curvature
> amplitude?**

The trade is explicit: the point-cloud arm pays **locality/sampling error** (measured:
`r/R = 0.906` at `d = 20`); the decoder arm pays **representation/smoothing bias** (`H_F` is
the exact curvature of possibly the wrong manifold). Stage 1 measured the first cost. 02.5-08
exists to measure the second, and the amplitude checks above are how it gets measured rather
than assumed.
