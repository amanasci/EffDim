## User Constraints (from CONTEXT.md)

### Locked Decisions

#### T1 — the topological fidelity statistic

- **D-01:** T1 is **the paper's `L_t` evaluated on held-out data**, not a combinatorial matched-pair
  fraction and not a Wasserstein diagram distance. Measures the method's own claim and stays
  comparable to published numbers. — **Reversibility:** one-way — once the threshold is committed
  with git-ancestry proof, changing which statistic gates is post-hoc metric selection, the exact
  move the milestone's pre-registration discipline exists to prevent.
- **D-02:** T1's threshold is a **baseline-relative margin** over the matched-capacity
  `cae.PlainAutoEncoder`, not an absolute number. Self-calibrating, needs no value invented for data
  it was never measured on, and reuses the "must beat, not tie" rule 02.2 established as CAE-03.
- **D-03:** T1 **gates on the MST over the entire held-out set** in one pass (n ≈ 2,000, a trivial
  2,000×2,000 matrix), because that captures *global* topology — a latent can preserve every
  64-point neighbourhood while scrambling how regions connect. The batch-wise average at training
  batch size is **reported, non-gating**, for consistency with how the loss was optimised.
- **D-04:** `L_t` is a sum of two directional terms. They are **computed separately and the worse
  one gates** — not summed then compared. Input→latent catches destroyed structure, latent→input
  catches invented structure; these are different failures. Matches the worse-of-two rule Phase 2's
  gate already applies to `r` and `m`.
- **D-05:** Latent coordinates are **normalised to unit variance** before `L_t` is computed, rather
  than fitting a global scale from the target as `cae.fit_global_scale` does. Nothing is fitted from
  the target, which is cleaner. **Stated cost:** 02.4's T1 number is therefore **not** directly
  comparable to 02.2's CAE distortion figure — a different normalisation means a different
  statistic. Record this in the pre-registration so no later reader compares them.
  — **Reversibility:** costly — switching to `fit_global_scale` after thresholds are committed would
  require re-deriving the T1 margin and re-running every fit.

#### T3 — the rank-structure measure

- **D-06:** T3 is **`min(trustworthiness, continuity)`** at the gating `k`. Both are bounded [0,1],
  both are reported by Moor et al. themselves so the numbers are citable, and the worse-of-two rule
  matches T1 and Phase 2's gate.
- **D-07:** **`k` sweep over {5, 15, 30}, gating at `k = 15`.** 15 is `k*` — the neighbourhood size
  the Isomap graph behind every geodesic in this milestone was frozen at by 01-04's pre-registered
  plateau rule — so the gating `k` inherits an existing justification. The other two rungs are
  sensitivity, showing whether the verdict is an artifact of one neighbourhood scale. Ranks are
  computed once, so the sweep is near-free.
- **D-08:** T3's threshold is a **baseline-relative margin**, consistent with T1 and T2.
- **D-09:** T1 and T3 **gate independently, with their overlap documented**. Both are two-sided and
  may correlate — T1's MST measures the *connectivity skeleton*, T3 measures *local neighbourhood
  ordering*, and a latent can preserve one while breaking the other. The pre-registration states
  plainly that a joint FAIL on both is **one** structural finding, not two independent ones.
- **D-10:** T3 is *higher-is-better* but `cae.verdict_from_metrics` applies **strict less-than**.
  Express the gating quantity as `1 − min(trustworthiness, continuity)` so the comparison runs the
  same direction as T1 and T2. 02.2 hit this exact issue with its reconstruction margin and inverted
  the quantity for the same reason.

#### Batch size and loss weighting

- **D-11:** **λ is tuned on the Swiss roll fixture, then frozen** in `02.4-PREREGISTRATION.md`
  before any PU fit. Legitimate because the fixture is not the gate data. **Stated risk:** a λ tuned
  on a 2-d sheet in 3-d may not transfer to a ~20-d manifold in 768-d; the pre-registration must say
  so. — **Reversibility:** one-way — λ moves T1 and T2 in opposite directions, so re-selecting it
  after any gate result is seen is precisely the post-hoc tuning SPEC prohibition 1 forbids.
- **D-12:** **The λ sweep lives in a separate `notebooks/diagnostics/` runner, NOT inside the
  sanity-check notebook.** `CLAUDE.md` caps that notebook at ~15 cells with no threshold tables and
  no pre-registration apparatus; a sweep inside it would break those rules. The sweep runner and the
  sanity-check notebook share the same Swiss roll generator; the notebook consumes the frozen λ.
- **D-13:** **Phase task order is inverted from the obvious one.** Swiss roll fixture → λ sweep → λ
  frozen → `02.4-PREREGISTRATION.md` committed → only then any PU fit. The git-ancestry ordering
  proof still holds, but the planner must not assume the pre-registration comes first.
- **D-14:** **Batch size 64**, matching 02.2 under the same compute ceiling on the same data — keeps
  the two experiments comparable on training protocol, and the per-batch distance matrix stays a
  trivial 64×64. Pre-registered as a **method constant, not a tunable**: the MST is built per batch,
  so batch size sets the scale of topology the model can learn.
- **D-15:** **λ warm-up then constant.** Reconstruction-only for a stated number of epochs, then ramp
  λ to its frozen value, so the topological term cannot dominate before the latent means anything.
  Adds two constants — **warm-up length and ramp shape** — both of which must be pre-registered and
  reported in the verdict artifact.
- **D-16:** **The primary rung alone produces the verdict.** A split across other rungs — one
  passing T1 and failing T2, another the reverse — is reported as a measured **T1/T2 frontier**,
  genuinely informative about the method, and never promotes a different rung to primary.
  Pre-registering this is what makes SPEC prohibition 2 (no post-hoc primary-d selection)
  enforceable rather than aspirational. — **Reversibility:** one-way — same garden-of-forking-paths
  exposure as D-11.

#### Latent ladder, primary dimension, and seeds

- **D-17:** **Ladder = {8, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40}, primary `d = 20`.** Each end of the range answers a
  prior claim: 8 is 02.1's coordinate-branch elbow (`classical_p`), 40 tests the dimension-starvation
  finding directly (`02-FINDINGS.md` §6.4 — 100% of neighbourhoods needed more than 18 dims for 90%
  local variance, so every fit this milestone has produced may have been starved). Primary 20 sits
  inside the measured local intrinsic cluster (TwoNN 19.5, local-PCA median 25.0) **and** matches
  02.2's `D_CHART` exactly, so a TopoAE verdict is directly comparable to the CAE's FAIL at the same
  width. 20 was inserted into the ladder specifically so the primary rung is *in* the sweep — a
  primary outside the ladder would be an unpre-registered configuration.
  — **Reversibility:** costly — changing `d` after training invalidates all fits and thresholds.
- **D-18:** **3 seeds on the primary rung, 1 on each other rung** — 8 fits total. The verdict rests
  on the rung with a variance estimate; the others carry the sensitivity story.
- **D-19:** **Per-fit ceiling cut from 02.2's 2h to ~1h**, via a tighter pre-registered epoch cap, so
  8 fits land at ~8h — back inside 02.2's overnight envelope. **Stated limitation:** the larger rungs
  (32, 40) may be under-trained at that cap, which must be reported as a known limitation of the
  sensitivity rungs rather than discovered afterwards. The primary rung at `d = 20` must be verified
  to converge within the cap before the ladder is committed.
- **D-20:** **The worst of the 3 primary-rung seeds gates each metric**; mean and spread are
  reported. Makes a PASS mean "this works reliably" rather than "this worked once" — and the phase's
  own conclusion about 02.1's decoder check was that single-seed gaps cannot be separated from
  initialisation noise.

## Summary

This phase implements one published method (Moor, Horn, Rieck, Borgwardt, "Topological
Autoencoders," ICML 2020, arXiv:1906.00722) whose core computational primitive — 0-dimensional
persistent homology via a minimum spanning tree — is short enough (~40 lines) to hand-implement and
verify exactly against the paper's own public reference code, which this research did directly
(`BorgwardtLab/topological-autoencoders`, MIT-licensed, `src/topology.py` and
`src/models/approx_based.py`). That reference implementation resolved every open question CONTEXT.md
carried into this research: the exact topological-loss formula (`match_edges="symmetric"`: a sum of
two directional squared-error terms), the differentiable formulation (MST edge *selection* is done on
a `.detach()`'d copy of the distance matrix — a non-differentiable numpy step — but the loss itself
indexes the **original, gradient-carrying** distance tensor at the selected `(i, j)` positions, so
gradients flow through the *values*, never through *which* edges were picked), and the tie-break
mechanics (`np.argsort(weights, kind='stable')` over row-major upper-triangular index order — exactly
the `(distance, row_index, col_index)` lexicographic rule SPEC already locks). A from-scratch
reimplementation of this algorithm was written and cross-checked bit-for-bit against the reference on
a hand-built tied-distance fixture and a 200-point randomized tie-heavy fixture; both matched exactly
(see Sources, "Primary — empirically verified").

The second major finding changes the shape of `topoae.py` for the better: SPEC's own Requirement 2
already names `PlainAutoEncoder` as an import target from `cae.py`, and the reference code confirms
why that is not just the T2 baseline's role but the *correct* choice for the TopoAE model's backbone
itself — the paper's autoencoder is architecturally a plain encoder/decoder MLP (no chart atlas, no
partition of unity); only the *loss* differs from a plain autoencoder by adding `λ * L_topo`. This
means `topoae.py` needs no new `nn.Module` subclass at all. It needs a `persistence_pairs()` function,
a `topological_loss()` function, a `train_topoae()` training loop that wraps `cae.PlainAutoEncoder`
with the extra loss term (mirroring — not importing, since it is a private helper —
`cae._train_decoder_protocol`'s seeding/stopping-rule discipline), and the T3 rank-structure functions
(`sklearn.manifold.trustworthiness`, called twice with swapped arguments to get continuity for free —
verified against the installed `sklearn==1.9.0`, whose formula is textually identical to the paper's
own reference `measures.py`). Every other piece of infrastructure this phase needs —
`verdict_from_metrics`, `reconstruction_stats`, `activation_module`, `to_native`,
`write_cae_verdict`/`write_cae_handoff`, the cache layer, the git-ancestry pre-registration proof — is
generic and already sealed in `cae.py`/`cache.py`, confirmed unmodified by reading both files in full.

**Primary recommendation:** implement 0-dim persistence as a hand-rolled Union-Find/Kruskal routine in
pure numpy (mirroring the paper's own reference code, not `scipy.sparse.csgraph.minimum_spanning_tree`
— see Common Pitfalls #1 for why); build the TopoAE model as `cae.PlainAutoEncoder` plus a
`λ`-weighted topological loss term added inside a new, mirrored (not imported) training loop; reuse
every gate-engine and artifact-writer function from `cae.py` and `cache.py` unchanged; compute T3 via
two calls to `sklearn.manifold.trustworthiness` rather than a hand-rolled continuity function.

### Pattern 1: 0-dim persistence via hand-rolled Union-Find/Kruskal (not `scipy`'s MST)

**What:** Sort the complete graph's upper-triangular edges by `(distance, row_index, col_index)`
using `np.argsort(weights, kind='stable')` over `np.triu_indices(n, k=1)` (already row-major, so a
stable sort on weight alone realizes the full lexicographic tie-break with no extra key). Walk the
sorted edges with a Union-Find structure; an edge that merges two different components is a
persistence pair; stop once `n - 1` pairs are found (every later edge would only create a cycle in a
complete graph, so it is safe to skip).

**When to use:** Every place SPEC's Requirement 1 needs 0-dim persistence pairs — inside the training
loop's per-batch topological loss, and inside the evaluation runner's held-out T1 gate.

**Example (verified bit-identical to the paper's own reference implementation — see Sources):**
```python
# Source: written for this research, cross-checked byte-for-byte against
# BorgwardtLab/topological-autoencoders src/topology.py PersistentHomologyCalculation
def persistence_pairs(D: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """0-dim persistence pairs = the MST edge set of the complete graph on D's
    pairwise distances. D must be float64, symmetric, shape (n, n), n >= 2.
    Ties broken lexicographically on (distance, i, j) via a stable sort over
    row-major upper-triangular index order -- deterministic across calls and
    process restarts, and independent of any library's internal MST tie-break."""
    n = D.shape[0]
    if n < 2:
        raise ValueError(f"persistence_pairs: batch size {n} < 2 -- undefined below 2 points")
    iu, ju = np.triu_indices(n, k=1)
    order = np.argsort(D[iu, ju], kind="stable")

    parent = np.arange(n)
    def find(x):
        while parent[x] != x:
            parent[x] = parent[parent[x]]
            x = parent[x]
        return x

    pairs = []
    for k in order:
        i, j = int(iu[k]), int(ju[k])
        ri, rj = find(i), find(j)
        if ri == rj:
            continue
        parent[ri] = rj
        pairs.append((i, j))
        if len(pairs) == n - 1:
            break
    return np.array(pairs, dtype=np.int64)
```

### Pattern 2: symmetric two-directional topological loss

**What:** For pairs selected by X's own MST, compare their length in X vs. their length in Z
(catches destroyed structure); for pairs selected by Z's own MST, compare their length in Z vs. their
length in X (catches invented structure); sum for the training loss, keep separate for the T1 gate
(D-04).

**Example (mirrors `match_edges="symmetric"` in the paper's reference `TopologicalSignatureDistance`):**
```python
# Source: this research, structurally identical to
# BorgwardtLab/topological-autoencoders src/models/approx_based.py's
# TopologicalSignatureDistance.forward, match_edges="symmetric" branch
def topological_loss(d_x: torch.Tensor, d_z: torch.Tensor) -> dict:
    """d_x, d_z: (n, n) distance matrices over the SAME n points, same row order,
    float64, differentiable (do not .detach() these -- only persistence_pairs'
    internal numpy step touches a detached copy). Returns loss_x_to_z (destroyed
    structure), loss_z_to_x (invented structure), and total = their sum -- the
    TRAINING loss uses total; the T1 GATE evaluates the two components
    separately and gates on the worse one (CONTEXT.md D-04)."""
    pairs_x = persistence_pairs(d_x.detach().cpu().numpy())
    pairs_z = persistence_pairs(d_z.detach().cpu().numpy())
    ix, jx = pairs_x[:, 0], pairs_x[:, 1]
    iz, jz = pairs_z[:, 0], pairs_z[:, 1]

    loss_x_to_z = ((d_x[ix, jx] - d_z[ix, jx]) ** 2).sum()
    loss_z_to_x = ((d_z[iz, jz] - d_x[iz, jz]) ** 2).sum()
    return {"loss_x_to_z": loss_x_to_z, "loss_z_to_x": loss_z_to_x,
            "total": loss_x_to_z + loss_z_to_x}
```

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Trusting `scipy.sparse.csgraph.minimum_spanning_tree`'s tie-break for a determinism-critical
  loss:** it returns a deterministic result for a fixed scipy version (empirically confirmed, see
  Sources) but its tie-break order is not a documented, version-pinned contract — exactly the risk
  SPEC's own "a scipy version bump cannot silently move the loss" language warns about.
- **Reimplementing the FPS-seeded chart pre-training machinery for TopoAE:** `cae.fps_pretrain_loss`
  and `cae.farthest_point_sample` exist to prevent CAE's dead-chart failure mode. TopoAE has no
  charts — there is nothing to pre-train per-chart. Reusing this machinery here would be importing a
  solution to a problem this model does not have.
- **Computing persistence pairs and the loss's squared differences in float32:** cae.py's usual
  convention (`torch.tensor(X, dtype=torch.float32)`) is fine for reconstruction losses but SPEC's
  own Constraints section requires "distance matrices in float64" for determinism — tie detection
  after float32 rounding can silently change which edges tie.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: relying on `scipy`'s MST tie-break for a determinism-critical statistic
**What goes wrong:** `scipy.sparse.csgraph.minimum_spanning_tree` returns a deterministic result for
a fixed input under one scipy install (`[VERIFIED]` via direct testing — three repeated calls on a
tied 4-point distance matrix returned identical edges), but its internal tie-break order (a Prim/
Jarník variant over a CSR structure) is not documented as a version-stable API contract.
**Why it happens:** the SPEC's own language ("no new dependency... already used in
`geometry_probes_run.py`") reads as an endorsement of using scipy's function for this task, but that
prior usage (Phase 02.1's `geometry_probes_run.py`) uses it for a synthetic tree-fixture generator
where the *specific* tree chosen does not matter, only that some valid MST is produced — a
fundamentally different requirement from a loss whose value depends on which edges were selected.
**How to avoid:** implement the Union-Find/Kruskal routine directly (Pattern 1) — it is the same
algorithm the paper's own authors used, is provably equivalent (empirically cross-checked, see
Sources), and removes the version-coupling risk entirely.
**Warning signs:** any test that only checks "some MST" rather than "this exact edge set" would not
catch this; SPEC's Acceptance Criterion for R1 ("identical pairings across repeated calls and process
restarts") is thankfully strict enough to catch it if `scipy`'s function is ever swapped in.

### Pitfall 2: ambient (X-space) distance normalization is undecided
**What goes wrong:** the paper's reference implementation normalizes `x_distances` by the batch's own
maximum distance before computing the loss (`x_distances = x_distances / x_distances.max()`), and
normalizes `latent_distances` by a **learned** parameter (`self.latent_norm`, `nn.Parameter`, trained
jointly). CONTEXT.md's D-05 fixes the **latent** normalization (unit variance, not fitted from the
target) but says nothing about the **ambient** side.
**Why it happens:** the paper's normalization choices were tuned for image data with a known pixel
range; this project's ambient data is L2-normalized 768-d embeddings, so distances are already
bounded (points lie on the unit sphere), which may make ambient normalization less necessary here —
but "may" is not a decision. This is a genuine gap between what CONTEXT.md locked and what the
implementation needs.
**How to avoid:** the pre-registration must state explicitly whether `d_x` is used raw (float64
Euclidean in `R^768`) or normalized, and by what scalar, consistently between the per-batch training
loss and the full-held-out-set T1 gate. Flagged as an Open Question below — this is planner/
pre-registration territory, not something this research should decide unilaterally.
**Warning signs:** a λ tuned on the Swiss roll (3-d, unnormalized or trivially normalized) transferring
poorly to 768-d — exactly the risk D-11 already names, but this pitfall names a *specific*,
independently-tunable source of that risk (scale mismatch, not just intrinsic-dimension mismatch).

### Pitfall 3: "unit variance" latent normalization is ambiguous between isotropic and per-axis
**What goes wrong:** CONTEXT.md D-05 says latent coordinates are "normalised to unit variance before
`L_t` is computed." This could mean (a) one global isotropic scalar so the *overall* variance is 1
(preserves the latent's relative geometry exactly, only rescales it), or (b) per-dimension
standardization (subtract each dimension's mean, divide by that dimension's own std — the
`StandardScaler` reading). Reading (b) would **non-isometrically distort** the latent before computing
distances, corrupting the very topological structure T1 measures — an anisotropic rescaling changes
which points are nearest neighbours along different axes.
**Why it happens:** "unit variance" is standard-scaler vocabulary in most ML contexts, but that
reading is actively wrong for a distance-based topological statistic.
**How to avoid:** the pre-registration must state the formula explicitly, e.g.
`z_scaled = z / sqrt(mean(var(z, axis=0)))` (one global scalar derived from the mean per-dimension
variance) — isometric, preserves relative geometry, matches the spirit of `cae.fit_global_scale`
being "one global isotropic scale factor" while not fitting it from a target. This is flagged
`[ASSUMED]` in the Assumptions Log — confirm the intended reading before implementing T1.

### Pitfall 4: training loss (sum) vs. T1 gate (separate, worse-of-two) are different reductions of the same function
**What goes wrong:** `topological_loss()`'s `total` field (Pattern 2) is what the optimizer minimizes
during training. D-04 requires the T1 **gate** to look at `loss_x_to_z` and `loss_z_to_x`
**separately** and gate on whichever is worse relative to its own baseline value — not on `total`.
Implementing the gate as `metrics["distortion_like"] = total_loss / total_loss_baseline` would collapse
the two failure modes D-04 explicitly wants kept apart (destroyed vs. invented structure).
**How to avoid:** compute `loss_x_to_z` and `loss_z_to_x` as two separate scalars for both the TopoAE
latent and the baseline latent, form two baseline-relative ratios, and gate on
`max(ratio_x_to_z, ratio_z_to_x)` — exactly the pattern `cae_evaluate_run.py` already uses for T3's
two-control worst-case ratio (`max(ratio_vs_plainae20, ratio_vs_mdsdec8)`), just with directions
instead of baselines as the two things being maxed over.

### Pitfall 5: Python-loop MST cost at n≈2,000 (T1's full-held-out-set gate)
**What goes wrong:** the reference implementation's Union-Find loop is a pure-Python `for` loop over
sorted edges. At batch size 64 (training) this is ~2,016 edges per call, trivial. At n≈2,000 (T1's
held-out gate, D-03) the upper triangle has ~2,000,000 edges — a Python loop over all of them (rather
than stopping at `n-1` merges) could take several seconds per call.
**Why it happens:** the reference implementation does not early-break; it was written for MNIST/CIFAR
batch sizes (28-126 per the recovered `best_runs/*.json` configs), never for a single 2,000-point
evaluation pass.
**How to avoid:** Pattern 1's implementation above **does** early-break once `n-1` pairs are found
(verified to produce identical output with and without the break, since every edge after the (n-1)th
merge necessarily closes a cycle and is skipped anyway). This turns the dominant cost into the
`O(n^2 log n)` sort, which for n=2,000 is on the order of a few hundred milliseconds in numpy — fine
for a once-per-fit evaluation call.
**Warning signs:** a T1 gate computation that takes much longer than the sort alone suggests the
early-break was dropped.

### Pitfall 6: `sklearn.manifold.trustworthiness`'s `n_neighbors < n_samples/2` constraint
**What goes wrong:** sklearn raises if `n_neighbors >= n_samples / 2`. At `k=30` sweep rung and
`n_holdout ≈ 2,000 * (1 - holdout_fraction)` this is nowhere close (30 << 1,000), so this is a
non-issue at the currently planned scale — flagged only because a future change to holdout fraction
or dataset size could silently trip it.
**How to avoid:** no action needed at current scale; note the constraint in `topoae.py`'s docstring so
a future holdout-fraction change is caught by the error message rather than silently producing NaN.

### Pitfall 7: baseline `PlainAutoEncoder` fits needed per ladder rung, not just at the primary
**What goes wrong:** D-18 counts "8 fits total" for the TopoAE ladder (3 seeds at d=20, 1 seed at each
other rung), but T1/T2/T3 are all **baseline-relative** margins (D-02, D-08, and T2's inherited
CAE-03 rule). A margin needs a baseline **at the same dimension** — a `PlainAutoEncoder` fit at d=8
cannot serve as the baseline for a TopoAE fit at d=40. D-18's fit count appears to cover only the
TopoAE side; the compute budget (D-19: "~8h... back inside 02.2's overnight envelope") may not have
priced in the matched-capacity baseline fits at each rung.
**How to avoid:** budget at least one `PlainAutoEncoder` baseline fit per ladder rung used for gating
(minimally: the primary rung, `d=20`; if the T1/T2 frontier (D-16) is reported with baseline-relative
numbers at every rung rather than raw values, all six rungs need a baseline). `PlainAutoEncoder`
baseline fits are cheap relative to TopoAE fits — 02.2's two plain-AE controls totalled "≤0.5h" per
`02.2-PREREGISTRATION.md` §7 — so even six additional baseline fits fit comfortably inside a
~10-hour overnight envelope, but this must be **counted explicitly** in `02.4-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s
own cost section (mirroring `02.2-PREREGISTRATION.md` §7's pattern), not assumed free.
**Warning signs:** a pre-registration whose "expected cost" section only lists 8 fits.

### Pitfall 8: FPS pre-training machinery does not apply
**What goes wrong:** copying `cae.py`'s two-stage training pattern (FPS-seeded chart pre-training,
then main loop) wholesale into `train_topoae` would try to pre-train per-chart, but TopoAE has no
charts (Pattern 3). `cae.farthest_point_sample`/`cae.fps_pretrain_loss` exist specifically for CAE's
dead-chart failure mode.
**How to avoid:** `train_topoae`'s training loop should mirror `cae._train_decoder_protocol`'s
single-stage structure (seeding, batching, three-way stopping rule) — not `cae.train_cae`'s two-stage
structure — with the topological loss term inserted into the per-batch loss computation and the λ
warm-up (D-15) applied as an epoch-indexed schedule.

### Pitfall 9: reconciliation runner (R7) has no direct precedent in this codebase
**What goes wrong:** every prior phase's runner only ever wrote to `notebooks/.cache/` (gitignored).
R7 requires a runner (on PASS only) to write **tracked** files — a new `02.1-AMENDMENT-03.md` and a
`REQUIREMENTS.md` edit — programmatically, existence-guarded so a second run is a no-op. No existing
runner does this; `cae_evaluate_run.py`'s PASS branch only writes to the cache.
**How to avoid:** treat this as new, careful code: check for `02.1-AMENDMENT-03.md`'s existence (and/or
a marker comment inside `REQUIREMENTS.md`) before writing anything, print a clear "already written"
message on the second run (matching the acceptance criterion's exact wording), and keep the amendment
content itself append-only / additive per SPEC's explicit prohibition against editing or overwriting
any sealed 02.1 artifact.

### Pitfall 10: batch-level distance-matrix dtype defaults to float32 in this codebase's usual convention
**What goes wrong:** every existing training loop in `cae.py` uses `torch.tensor(X, dtype=torch.float32)`
for the main data tensors. SPEC's Constraints section requires "distance matrices in float64" for
determinism. If `torch.cdist` is called directly on the float32 batch tensors, the resulting distance
matrix (and therefore the persistence tie-detection) is computed in float32, silently violating the
determinism constraint.
**How to avoid:** explicitly `.double()` the batch before computing `d_x`/`d_z` (or cast the distance
matrix immediately after `torch.cdist`), matching the float64 discipline `geometry_probes.py` and
`cae.embedding_distortion` already use elsewhere in this codebase.

### 0-dim persistence pairs (cross-checked byte-for-byte against the paper's reference code)
```python
# Source: this research; verified identical to BorgwardtLab/topological-autoencoders
# src/topology.py PersistentHomologyCalculation on both a hand-built tied 5x5 fixture
# and a randomized 200x200 tie-heavy fixture (integer distances -> many exact ties)
def persistence_pairs(D: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    n = D.shape[0]
    if n < 2:
        raise ValueError(f"persistence_pairs: batch size {n} < 2 -- undefined below 2 points")
    iu, ju = np.triu_indices(n, k=1)
    order = np.argsort(D[iu, ju], kind="stable")
    parent = np.arange(n)
    def find(x):
        while parent[x] != x:
            parent[x] = parent[parent[x]]
            x = parent[x]
        return x
    pairs = []
    for k in order:
        i, j = int(iu[k]), int(ju[k])
        ri, rj = find(i), find(j)
        if ri == rj:
            continue
        parent[ri] = rj
        pairs.append((i, j))
        if len(pairs) == n - 1:
            break
    return np.array(pairs, dtype=np.int64)
```

### T3 rank-structure metric (continuity via swapped `trustworthiness` arguments)
```python
# Source: sklearn.manifold.trustworthiness, installed sklearn==1.9.0
# [VERIFIED: .venv/bin/python3 -c "from sklearn.manifold import trustworthiness; help(...)"]
# Formula printed by sklearn's own docstring is textually identical to the paper's
# reference measures.py: T(k) = 1 - 2/(nk(2n-3k-1)) * sum(max(0, r(i,j)-k))
from sklearn.manifold import trustworthiness

def rank_structure(x_ambient: np.ndarray, z_latent: np.ndarray, k: int) -> dict:
    trust = trustworthiness(x_ambient, z_latent, n_neighbors=k)
    cont = trustworthiness(z_latent, x_ambient, n_neighbors=k)  # continuity = swapped args
    return {"trustworthiness": trust, "continuity": cont,
            "gate_value": 1.0 - min(trust, cont)}  # D-10: higher-is-better -> strict-less-than form
```

     CONTEXT.md's 20 locked decisions) and state the choice as its own named constant so a later
     reader does not have to infer it from the code.

2. **Baseline fit coverage across the ladder (Pitfall #7)**
   - What we know: D-18 counts 8 TopoAE fits; T1/T2/T3 need a matched-dimension `PlainAutoEncoder`
     baseline for every rung whose margin is gated or reported as part of the T1/T2 frontier (D-16).
   - What's unclear: whether the frontier reporting for non-primary rungs needs its own baseline (full
     margin numbers at every rung) or can report raw values only (baseline only at the primary rung).
   - Recommendation: the planner should resolve this explicitly and add the resulting baseline-fit
     count to `02.4-PREREGISTRATION.md`'s cost section, mirroring `02.2-PREREGISTRATION.md` §7.

3. **Warm-up length and ramp shape (D-15, Claude's Discretion but must be pre-registered)**
   - What we know: reconstruction-only for a stated number of epochs, then ramp λ to its frozen value.
   - What's unclear: concrete numbers — these depend on `MAX_EPOCHS`, which itself depends on the
     timing-probe result (mirroring `cae_train_run.py`'s D-07-style compute contingency), not yet run.
   - Recommendation: express warm-up length as a **fraction** of `MAX_EPOCHS` decided after the timing
     probe, ramp shape linear (simplest defensible choice, no precedent argues for anything fancier),
     and pre-register the resulting concrete epoch counts once `MAX_EPOCHS` is known.

### Known Threat Patterns for this stack

| Pattern | STRIDE | Standard Mitigation |
|---------|--------|---------------------|
| Pickle deserialization of an untrusted or corrupted joblib/pickle artifact | Tampering | Already avoided project-wide: `cae.py`'s `state_dict_to_arrays`/`arrays_to_state_dict` route model weights through plain numpy arrays into `cache.npz_cache`, never through `joblib_cache`'s pickle path (documented in `cae.py`'s own module docstring, T-01-01). `topoae.py` should follow the identical pattern for its fit artifacts. |
| Cache-stem path traversal (a stem containing `..` escaping `CACHE_DIR`) | Tampering / Information Disclosure | Already mitigated generically by `cache._assert_inside_cache`, applies unchanged to every new stem this phase introduces (`topoae_fit_*`, `topoae_verdict_*`, etc.) — no new code needed, just continued use of `cache.npz_cache`/`json_cache`/`cache_path` rather than raw file I/O |
| Silent stale-cache reuse (a manifest mismatch treated as a hit) | Tampering | Already mitigated by `cache._manifest_matches` raising `ValueError` on any cfg mismatch rather than returning `False`; applies unchanged |
