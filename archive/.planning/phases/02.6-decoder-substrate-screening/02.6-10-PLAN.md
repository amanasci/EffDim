<objective>
Build the derivative-usability bridge instrument (SC-5; D-16, D-17, D-18).

`02.6-FINDINGS.md` §4's second separable property — *are a trained net's second derivatives
trustworthy* — was never tested by the halted run, and `02.5-10` cannot seal a curvature threshold
on a decoder without knowing whether that decoder's second derivatives are even computable. D-16
chooses autodiff-versus-finite-difference agreement as the instrument precisely because it needs no
ground truth, so it runs on PU exactly as it runs on the roll.

CONTEXT.md records the honest limit as assumption A-01: this establishes that the derivative
*computation* is stable, not that the surface is right. A net that learned a smooth but wrong surface
passes it cleanly. The phase proceeds with that recorded rather than closed, and the separator (plan
02.6-08) is the nearest thing to a cover.

`02.6-RESEARCH.md` Pitfall 5 measured the step-size error curve directly against this project's own
sphere fixture and found the float64 U-shape with its minimum at `h ~ 1e-4` (max abs error `4.5e-8`,
degrading roughly four orders of magnitude one decade either side). That is a calibrated starting
point for this decoder shape and scale, not a universal constant — assumption A-06 flags that a
differently-scaled trained decoder can shift the optimum, so this module calibrates rather than
assumes.

Purpose: an instrument that measures derivative usability without needing an answer key, so it
transfers to the regime the phase's question is actually about.
Output: `notebooks/pu_manifold/derivative_bridge.py` and its test file. **No bar is applied here**
(D-18) — the plan that runs it (02.6-14) prints numbers, and `02.5-10` decides what it can use.
</objective>

  <action>
Create `notebooks/pu_manifold/derivative_bridge.py`, a new sibling module. Additive: nothing under
`notebooks/pu_manifold/` is edited, and `decoder_curvature.py` in particular carries forward
byte-unchanged. Mirror `decoder_curvature.py`'s module-header discipline, including its
"reused-unchanged-from-sealed-code, not re-derived" opening paragraph and its convention-constant
drift guard.

Public symbols, exactly these names:

- `FD_STENCIL_CONVENTION` — a module constant string pinning the stencil this module uses, so it
  cannot drift: the diagonal entries are the standard second central difference of the map along one
  latent direction, and the off-diagonal entries are the standard four-point mixed central
  difference along two directions, each divided by the appropriate power of the step. Write the two
  formulas out in the constant's text.

- `DEFAULT_FD_STEP = 1e-4` — with a comment recording that this is the measured minimum of the
  float64 error curve for this project's own sphere-decoder fixture at its own scale, and
  explicitly that it is a starting point and not a universal constant: the optimum depends on the
  local curvature scale of whatever decoder is being tested, so callers working on a
  differently-scaled decoder are expected to call `calibrate_fd_step` rather than trust it blind
  (assumption A-06).

- `MAX_FD_ROWS` — the row cap for one batched decoder forward, so the point chunk is derived rather
  than guessed. Record the arithmetic in its comment: the stencil needs `1 + 2*d + 4*d*(d-1)/2`
  evaluations per point, which is `9` at the roll's `d = 2` and `3201` at the PU control's
  `d = 40`; at `d = 40` with a 768-dimensional output, 64 points in one shot would be roughly
  200,000 rows and over a gigabyte of float64 output, which is why chunking is not optional.

- `finite_difference_jacobian(decode_batch, z, h)` — central-difference Jacobian, shape
  `(batch, out_dim, d)`.

- `finite_difference_hessian(decode_batch, z, h=DEFAULT_FD_STEP)` — central-difference Hessian,
  shape `(batch, out_dim, d, d)`, symmetric by construction (compute the upper triangle and mirror
  it). `decode_batch` takes an `(m, d)` tensor and returns `(m, out_dim)` — on every model in this
  project that is `model.decode`, the same map `plain_decoder_map` wraps, and deliberately never
  `model.forward`, so what is differentiated is the decoder's own image manifold and not the
  encoder-composed round trip. Build every perturbed point for a chunk of points into one stacked
  tensor and issue one forward per chunk, sized by `MAX_FD_ROWS`. Require float64 input, using
  `chart_curvature._assert_float64` unchanged rather than a new check.

- `reduce_to_H_vec(J, Hess)` — the mean curvature vector from a Jacobian `(batch, out_dim, d)` and a
  Hessian `(batch, out_dim, d, d)`, mirroring `chart_curvature.chart_mean_curvature`'s body exactly:
  the trace-first-then-project `d x d` solve, never an explicit output-by-output projector and never
  a materialised second-fundamental-form tensor. Read that body directly and mirror it; do not
  re-derive the algebra. State in the docstring that this is a mirror whose only warrant is the
  pinning test against `plain_decoder_curvature`, and that the sealed file is not edited.

- `calibrate_fd_step(decode_batch, z, steps=...)` — evaluates the finite-difference Hessian at each
  candidate step against the autodiff Hessian of the same map, returns the per-step max abs error
  and the argmin, and does not mutate `DEFAULT_FD_STEP`. This is the function that makes A-06
  actionable instead of a warning.

- `derivative_agreement(model, z, h=None)` — the bridge's reporting unit. Computes the autodiff
  Jacobian and Hessian through `torch.func` exactly as `plain_decoder_curvature` does (reuse
  `chart_curvature.VMAP_CHUNK` and `_pad_to_chunk` unchanged rather than re-deriving the chunking
  discipline), computes the finite-difference Jacobian and Hessian at `h` (calibrating first when
  `h` is `None`), and returns a dict with, under separate keys: full-Hessian agreement (max, median
  and 90th-percentile absolute difference, and the same relative to the autodiff tensor's own scale),
  reduced mean-curvature agreement (the same statistics on `H_vec`, plus on `H_norm`), the step size
  used, the per-point metric condition number the tracer already reports, and the activation string
  `assert_c2_decoder` returned. Apply **no** threshold, return **no** boolean judgement, and produce
  **no** key combining the two levels — this phase measures and `02.5-10` ratifies the bar.

Then write `notebooks/pu_manifold/tests/test_derivative_bridge.py`, mirroring

and `_LinearDecoder` from `test_decoder_curvature.py` rather than redefining them, with one test per

The tracer test, named `test_derivative_agreement_end_to_end_sphere_known_answer`, runs the whole
slice on the sphere fixture: autodiff Hessian, calibrated step, finite-difference Hessian, both
agreement levels, and the pin of `reduce_to_H_vec` against `plain_decoder_curvature`'s own `H_vec` —
one path, one assertion set, no threshold on a trained model anywhere.
